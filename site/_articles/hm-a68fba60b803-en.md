At an exam, an associate professor tries to coax a very weak university applicant towards a pass. They get as far as inequalities, only to find that things look bleak even there.

“Tell me, which is greater: $-1$ or $0$?”

The applicant brightens and answers confidently:

“Minus one, of course!”

The examiner throws up his hands:

“But why?!”

“Well… $-1$ is at least something! But $0$ is absolutely nothing!”

The moral: explain numbers through the philosophy of “nothingness”, and you may unexpectedly defeat mathematics… For a minute, at least!

P. S. Funnily enough, mathematical “nothingness” was once the subject of serious debate: Brahmagupta was the first to formulate clear rules for treating zero as a number, in the *Brahmasphutasiddhanta* of 628. But for our applicant, zero is still “absolutely nothing”. [More about Brahmagupta](https://t.me/history_math/728).
