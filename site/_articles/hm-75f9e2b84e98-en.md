*Marking the 155th anniversary of his birth*

We remember Veniamin Kagan as a scholar, mathematics educator, organizer of science and education, publisher and editor, popularizer and historian of mathematics.

A Russian and Soviet mathematician, researcher and popularizer of Nikolai Lobachevsky’s ideas. He edited the *Bulletin of Experimental Physics and Elementary Mathematics* and was one of the founders of the Mathesis publishing house.

He held a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree and a professorship at Moscow State University. He was named an Honoured Scientist of the RSFSR in 1929 and received the Stalin Prize in 1943.

## Education and Teaching in Odesa

He was born on 25 February 1869, or 9 March in the Gregorian calendar, in Shavli, Kovno Governorate, in the Russian Empire, now Šiauliai, Lithuania.

In 1879 he entered the gymnasium in Yekaterinoslav, graduating with a gold medal in 1887. He became interested in mathematics while still at school. His article “Expanding the Roots of a Quadratic Equation into a Continued Fraction” appeared in the *Bulletin of Experimental Physics and Elementary Mathematics*, one of Russia’s earliest popular science journals devoted to physics and mathematics; its first issue was published in Kyiv, and the journal moved to Odesa in 1891.

In 1887 Kagan entered the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at Novorossiysk University in Odesa. There he began studying Lobachevsky’s geometry independently. In autumn 1889, however, he was expelled for taking part in “student disturbances” commemorating the writer Nikolai Chernyshevsky, who had recently died.

Kagan continued his work on non-Euclidean geometry, studied the university curriculum independently and in 1892 passed the examinations for the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics as an external candidate at Kyiv University. In 1896–1897 he passed the examinations for the degree of magister of pure mathematics at St Petersburg University. In 1897 he joined the teaching staff of Novorossiysk University’s Department of Pure Mathematics. He was among the first in Russia to teach university courses on Lobachevsky geometry and the foundations of geometry. He worked in Odesa until 1922, when he moved to Moscow.

## Editor and Organizer of Education

He was a member of the International Commission on Mathematical Instruction in 1909. In December 1911 and January 1912 he was a member and co-chair of the organizing committee of the First All-Russian Congress of Mathematics Teachers.

From 1902 he was on the editorial staff of the *Bulletin of Experimental Physics and Elementary Mathematics*, becoming editor-in-chief in 1904 and remaining in that role until the journal closed in 1917. From 1905 he chaired the scientific committee of Mathesis, which published scholarly and popular literature on physics and mathematics.

In 1920–1922 he was a professor in the mathematics division of the Faculty of Vocational Education at the Odesa Institute of People’s Education.

He served on the mathematics subject commission of the committee charged with establishing the Soviet secondary school.

## The Moscow Years

In 1922 he moved to Moscow, where Otto Schmidt invited him to head the scientific department of the State Publishing House. In 1922–1930 he was a professor in the Mathematics Department of Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, and in 1930–1931 he headed the Department of Foundations of Geometry, Tensor and Vector Algebra. At the same time, in 1922–1934, he was a full member—that is, a research associate—of the University’s Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics. He was the first head of the Department of Differential Geometry established in 1933 in the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, serving in 1933–1952. In 1934 he received the Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree without defending a dissertation.

Kagan edited the mathematics section of the first edition of the *Great Soviet Encyclopedia* and wrote many of its articles.

## Geometry and Lobachevsky’s Legacy

His principal research fields were the foundations of geometry, non-Euclidean geometry, differential geometry, tensor analysis and its applications to Riemannian geometry and generalizations of it.

Kagan was a leading authority on Lobachevsky’s works and made a substantial contribution to sharing his scientific legacy. He edited an edition of Lobachevsky’s works, supplied scholarly commentaries and wrote a scientific biography of him.

Some of Kagan’s works are listed below; these are Russian editions.

- V. F. Kagan, *Lobachevsky*. Moscow–Leningrad: USSR Academy of Sciences, 1948.
- V. F. Kagan, *Archimedes: A Brief Account of His Life and Work*. Moscow–Leningrad: Gostekhizdat, 1949.
- V. F. Kagan, *Foundations of Geometry: The Historical Development of the Foundations of Geometry*. Part 1, *Lobachevsky Geometry and Its Prehistory*. Moscow–Leningrad: Gostekhizdat, 1949. Part 2, *The Interpretation of Lobachevsky Geometry and the Development of Its Ideas*. Moscow–Leningrad: Gostekhizdat, 1956.
