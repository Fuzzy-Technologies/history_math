An Italian painter, sculptor, architect, engineer and inventor, Leonardo was a leading figure of the Renaissance.

He was born on 15 April 1452 in the village of Anchiano near Florence. He trained in Verrocchio’s workshop in Florence.

His famous paintings include the *Mona Lisa* (*La Gioconda*), *The Last Supper*, *Lady with an Ermine*, the *Benois Madonna* and the *Litta Madonna*. He also created the *Vitruvian Man*, the celebrated drawing of canonical human proportions.

Leonardo illustrated Luca Pacioli’s *Divina proportione* (*The Divine Proportion*). The expression “golden section” first appeared in print much later, in 1835, and was not coined by Leonardo.

Leonardo regarded mathematics as a model of scientific knowledge: “No human investigation can be called true science unless it has passed through mathematical demonstrations.” He studied perspective, the construction of regular polygons, the calculation of centres of gravity, including those of a half-disc and a tetrahedron, and the determination of areas, including that of an ellipse. He also invented a compass for making proportional copies of figures and an instrument for drawing parabolas.

His manuscripts contain designs for an ornithopter, a parachute, an armoured vehicle, diving equipment and a multi-barrelled weapon, as well as notes on optical devices for viewing the Moon enlarged. Many of these ideas remained drawings and experiments rather than completed inventions.

In Milan, Leonardo helped design court festivities, scenery and theatrical machinery.

At the court of Francis I of France, Leonardo held the title of the king’s first painter, engineer and architect.

At the airport in Rome that bears Leonardo da Vinci’s name stands a gigantic statue of the scholar, reaching into the sky and holding a model helicopter. The saying “One who strives towards a star does not turn back” is traditionally attributed to Leonardo.
