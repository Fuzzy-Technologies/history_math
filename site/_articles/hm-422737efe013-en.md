*Marking the 95th anniversary of her birth*

> “To be a historian of mathematics, you must know mathematics and understand it. In essence, it is the same mathematics, but the historian asks how, when and in solving which problems mathematical ideas arose whose development led to modern science.” — G. P. Matvievskaya, 2007

Galina Pavlovna Matvievskaya was a Soviet and Russian historian of mathematics, scholar of Eastern cultures, local historian and literary scholar. She received her Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1969, became an Honoured Scientist of the Uzbek SSR in 1980 and a corresponding member of its Academy of Sciences in 1984. She became a full member of the Academy of Sciences of Uzbekistan in 2000, a full member of the International Academy of the History of Science in 1995 and a member of the Union of Writers of Russia in 2000.

## Education and Euler’s Manuscripts

She was born on 13 July 1930 in Dnepropetrovsk and attended schools in Kharkov and Orenburg. She finished school with a gold medal in 1948 and entered the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics at Leningrad University.

> “I became a historian of science and a scholar of the East, so now I sometimes even have to prove that I am a mathematician. But I graduated from the university’s Department of Number Theory—that is pure mathematics. And my degree was with distinction,” Galina Pavlovna said.

After graduating in 1954, she entered postgraduate study at the USSR Academy of Sciences’ Institute for the History of Science and Technology, supervised by Academician V. I. Smirnov.

> “It was at this time that Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov began working on Leonhard Euler’s manuscripts, which had just been returned from Switzerland to the USSR Academy of Sciences archive.”

She too studied the great mathematician’s unpublished manuscripts. In 1959 she defended her Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences dissertation, “Leonhard Euler’s Unpublished Manuscripts on Number Theory”.

## Tashkent and the History of Eastern Mathematics

From 1959 she lived in Tashkent, her husband Professor K. R. Rakhimov’s home city, and worked at the Institute of Mathematics of the Uzbek SSR Academy of Sciences. She learned Arabic and knew Latin, German, English and French. She studied the history of mathematics in the Near and Middle East through medieval Arabic manuscripts. In 1968 she defended her doctoral dissertation, “The Theory of Number in the Middle Ages”.

Her principal research interests were Euler’s manuscripts, mathematics and mathematical astronomy in the medieval East, science in medieval Europe, the history of number theory and the cultural history of the Orenburg region. Her articles and books on great Eastern scholars—including al-Khwarizmi, al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, Abu Rayhan al-Biruni, Nasir al-Din al-Tusi and Ulugh Beg—occupy an important place in her work. Her books in the scholarly Scientific Biographies series included *René Descartes* (1976), *Ramus, 1515–1572* (1981), *Albrecht Dürer as a Scientist* (1987), the collective monograph *Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov* (1994), *Ulugh Beg, 1394–1449* (1997, with Z. K. Sokolovskaya), and *Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi, 903–986* (1999).

She wrote more than 300 scholarly works, including thirty monographs.

## Orenburg and Recognition

From 1994 she lived in Orenburg and worked as a professor at Orenburg State Pedagogical University. She died in Orenburg on 30 January 2025, aged ninety-four.

She received the title of Honoured Worker of Higher Professional Education in 2010 and the Uzbek SSR’s Abu Rayhan al-Biruni State Prize in Science and Technology in 1974.

## Notes

P.S. I had the good fortune to meet G. P. Matvievskaya at the Kolmogorov Readings in Yaroslavl in 2004 and to correspond with her. She asked me to write about Academician V. I. Smirnov’s years in Yelabuga. He was evacuated there from 1941 to 1944 as part of Leningrad University’s research branch. During those years he also headed the Department of Physics and Mathematics at Yelabuga Teacher Training Institute.

My article “In the Harsh War Years…” was included in the second edition of *Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov, 1887–1974*, edited by O. A. Ladyzhenskaya and V. M. Babich. Moscow: Nauka, 2006. 328 pp. Russian Academy of Sciences Scientific Biographies series.

Galina Pavlovna gave me a copy of the book with her inscription.
