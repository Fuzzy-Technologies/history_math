*Marking the 155th anniversary of his birth*

A Russian and Soviet mathematician, corresponding member of the Russian Academy of Sciences (1924) and president of the Moscow Mathematical Society (1923–1931).

He was born in Moscow on 10 December 1869, or 22 December in the Gregorian calendar.

In 1891 he graduated from the mathematics division of Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics and remained there to prepare for a professorial career. He became a privatdozent in 1894 and a full professor in 1909.

From 1910 he regularly ran a mathematical seminar, devoted to different branches of mathematics from year to year. From then on, mathematical seminars became a usual form of university teaching. He also created a seminar in mathematical analysis which marked the beginning of Moscow’s school of real-variable function theory.

In May 1917, Moscow University’s council elected him assistant to the rector for a four-year term. From the foundation of the University’s Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics in 1921 until 1930, he was a full member of the Institute, serving as its director from 1924.

He became vice-president of the Moscow Mathematical Society in 1921 and president in 1923.

His pupils included leading Soviet scholars: N. N. Luzin, P. S. Alexandrov, A. N. Kolmogorov, I. G. Petrovsky, I. I. Privalov, D. E. Menshov and S. P. Finikov.

Egorov’s scientific reputation was very high. He was elected an honorary member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1929 and also belonged to the Kazan Physics and Mathematics Society.

In 1929 he was persecuted for his religious beliefs, arrested in October 1930 and exiled to Kazan for five years. He died in hospital on 10 September 1931. His grave is on the first avenue of Arskoye Cemetery in Kazan, opposite Lobachevsky’s grave.
