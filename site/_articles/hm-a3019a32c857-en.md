For an arbitrary number of denominations, no general formula for the Frobenius coin problem is known, although many of its cases have been solved completely.

Ferdinand Georg Frobenius (1849–1917) was a distinguished German mathematician whose 175th birth anniversary was marked in 2024.

The coin problem can be stated as follows:

“For specified positive integer denominations with greatest common divisor 1, find the largest monetary amount that cannot be made with coins of those denominations.”

For example, the largest amount that cannot be made using only coins worth 3 and 5 units is 7. These denominations cannot make $1,2,4,7$, the last of which is the largest; all larger amounts can be made.

Here is an algebraic formulation for two coprime natural numbers $a,b>1$—the coin denominations:

There are coins of denominations $a$ and $b$, with an unlimited supply of each. Find the largest amount $F(a,b)$ that cannot be paid using these denominations: that is, cannot be written as $xa+yb$, where $x,y$ are nonnegative integers.

For example, $F(3,5)=7$.

How would you solve it? Our answer is below.

## Solution

For two coprime numbers $a,b>1$ there is a simple formula:

$F(a,b)=ab-a-b$.

If $\gcd(a,b)>1$, infinitely many amounts cannot be paid using the two denominations. Every number not divisible by $\gcd(a,b)$ is such an amount.

If $\gcd(a,b)=1$, then $F(a,b)=ab-a-b$.

The total number of amounts that cannot be made with the two denominations is:

$\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}{2}$.

For three numbers the situation is much more complicated. In the special case where $a,b,c>1$ are pairwise coprime, $F(ab,ac,bc)=2abc-ab-ac-bc$.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

For two denominations there is a complete solution. Moreover, the total number of amounts that cannot be made with these coins is also known.

A fast algorithm for $F(a,b,c)$ was not found until 1978.

For larger numbers of denominations, a general formula for Frobenius numbers is unknown. For every fixed number of denominations there is an algorithm that computes the Frobenius number in polynomial time.

The Frobenius problem is closely connected with discrete optimisation and cryptographic methods of protecting information.
