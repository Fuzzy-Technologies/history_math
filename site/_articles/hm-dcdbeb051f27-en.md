In an old firm, Brown and Jones, Brown’s share of the capital was one and a half times Jones’s. They decided to admit Robinson as a partner on condition that he paid 2,500 dollars. This sum was to be divided between the existing owners so that all three partners would hold equal shares, with the firm’s total capital unchanged.

How should the 2,500 dollars be divided?

## Solution

By paying 2,500 dollars, Robinson bought one third of the firm’s capital. Before he joined, therefore, the capital was 7,500 dollars. Brown’s share was 4,500 dollars and Jones’s 3,000 dollars. Brown should consequently receive 2,000 dollars from Robinson’s payment ($4500 - 2500$), and Jones 500 dollars ($3000 - 2500$).
