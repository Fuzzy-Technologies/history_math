*Marking the 140th anniversary of his birth*

The year 2025 marks the 140th anniversary of Ivan Yakovlevich Depman’s birth. He was a Soviet mathematician, educator, specialist in teaching methods, historian of science and popularizer of mathematics. Pupils and teachers still enjoy his books on mathematical history, and generations of teachers have used his ideas and materials in lessons and extracurricular activities.

## Education and Teaching

I. Ya. Depman, known in Estonian as Jaan Depman, was born on 17 July 1885 in Tarvastu, now in Estonia.

He graduated from the Yuryev, or Tartu, Teacher Training Seminary in 1903 and began teaching in a village school. In 1907 he entered the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at St Petersburg University, graduating in 1912. He taught at gymnasia in Yamburg, now Kingisepp, and Smolensk.

He had an extraordinary gift for languages: he spoke Estonian, Russian, German, French, English and Latin fluently and read other European languages. In 1915 he translated J. W. Mackail’s *Russia’s Gift to the World* from English, a book about Russian scholars’ contribution to world culture.

In 1917 he took part in the First Estonian Mathematical Congress in Tartu, an important event in the development of the region’s scholarly community. From 1917 to 1925 he taught at the Vyatka Pedagogical Institute, becoming a professor in 1922. From 1925 to 1970 he was a professor at the M. N. Pokrovsky Leningrad Pedagogical Institute, subsequently named after A. I. Herzen, where he established a strong school of historical research and mathematics education.

## Books and Historical Research

He taught courses on the history of mathematics at teacher training institutes and Leningrad University. Over his career he published more than a hundred books and pamphlets for teachers and pupils, including these popular works:

- *From the History of Mathematics* (1950).
- *The World of Numbers* (1966, 1982).
- *Stories about Old and New Algebra* (1967).
- *Beyond the Pages of a Mathematics Textbook*, co-authored with N. Ya. Vilenkin (1989).

His major studies included:

- *A History of Arithmetic* (1965).
- *A History of Mathematics* (1959).

These played an important role in developing the history of mathematics as a field of scholarship in the USSR.

He wrote extensively about mathematics in Russia, studying N. I. Lobachevsky’s legacy and the work of his teachers Bartels and Littrow. He also examined contributions from mathematicians in Slavic countries, including Bolzano and Vega, and the development of mathematics at Dorpat, now Tartu, University.

## The Siege and Recognition

During the siege of Leningrad he headed the mathematics department at the Institute for Teacher Development. Even under the most difficult conditions he continued teaching and organized school mathematics olympiads, sometimes in bomb shelters.

He received the Order of Lenin in 1961 and the title of Outstanding Worker in Public Education of the RSFSR. His contribution to education and mathematics teaching was recognized both in the USSR and abroad.

Ivan Yakovlevich Depman was a humanist educator who devoted his life to the history and teaching of mathematics. His work continues to inspire teachers, education specialists and lovers of science.
