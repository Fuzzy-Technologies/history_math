(Dedicated to the 220th anniversary of Kazan University)

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-1" %}

Further advances in mathematics and the widening range of research in Kazan University's mathematics department meant that, by the 1930s, its staff comprised several creative groups united by common research interests. Specialized scientific seminars appeared. The faculty itself also grew: from 30 mathematics students in the early 1920s to 100 in the early 1930s.

As a result, in September 1934 the mathematics department of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics split into three departments: mathematical analysis, geometry and algebra. Their first heads were Nikolai Grigoryevich Chebotaryov in algebra, Pyotr Alexeyevich Shirokov in geometry, and Boris Mikhailovich Gagayev in mathematical analysis.

Credit for creating a first-rate mathematical centre in Kazan belongs to Chebotaryov. His arrival in 1927 marked the beginning of the systematic development of algebra. This year can be regarded as the birth of the Kazan algebraic school.

Professor Chebotaryov, elected a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1929, worked on higher algebra, group theory, number theory and geometry. His monograph *Foundations of Galois Theory* (1934) entered the record of mathematics' most important achievements across all times and nations. He became an Honoured Scientist of the RSFSR in 1943 and received the USSR State Prize in 1947 for his fundamental results in Galois theory. The Academy's Presidium established the N. G. Chebotaryov Prize in 1947. His pupils included the prominent Soviet algebraists I. D. Ado, V. V. Morozov and N. N. Meiman.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Nikolai Nikolayevich Parfentyev began his scientific work at the start of the twentieth century. From 1930 until his death, he headed the department of theoretical mechanics. He developed function theory, theoretical mechanics, elasticity and hydromechanics, mathematical physics, and the history and methodology of mathematics.

His varied research interests helped train pupils in diverse specialities. In the first decades after the Revolution, they laid the foundations of original scientific directions and ensured the continued development of mathematics and mechanics at Kazan University. They included Pyotr Alexeyevich Shirokov, Boris Mikhailovich Gagayev, Vasily Andreyevich Yablokov, Konstantin Petrovich Persidsky, Mazit Ifatovich Almukhamedov, Garun Samigullovich Salekhov, Kurban Zakirovich Galimov and others.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

Professor Shirokov developed problems in non-Euclidean geometry and mechanics, as well as tensor analysis. He trained many geometers who became Doctors of Sciences, including I. P. Egorov and B. L. Laptev. Through Shirokov's work, followed by that of A. P. Norden and their pupils, the Kazan geometric school emerged, continuing Lobachevsky's work in new forms and at a modern level.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

Nikolai Guryevich Chetaev founded the Kazan school of stability theory. He was a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences (1943), a Lenin Prize laureate (1960), a full member of the Academy of Artillery Sciences (1947), and an engineer-major (1955). His name was included among 1500 outstanding mathematicians of all times and nations.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

Professor Gagayev studied real-variable function theory, the analytic theory of differential equations and mathematical physics. He chaired the Kazan Physical and Mathematical Society in 1947–1949 and was dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics in 1945–1947. He wrote several essays on mathematical history. As head of the mathematical analysis department, he trained more than 80 Candidates of Sciences and 15 Doctors of Sciences. He became an Honoured Scientist of the Tatar ASSR in 1954 and of the RSFSR in 1966.

Gagayev's pupils included Professor F. D. Gakhov, founder of Kazan University's differential equations department and later an academician of the Belarusian Academy of Sciences; Voronezh University Professor Yu. G. Borisovich; Russian Academy of Sciences Academician V. N. Monakhov; Kazan University Professors G. S. Salekhov, M. A. Pudovkin, A. D. Lyashko, A. N. Sherstnev and B. G. Gabdullayev; and many others.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

The outstanding mathematician Professor Alexander Petrovich Norden graduated from Moscow University and worked at Kazan State University from 1945, heading its geometry department. His main work concerned differential geometry. He devoted considerable attention to university geometry teaching, creating courses in the foundations of geometry and differential geometry. His *Elementary Introduction to Lobachevsky Geometry* (1953) is of interest to teachers. Norden played a key role in organizing the journal *Izvestiya Vysshikh Uchebnykh Zavedenii. Matematika* and led it for more than 20 years. He supervised about 40 Doctors and Candidates of Sciences. Lobachevsky's works were published under his editorship and with his commentary. He became an Honoured Scientist of the Tatar ASSR in 1954 and the RSFSR in 1964, and received the N. I. Lobachevsky Medal.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-7" %}

The prominent geometer Boris Lukich Laptev made a substantial contribution to the theory of generalized spaces. He is also known as a mathematical historian and scholar of Lobachevsky's work. He organized and led the annual Lobachevsky Readings. He headed the geometry department, served as dean of Kazan State University's Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, and directed its Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics. He became an Honoured Scientist of the Tatar ASSR in 1965 and the RSFSR in 1975.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-8" %}

The Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics opened at Kazan State University in 1935. Its first director was Chebotaryov. After his death, the institute was named after him and was subsequently headed by his pupil V. V. Morozov. Working in different branches of algebra, Chebotaryov's pupils extended the achievements of the Kazan algebraic school.

Vladimir Vladimirovich Morozov successfully solved the problem of enumerating all primitive representations of simple Lie groups in 1938. Anatoly Vasilyevich Dorodnov (1908–1989) completely solved the celebrated Hippocrates problem on squarable lunes.

Work in mathematical logic at the university developed fully from the 1960s. Its initiator was Professor Morozov, who headed the algebra department during those years. Since 2007, it has been the department of algebra and mathematical logic.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-9" %}

Mikhail Tikhonovich Nuzhin, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, professor and Honoured Scientist of the RSFSR, was rector of Kazan State University for 25 years. His research covered boundary-value problems, complex-variable function theory, differential equations, continuum mechanics and filtration theory. During his rectorship in 1954–1979, new scientific fields arose at the university: bionics, biophysics, biochemistry, and the physics and chemistry of semiconductors. The university's computing centre was also established.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-10" %}

The strength of the Kazan mathematical school has always shaped science and education in the region. Kazan University and its mathematical school, one of the largest international scientific centres, have existed for more than two centuries. During this period, the university's scholars have made immense contributions both to mathematics and to mathematical education. The school's distinguished representatives have earned it well-deserved recognition in Russia and abroad.
