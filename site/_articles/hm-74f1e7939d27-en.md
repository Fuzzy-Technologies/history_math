What do all married couples have in common, whether they married yesterday or fifty years ago?

This is neither mysticism, numerology nor karma — just arithmetic. Here is the statement:

“Every married person has this property: adding their age, year of birth, year of marriage and number of years of married life gives the same total for husband and wife.”

## Try it

Want to check? Add your year of birth, age in completed years, wedding year and completed years of marriage. Ask your spouse to do the same.

For example:

Husband: $40+1985+2018+7=4050$.<br>
Wife: $34+1991+2018+7=4050$.

If both birthdays have already occurred, or neither has yet occurred, the answers will match. How is that possible?

## Revealing the secret

It is true and easily proved. The quantities being added are related.

Use these symbols, counting completed years as of today:

- $T$: the current year;
- $A$: age in completed years;
- $B$: birth year;
- $W$: wedding year;
- $M$: completed years of marriage.

Let $\varepsilon_b$ be 1 if the birthday has not yet occurred in the current year and 0 otherwise. Similarly, let $\varepsilon_w=1$ before the wedding anniversary and 0 after it. Then

$A+B=T-\varepsilon_b$.<br>
$M+W=T-\varepsilon_w$.

The required sum is $(A+B)+(W+M)=2T-\varepsilon_b-\varepsilon_w$.

## Conclusion

The sum equals $2T$ only after both the birthday and wedding anniversary; before one or both dates it is smaller by 1 or 2. The anniversary is shared, so its adjustment is the same for both spouses. A difference can arise only from birthdays: if one birthday has occurred and the other has not, one total is smaller by 1. If both birthdays have occurred or neither has, the totals match. The 2025 example is valid on a date after both birthdays and the wedding anniversary.
