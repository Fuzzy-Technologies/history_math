A Soviet mathematician and educator, Doctor of Pedagogical Sciences from 1941 and professor from 1943. He became a corresponding member of the RSFSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences in 1947.

He was born on 6 March Old Style, 19 March New Style, 1900 in Kharkov.

## Education and Early Teaching

In 1908 his family moved to Switzerland, where he attended school in Zurich. In 1912 they moved to Germany, returning to Russia in 1913 and settling in Odessa. He attended a gymnasium from 1914 to 1918, then entered the Mathematics Division of Novorossiysk University.

From 1922 to 1924 he taught mathematics in the workers’ faculties of the Odessa Higher Agricultural Institute and the Polytechnic Institute.

In 1924 he moved to Moscow and entered the third year of the Mathematics Division at Moscow University. He graduated in 1929 and pursued postgraduate studies there from 1929 to 1932. He researched the methodology of mathematics in the Natural Sciences Section of the Communist Academy.

He taught at Moscow University from 1929. He also lectured on number theory and theoretical arithmetic at the A. S. Bubnov Moscow Pedagogical Institute, later the V. I. Lenin Moscow State Pedagogical Institute and today Moscow Pedagogical State University, and at the Moscow City Institute for Teacher Development. In 1935 he received the Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree. He was awarded the title of professor in 1943.

## Mathematics Education and the Doctoral Work

I. V. Arnold investigated questions of mathematics teaching in secondary and higher education.

In 1941 he defended a dissertation for the Doctor of Pedagogical Sciences degree in mathematics education. The work submitted was *Theoretical Arithmetic*, published in 1938 as a textbook for the mathematics divisions of physics and mathematics faculties in teacher training institutes. This was the first defence and award of such a degree in the USSR.

## The War and Postwar Years

He worked at Moscow University until 1941, when he moved to Magnitogorsk and continued teaching.

In 1944 he returned to Moscow to head the Department of Higher Mathematics at the Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys. He was also a professor in Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics.

From 1944 to 1948 he worked as a researcher in the Mathematics Education Section of the Research Institute of Teaching Methods at the RSFSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences. In 1947 he was elected a corresponding member of the Academy.

## Notes

Principal works by I. V. Arnold:

- *Theoretical Arithmetic* (1938).

- *Number Theory* (1939).

- *Theory of Series: Lecture Notes* (1939).

- *Principles for Selecting and Composing Arithmetic Problems* (1946).

- *An Operational Interpretation of Number in Elementary Mathematics* (1947).

- *Negative Numbers in the Algebra Course* (1947).

- *Exponents and Logarithms in Elementary Algebra* (1948).

From the blog’s author, Mansur Gilmullin:

Before I. V. Arnold’s *Theoretical Arithmetic*, no works on theoretical arithmetic in Russia or the USSR reflected modern mathematical ideas. The book is built on a set-theoretic approach. It systematically develops the concept of number from the natural numbers to quaternions and hypercomplex numbers. Particular attention is given to the construction of the real numbers following Cantor, Dedekind and Weierstrass.

Drawing on Arnold’s *Theoretical Arithmetic* enabled teacher training institutes to teach the subject at a higher mathematical and pedagogical level.

The course was later renamed “Number Systems”. I had the good fortune to teach it at a teacher training institute for thirty years.
