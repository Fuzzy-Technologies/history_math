For your mathematical leisure, here is an old primary-school problem from Yemelyan Ignatyevich Ignatyev’s *V tsarstve smekalki* (In the Realm of Ingenuity):

“Two people cooked porridge. One supplied 2 pounds of grain and the other 3. When it was ready, a third person arrived and asked to eat with them for a payment. After eating he paid 5 kopecks. How did the cooks divide this money?”

Assuming that all three ate equal amounts, the answer “One gets 2 kopecks and the other 3” is wrong. Can you explain why?

## Historical background

Yemelyan Ignatyevich Ignatyev (1869–1923) was a Russian and Soviet mathematics teacher and popular-science author. His best-known work, *V tsarstve smekalki*, appeared in three volumes in 1908–1911.

## Solution

Since the third person paid 5 kopecks, all the porridge was worth 15. It was made from 5 pounds of grain, so each pound accounts for 3 kopecks.

The first person contributed 6 kopecks’ worth and the second 9. Each ate 5 kopecks’ worth. The first should therefore receive $6-5=1$ kopeck and the second $9-5=4$.

Answer: 1 kopeck and 4 kopecks.

## Notes

All three volumes of Y. I. Ignatyev’s *V tsarstve smekalki* (1908–1911).

Y. I. Ignatyev, *V tsarstve smekalki, ili Arifmetika dlya vsekh* (In the Realm of Ingenuity, or Arithmetic for Everyone), Parts 1–3, St Petersburg: V. L. Bogushevsky, 1908–1911 (in Russian).

### Reading texts with Ъ

Pre-revolutionary Russian texts can be difficult to read at first, but the basic word-final rule is straightforward.

Before the 1918 spelling reform, word-final Ъ was written after a final hard consonant. It was not added after й or ь and was not pronounced.

When reading such a text today, omit only word-final Ъ. The separating Ъ inside a word has a different spelling function and must not be ignored. For example:

- Old spelling: «столъ стоитъ на полу».
- Modern reading: «стол стоит на полу».

### A little more detail

The 1918 reform abolished compulsory word-final Ъ. This made new typesetting more economical by removing a frequently repeated, unpronounced character; books that had already been printed did not, of course, change.

During the 1917 Revolution, Ignatyev played an active part in establishing Soviet power. He was a member of the Petrograd Soviet and the executive committee of the Spassky district soviet. Before the Constituent Assembly election he published *Izbiratelnaya arifmetika* (Electoral Arithmetic), explaining the principal ways of allocating seats under proportional representation: the largest-remainder method and the D’Hondt method.
