*For the Year of the Teacher and Mentor, 2023*

> “I am happy to have lived to see mathematics become the possession of the broadest masses. Can the tiny print runs before the Revolution compare with those of today? And that is hardly surprising: the whole country is studying now. I am glad that even in old age I can be useful to my great Motherland.”
>
> — A. P. Kiselyov, 1938

A Russian and Soviet mathematics educator and the author of school textbooks on arithmetic, algebra, geometry and physics.

He was born on 30 November 1852, or 12 December in the Gregorian calendar, in Mtsensk, Oryol Governorate. He graduated from St Petersburg University in 1875.

He taught mathematics, mechanics and technical drawing in Voronezh secondary schools: at a secondary school specialising in science and practical subjects until 1891 and then at the cadet corps until 1901. After twenty-five years in Voronezh, he retired in 1901. Following the Revolution he returned to teaching mathematics. In 1918–1921 he taught at the Voronezh Institute of Public Education and on teacher-training courses. From 1922 he lived and worked in Petrograd, where he taught until 1925.

The second half of the nineteenth century saw the creation of secondary-school textbooks that were advanced for their time, including Kiselyov’s *A Systematic Course of Arithmetic for Secondary Educational Institutions* (1884), *Elementary Algebra* (1888) and *Elementary Geometry* (1892). These differed from existing textbooks in their high theoretical standards, orderly presentation, clarity and concision. Kiselyov wrote for several kinds of school, including technical secondary schools, classical gymnasiums, seminaries and cadet corps. His *Elementary Physics for Secondary Educational Institutions* was reprinted in Russia until 1918. *Elementary Geometry* quickly became popular and had reached twenty-six editions by 1917.

Kiselyov improved his manuals from edition to edition, and they gradually became the principal textbooks in Russian schools. After revision in the 1930s, his books became standard texts and remained among the principal mathematics textbooks used in Soviet schools into the 1970s.

He received the Orders of St Anne (1894 and 1899), St Stanislaus (1896) and the Red Banner of Labour (1933).

Kiselyov’s textbooks continue to be reprinted regularly.

> “I would return to Kiselyov.” — Academician Vladimir Arnold
