(On the 190th anniversary of his birth, and in the Year of the Teacher and Mentor)

> The question of public education is not a question of an isolated harm or benefit. “It concerns Russia’s future and is more important than questions of finance, legal proceedings, policing and state institutions in general.”  
> S. A. Rachinsky

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-1" %}

Many of you have seen *Mental Arithmetic*, by the well-known Russian artist N. P. Bogdanov-Belsky. Its other title is *At S. A. Rachinsky’s Village School*. Few people know that Rachinsky was a professor at Moscow University who left during the rise of the Narodnik movement, returned to his native village and devoted himself to educating the people. Academician Bogdanov-Belsky, himself once Rachinsky’s pupil, dedicated his painting to an episode of school life and the creative atmosphere of his teacher’s lessons.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

Sergei Alexandrovich Rachinsky, a Russian scientist, botanist and mathematician, was born on 2(14) May 1833 in Tatevo, Smolensk Governorate, now in Tver Region.

He studied at Moscow University’s medical faculty, without graduating, and its Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, from which he did graduate. He pursued a master’s degree in botany at German universities. He became a Doctor of Botany, a professor at Moscow University, and a corresponding member of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences. Charles Darwin’s *On the Origin of Species* first appeared in Russian in Rachinsky’s translation in 1864. Students and colleagues at the university loved him for his extensive and selfless public service.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

Rachinsky had wide-ranging knowledge and interests and also wrote literary works. Scholars, writers and artists gathered in his Moscow home. There he met L. N. Tolstoy, P. I. Tchaikovsky and the Aksakov brothers.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

In Russian history, however, Rachinsky is better known as a teacher and educator. His role in Russian education was highly significant and remains relevant in many respects today.

In 1867, he resigned after a conflict between progressive professors and the administration of Moscow University. In 1872, he returned to his ancestral village of Tatevo. Rachinsky built Russia’s first rural school with boarding accommodation for peasant children and taught there himself. He also improved existing schools and founded new ones. He spent his entire fortune on rural schools. In the 1880s, he became Russia’s leading advocate of parish schools. Many university graduates came to Tatevo to work under his guidance.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

The school became a parish school in 1878. In the 1870–1880s it offered a four-year course, and in 1898 a six-year course. Until 1924 it bore its founder’s name, S. A. Rachinsky, after which both his name and his achievements were forgotten for decades. After the Great Patriotic War, the school became a general secondary school. Rachinsky’s name was restored to it in 1998.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-7" %}

Rachinsky limited his school’s core curriculum to Russian grammar and the arithmetic of whole numbers. He shifted the emphasis of education towards practical abilities and skills. The elementary rural school in Tatevo offered four years of study. Much attention was given to religious instruction. The Tatevo teacher himself taught arithmetic and grammar, singing and drawing, geography and botany.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-8" %}

The children’s first books at Rachinsky’s school were K. Ushinsky’s *The Native Word* and L. Tolstoy’s *ABC*. Rachinsky maintained a friendly correspondence with Tolstoy for many years, exchanging advice on school matters.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-9" %}

Beyond these subjects, Sergei Alexandrovich introduced other lessons that greatly broadened the pupils’ horizons and shaped their development: natural history, agriculture, drawing and technical drawing. In the school’s art workshop, Rachinsky himself taught painting, technical drawing and drawing.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-10" %}

Rachinsky was among the first to advocate the education of girls.

He always emphasised that every school is a living, creative endeavour arising from the interaction between teachers and pupils. In his words, “Teaching in a Russian school is not a trade, but a calling.” He sought and found a “spark of God” in his pupils and believed that everyone possessed it.

Rachinsky developed a distinctive method of teaching mental arithmetic, giving village children its practical skills and the foundations of mathematical thinking.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-11" %}

D. S. Fraermark writes in *A Problem Came from a Painting*: *“Arithmetic lessons resembled a competitive game between pupils to solve problems and calculations correctly and quickly.”*

Rachinsky’s well-known mathematics manual, *1001 Problems for Mental Arithmetic* (1891), was intended for teachers in rural schools.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-12" %}

> **Here are some problems from that book:**
>
> 1\. During February I read the whole Old Testament. I read 36 pages a day. How many pages are there in the Old Testament? \[1008\]

> 10\. How many vershoks are there in 375 arshins? \[6000\]

> 13\. A merchant bought 120 arshins of cloth for 150 roubles. What does one arshin cost? \[1 rouble 25 kopecks\]

> 27\. How many vershoks are there in a verst? How many inches? \[24000; 42000\]

> 34\. A man drinks a glass of vodka every day except Sunday and 6 glasses on Sundays. A glass costs 4 kopecks. How much money does he drink away in a year? \[25 roubles\]

> 77\. A gross (12 dozen) of steel pen nibs costs 60 kopecks. A shopkeeper bought 5 gross and sold the nibs in tens at 5 kopecks per ten. What was his profit? \[60 kopecks\]

> 152\. Two people set out simultaneously towards one another from two towns 63 versts apart. One takes 15 minutes to walk a verst, the other 12 minutes. When will they meet? \[After 7 hours\]

> 283\. Working 7 hours a day, a scribe copied 140 pages in 4 days. How many pages will he copy in 6 days if he works 8 hours a day? \[240\]

> 753\. A husband and wife saved money for 4 years. Each saved 10 roubles in the first year. In each following year, however, the husband saved 7 times as much as in the previous year, while the wife saved only 3 times as much. How many times greater are the husband’s savings than the wife’s? \[10 times\]

> 820\. If I spend 60 roubles a week, I will be 200 roubles short over a year. How much can I spend each day without going into debt? \[8 roubles\]

> 932\. St Petersburg and Moscow are 604 versts apart. A train leaves St Petersburg at 7 in the morning and travels at 39 versts per hour. At 1 in the afternoon, a train leaves Moscow towards it at 35 versts per hour. When will they meet? \[At 6 in the evening\]

> 1001\. A pond was dug for me, 8 sazhens long, 4 sazhens wide, and 1 sazhen 1 arshin 8 vershoks deep. Each cubic sazhen excavated costs 1 rouble 50 kopecks. How much did I pay? \[72 roubles\]

In 1893, poor health forced Rachinsky to stop teaching. In his spare time he wrote the series “Arithmetic Amusements” (1900) and “Geometric Amusements” (1901), published in the journal *Public Education*.

Sergei Alexandrovich died on 15 May 1902, his birthday, and was buried in Tatevo.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-13" %}

Leo Tolstoy, Vasily Rozanov, Pyotr Tchaikovsky and Konstantin Pobedonostsev turned to Rachinsky for advice. The emperors Alexander III and Nicholas II thanked this village teacher for his devoted work. His profound patriotism, fidelity to national values and his homeland, and desire to build a truly popular school are seen by us as a great act of civic service by one of Russia’s distinguished sons: S. A. Rachinsky, a teacher of the people.

P. S. I am also the author of a book on the [history of mathematics](https://ridero.ru/books/istoriya_matematiki/). Its cover features *Mental Arithmetic* in memory of this great teacher and mathematician. I want all teachers to remember the nobility of teaching mathematics to children.
