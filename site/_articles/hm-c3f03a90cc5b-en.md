In 1892 Kazan University was preparing to celebrate the centenary of Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky (1792–1856), the brilliant creator of non-Euclidean geometry. With the anniversary approaching, the local Physics and Mathematics Society proposed raising money by public subscription for a monument and a prize in his name. The project needed approval from the Ministry of Public Education. Instead of an answer, the ministry sent the university a query: “Who is this Lobachevsky, and what exceptional scientific achievements are credited to him?”
