*Written to mark the 400th anniversary of his birth*

He was born on 19 June 1623 in Clermont-Ferrand, France.

A French mathematician, physicist, philosopher, writer and religious thinker.

He made major contributions to the foundations of many branches of mathematics: probability theory, mathematical analysis and projective geometry.

Blaise was a gifted child. His father, Étienne, educated him himself. From the age of fourteen, Pascal took part in Mersenne’s weekly seminars.

At sixteen he proved the theorem that now bears his name, concerning a hexagon inscribed in a conic. It is a classical theorem of projective geometry: the intersection points of the opposite sides of a hexagon inscribed in a conic lie on one straight line.

In 1658 Pascal turned to problems in the emerging analysis of infinitesimals. He discovered and applied methods that justify regarding him as one of the founders of analysis. As Leibniz noted, Pascal’s theorem on the characteristic triangle was one of the sources he drew on when developing differential and integral calculus.

Pascal also investigated the cycloid. He used integral sums to solve problems of quadrature, cubature and the centres of gravity of figures associated with the cycloid.

His *Treatise on the Arithmetical Triangle* (1665) played an important part in the development of combinatorics. He systematically studied the triangular array of combinations and developed its applications in detail. The array itself had been known in several mathematical traditions long before Pascal.

Pascal’s law concerning the distribution of pressure in liquids made a fundamental contribution to hydrostatics. In 1648 an experiment devised by Pascal confirmed that atmospheric pressure varies with altitude. He also formulated several laws in meteorology. The unit of pressure, the pascal, is named in his memory.

In 1642 Pascal developed one of the earliest mechanical calculating machines, the Pascaline, which performed addition and subtraction. About fifty such machines were made during his lifetime. The principle he devised underpinned the construction of most arithmometers for almost three centuries. The programming language Pascal is named in his memory.

Pascal’s *Thoughts on Religion and Other Subjects* (1670), the *Pensées*, became a classic of French literature. His legacy combines outstanding French prose with some of the major mathematical achievements of the seventeenth century.
