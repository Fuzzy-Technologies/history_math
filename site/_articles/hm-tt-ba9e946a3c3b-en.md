*“All is number.”*

Number is the measure of things, underlying existence and causing harmony and order. 1, the monad, is the mother of all numbers. The numbers 1, 2, 3, 4 form the sacred tetrad, the foundation of everything: fire, earth, water and air. Their sum, 10, the decade, is the perfect number, the root of all nature.

In this way, the Pythagoreans began assigning nonmathematical properties to numbers, leading to mysticism. Their arithmetic was to some extent a speculative science. Nevertheless, they laid the foundations of number theory. Studying divisibility allowed numbers to be divided into classes, and Pythagorean arithmetic was also closely connected with geometry.

Parity was regarded as a number’s principal property. The Pythagoreans developed a theory of even and odd numbers, later included by Euclid in Book IX of his *Elements*.

The units making up a number were considered indivisible and represented by points, which the Pythagoreans arranged in regular geometric figures. Thus arose figurate numbers: linear, plane, triangular, square and pentagonal.

***Linear numbers, or primes,*** are divisible only by 1 and themselves and are represented by points arranged in a line. An example is the prime number 5.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

***Plane numbers*** can be expressed as the product of two factors and are therefore represented as a rectangle. An example is 6.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

***Solid numbers*** can be expressed as the product of three factors and are represented as a rectangular parallelepiped. An example is 8.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

Today, primes are not classified as figurate numbers, and the terms “plane number” and “solid number” have fallen out of use.

***Triangular numbers*** are 1, 3, 6, 10, ... or, generally, partial sums of the arithmetic progression 1, 2, 3, ...: 1 + 2 + 3 + ... + n = n(n + 1)/2.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

***Square numbers*** are 1, 4, 9, 16, ... or, generally, partial sums of the arithmetic progression 1, 3, 5, ...: 1 + 3 + 5 + ... + (2n − 1) = n² (the sum of consecutive odd numbers beginning with 1).

The modern expression “square” for n² is a survival of Pythagorean terminology. The expressions “square a number” and “cube a number” originated in figurate numbers.

A remarkable property is that every square number except one is the sum of two consecutive triangular numbers.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

***Pentagonal numbers*** are 1, 5, 12, 22, ... or, generally, partial sums of the arithmetic progression 1, 4, 7, ...: 1 + 4 + 7 + ... + (3n − 2) = n(3n − 1) / 2.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-7" %}

A general definition of a k-gonal number is possible for any k greater than or equal to 3: the nth k-gonal number is the sum of the first n terms of an arithmetic progression whose first term is 1 and common difference is (k − 2).

In number theory and combinatorics, figurate numbers are connected with many other classes of integers: binomial coefficients, perfect numbers, and Mersenne, Fermat and Fibonacci numbers.

The Pythagoreans believed figurate numbers played an important role in the structure of the universe. Many prominent mathematicians studied them, including Eratosthenes, Diophantus, Fermat, Euler, Lagrange and Gauss.

Read about figurate numbers and Pythagorean arithmetic in Mansur Gilmullin’s [*History of Mathematics*](https://ridero.ru/books/istoriya_matematiki/).
