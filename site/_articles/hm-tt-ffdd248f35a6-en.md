An area grid, called a *paletka* in Russian from the French *palette*, meaning a plate, palette or tray, is a transparent sheet of film or paper carrying a grid of lines or dots. The grid forms squares of a specified size: 1 mm, 1 cm or 0.1 inch. Surveyors use it to estimate the areas of irregular shapes bounded by curves on plans and maps.

To measure an area, place the grid over the outline and count the squares inside it. In practice, the estimate is usually the number of complete squares inside the outline plus half the number of squares crossed by the boundary. This is the method recommended in instructions for using an area grid.

When measuring a shape on a map, the map's scale must also be taken into account.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

In the example, the area is taken as 34 + 8:2 = 38 cm². A centimetre grid is used here.

An area grid can also be made by hand from any transparent film or paper.

This instrument is used in primary-school practice, in grades 3–4.
