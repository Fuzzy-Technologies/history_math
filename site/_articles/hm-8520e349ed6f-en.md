*Marking the 230th anniversary of his birth*

A Russian educator and mathematician, an Active State Councillor, director of St Petersburg’s Third Gymnasium and professor of mathematics at the Main Pedagogical Institute.

## Education and Teaching Experience

He was born in 1794 in St Petersburg.

He graduated from the St Petersburg Provincial Gymnasium in 1811 and entered the St Petersburg Pedagogical Institute. In his final year he attended mathematics courses to gain that specialization. After graduating, he was sent to London to learn about the Lancasterian system, or “method of mutual instruction”. This was a system of primary-school organization and teaching in which older and more advanced pupils assisted the teacher and, under the teacher’s direction, taught the other pupils. In Switzerland he then visited Pestalozzi’s institute, where methods of primary education were being actively developed.

## Teaching and the Gymnasium

On returning to Russia, he taught mathematics at the St Petersburg Teacher Training Institute in 1819–1823 and at the school later known as the Third St Petersburg Gymnasium.

In 1829 the Main Pedagogical Institute reopened, having closed in 1819 when St Petersburg University was established. Busse was appointed to teach mathematics there and received the rank of adjunct.

In 1823 the Teacher Training Institute was combined with a gymnasium to form a new institution, the St Petersburg Gymnasium, which was renamed the Third St Petersburg Gymnasium in 1830. Busse was appointed its senior mathematics teacher. He also served as inspector and then as director in 1838–1859.

He was also a professor at the Main Pedagogical Institute from 1838 to 1859.

## Curriculum Work

In the early 1850s, Busse became a member for mathematical sciences of the Scientific Committee of the Ministry of Public Education.

In 1846 a new distribution of mathematics lessons within the gymnasium curriculum was proposed. Busse used this allocation to draw up a model mathematics curriculum. The removal of analytic and descriptive geometry from the course was compensated by some expansion of algebra.

## Notes

Busse wrote several textbooks and guides on arithmetic and geometry that served as principal textbooks in secondary schools. These Russian works include:

- *A Guide to Teaching Geometry for Schools of Military Settlements* (1826);
- *A Guide to Establishing Schools Using the Method of Mutual Instruction* (1829);
- *A Guide to Arithmetic for Use in District Schools of the Russian Empire*, issued by the Department of Public Education, Parts 1–2 (1829–1830);
- *A Guide to Geometry for Use in District Schools of the Russian Empire* (1831);
- *A Guide to Teaching Arithmetic for Teachers* (1832);
- *Foundations of Geometry* (1845);
- *A Collection of Arithmetic Problems Arranged According to the Guide to Arithmetic Compiled for District Schools* (1874).
