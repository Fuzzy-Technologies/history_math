Liu Hui (Chinese: 劉徽; pinyin: Liú Huī) wrote the celebrated commentary (263 CE) on *The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art*. The treatise itself developed gradually and had reached something close to its classical form by the first century CE, incorporating earlier material. The *Nine Chapters* are an encyclopaedia of ancient Chinese mathematics; their importance in China is comparable to that of Euclid’s *Elements*. In his commentary, Liu described the “circle-cutting method” and a simple, accurate iterative procedure now known as Liu Hui’s π algorithm. By refining the areas of inscribed polygons, he carried the calculation to a 3,072-sided polygon and obtained $\pi \approx 3.14159$; he used $3927/1250 = 3.1416$ as a convenient approximation.

He also became renowned for unusual intellectual honesty. While trying to derive the volume of a sphere, Liu Hui gave an approximate formula, then discovered that it was incorrect and wrote:

“Let us leave the problem to whoever can tell the truth.”

P. S. If you are not sure, do not pretend. Honesty takes science further than any elegant but incorrect formula.
