Bhaskara II, an Indian mathematician of the twelfth century AD, wrote the four-part treatise *Siddhanta Shiromani* (*Crown of Learning*), devoted to astronomy and mathematics. Its arithmetic section, the book *Lilavati* (*The Beautiful*), remained a model textbook for centuries.

One day he decided to demonstrate the importance of zero. He wrote on the board: “If you have zero apples, you have no apples.” His students laughed, but Bhaskara added seriously: “But if you have zero in an equation, you can solve it!”

The upper photograph shows a statue of Bhaskara II at Patnadevi, near Chalisgaon, India. This is believed to be where he wrote his *Siddhanta Shiromani*. Below is a page from *Lilavati*, Volume I, 1650. The use of the Pythagorean theorem is described in the corner. The title *Lilavati* (*The Beautiful*) is thought to refer to his daughter. The book contains 13 chapters and 278 verses, chiefly on arithmetic and practical mensuration.
