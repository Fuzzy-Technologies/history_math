Three glasses stand on a table: one upright and two upside down:

```text
|‾| |_| |‾|
```

Turn two glasses over at the same time on every move. In exactly three moves, make all three upright:

```text
|_| |_| |_|
```

One person can play, or you can hold a speed competition with an independent judge.

## Solution

0. `|‾| |_| |‾|`

1. `|_| |‾| |‾|`

2. `|‾| |‾| |_|`

3. `|_| |_| |_|`

## Notes

Tatyana writes: “Why not simply turn over the first and third glasses straight away? 🤔 Am I missing something in the condition?”

Thank you for noticing! You are right. We left out the word “exactly”, which has now been added: by turning two glasses over at a time, make all three upright in EXACTLY three moves.

The same three moves appear in Martin Gardner’s [*Mathematical Diversions*](https://djvu.online/file/EJlLRPMjp6sAo) (Russian edition, Moscow: Mir, 1972, pp. 394–395, fig. 193). After the demonstration, the performer secretly turns the middle glass over and invites a spectator to try the trick from this different starting position.
