Augustin-Louis Cauchy (1789–1857), the celebrated French mathematician, taught at the École Polytechnique and the Sorbonne. He once spent a long time trying to explain the proof of a theorem to a well-mannered young count, who simply could not understand it. Driven to despair, Cauchy exclaimed, “The theorem is true! I give you my word of honour!” “Ah, maestro,” the count replied gallantly, “why did you not say so from the start? I would never presume to doubt the word of so respected a gentleman.”
