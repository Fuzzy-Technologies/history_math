Augustin-Louis Cauchy (1789–1857), the celebrated French mathematician, taught at the École Polytechnique and the Sorbonne. In Prague around 1833, he was explaining to the Duke of Bordeaux a proof that a plane section of a cone, with the plane in the appropriate position, is an ellipse. The pupil simply could not understand it. Driven to despair, Cauchy exclaimed, “The theorem is true! I give you my word of honour!” “Ah, maestro,” the Duke replied gallantly, “why did you not say so from the start? I would never presume to doubt the word of so respected a gentleman.”
