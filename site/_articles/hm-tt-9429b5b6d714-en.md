In elementary geometry, the straightedge is one of the basic tools for plane constructions, alongside the compass.

A ruler is a tool for drawing straight lines, the simplest instrument for geometric construction. It usually takes the form of a narrow strip with at least one straight edge.

Later, rulers also began to be used for various measurements, with small marks added to them. Such rulers usually carry a scale whose smallest division depends on their intended purpose.

The history of the ruler stretches back several thousand years. People learned long ago to draw straight lines using a cord. The word “line”, from which the Russian word for ruler derives, comes from the Latin *linum*, “flax thread”. In ancient Egypt, knotted cords were already used to divide up land. Ancient China also had its own measuring rulers.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

The oldest ruler is a copper measuring rod discovered in Nippur, Mesopotamia, in present-day Iraq. It dates to 2650 BCE.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

Russian scribes also used rulers, calling one a *praviltse*. Rulers became firmly established in Russian classrooms in the early nineteenth century. After 1812, French rulers reached Russia as war trophies, but domestic production began only in 1899, at the initiative of D. I. Mendeleev. He helped introduce the metric system in Russia, and the ruler along with it.

A ruler with one straight edge is called a single-edged straightedge. It is a basic tool for plane geometric constructions and constructions in projective geometry.

A ruler with parallel edges, which can be used to draw parallel lines, is called a double-edged ruler.

Rulers are usually made of plastic or wood, and less often of metal.

In 1851, the German industrialist Anton Ullrich invented the folding ruler.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

Later, in 1902, Frank Hunt created a flexible ruler. A fabric measuring tape is an example of a flexible instrument for measuring length that need not be straight when used.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

A segment is measured using a graduated ruler, which specifies its scale and units or the shortest segment length that it can measure.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

Combining the drawing, measuring and calculating functions of different versions of the instrument led to navigational, artillery and officers’ rulers. Medicine has a cardiology ruler for interpreting electrocardiograms. Sewing uses a dressmaker’s ruler, and builders have their own construction rulers.

A ruler program, also called an on-screen ruler, can measure pixels on a computer or mobile-phone screen. A digital ruler or tape measure is also available: a functional, easy-to-use tool for many smartphones and tablets. It works using a built-in laser rangefinder and can measure distances across surfaces.

The history and use of simple, familiar and widely accessible instruments such as the ruler are closely connected with the emergence of science, great discoveries and humanity’s progress.
