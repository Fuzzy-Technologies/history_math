Dmitry Yevgenyevich Menshov (1892–1988) was a remarkable Soviet mathematician and a legendary figure among his colleagues. He took little interest in everyday affairs and was entirely absorbed in mathematics, which gave his life its meaning. Alongside his invaluable scientific work, he left his colleagues a wealth of anecdotes that revealed unexpected sides of him.

Here is one of those legends. Nina Karlovna Bari (1901–1961), a professor and a student of Nikolai Nikolayevich Luzin (1883–1950), always looked after Menshov with great affection. When the war came and Bari was preparing to leave Moscow with her husband, she said to him, “Dmitry Yevgenyevich, there’s a war on, I’m leaving, and there’s nobody to look after you. Why don’t you get married? Your wife would take care of you, and I could stop worrying!” Menshov listened attentively and thought it over. Then a look of bewilderment crossed his face: “But when the war ends, what am I supposed to do with her?!”

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Dmitry Yevgenyevich Menshov](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8C%D1%88%D0%BE%D0%B2,_%D0%94%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%B3%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8C%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87) (1892–1988) was a Soviet mathematician, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, professor at Moscow State University and corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences. He obtained fundamental results and published many works on trigonometric series.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

[Nikolai Nikolayevich Luzin](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9B%D1%83%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BD,_%D0%9D%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B9_%D0%9D%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87) (1883–1950) was a Soviet mathematician, member of the USSR Academy of Sciences, professor at Moscow University, foreign member of the Polish Academy of Sciences and honorary member of mathematical societies in Poland, India, Belgium, France and Italy.

Luzin founded the Moscow school of mathematics. His students included M. A. Aizerman, P. S. Alexandrov, N. K. Bari, V. I. Glivenko, L. V. Keldysh, A. N. Kolmogorov, A. S. Kronrod, M. A. Lavrentyev, L. A. Lyusternik, A. A. Lyapunov, D. E. Menshov, V. V. Nemytsky, P. S. Novikov, M. Ya. Suslin, P. S. Urysohn, A. Ya. Khinchin and L. G. Shnirelman. We have written about many of them in the blog and in our book [History of Mathematics](https://ridero.ru/books/istoriya_matematiki/) (in Russian).
