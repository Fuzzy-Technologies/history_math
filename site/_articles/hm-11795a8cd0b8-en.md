Gerbert of Aurillac, also known as Gerbert d’Aurillac (946–1003), the future Pope Sylvester II, was one of the first Western scholars to travel to Spain and study the mathematics of the Arabic-speaking world. Several mathematical works are attributed to him, including a book on the abacus, or counting board. He was known for his skill in using it.

One day he so amazed his contemporaries with the speed of his calculations that they accused him of sorcery. Laughing, Gerbert said: “If I were a magician, I would have become a king long ago, not a mathematician!”
