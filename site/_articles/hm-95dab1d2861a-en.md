For almost twenty years, from 1964 to 1983, the Department of Differential Geometry in the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics at Moscow State University was headed by the distinguished Soviet mathematician and geometer Pyotr Konstantinovich Rashevsky (1907–1983), a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences and professor.

One day, Pyotr Konstantinovich was running very late for a lecture. In the corridor he ran into a colleague, also a professor. “Well, Pyotr Konstantinovich, late for your lecture?” the man asked slyly. “My lecture hasn’t started yet!” Rashevsky replied without missing a beat.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Pyotr Konstantinovich Rashevsky](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A0%D0%B0%D1%88%D0%B5%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9,_%D0%9F%D1%91%D1%82%D1%80_%D0%9A%D0%BE%D0%BD%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87) (1907–1983) was a Soviet mathematician and geometer, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences (1938), professor (1934) and Honoured Scientist. He was born in Moscow into the family of the equally well-known mathematics educator Konstantin Nikolayevich Rashevsky, the author of several original school textbooks.

P. K. Rashevsky made fundamental contributions to several branches of modern geometry. His research concerned Riemannian geometry, tensor analysis, the theory of spaces with an affine connection, the foundations of projective geometry, the theory of homogeneous spaces, the representation theory of Lie groups and Lie algebras, and mathematical physics, quantum electrodynamics and quantum statistics. He developed polymetric geometry, in which there is more than one way to measure the distance between two points. It has been used to study certain physical structures.
