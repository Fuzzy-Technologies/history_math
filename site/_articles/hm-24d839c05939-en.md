*Marking the 165th anniversary of his birth*

Samuil Shatunovsky was a Russian and Soviet mathematician, logician and educator. Together with V. F. Kagan, he was one of the founders of the celebrated Odessa publishing house Mathesis, which issued popular science literature.

## The Path to University

He was born on 13 March 1859, or 25 March in the Gregorian calendar, in the village of Velikaya Znamenka, Taurida Governorate, in the Russian Empire, in what is now Zaporizhzhia Region, Ukraine.

He graduated from Kherson Realschule and completed an additional class in Rostov. Under the rules of the time he was barred from entering a university and had to enrol in a technical institution. He spent a short time first at the St Petersburg Technological Institute and then at the Institute of Railway Engineers. Eventually he left them and became a non-degree student at St Petersburg University.

He gave private lessons in Ekaterinoslav and Bessarabia governorates. He was invited to Odessa, where professors at Novorossiysk University, now I. I. Mechnikov Odessa National University, petitioned the minister of public education to allow him to take the examinations for a master’s degree.

In 1917 he wrote, and in 1918 defended, a dissertation entitled “Algebra as a Theory of Congruences Modulo Functions” (the Russian title translated here).

## Teaching and Research

Under Soviet rule he was elected a professor at Odessa University. From 1920 he taught at the Odessa Institute of Public Education, heading the city’s first department of algebra. In 1922–1925 he also headed the Department of Mathematics at Odessa Polytechnic Institute.

His principal work concerned algebra, the axiomatic treatment of linear magnitudes and area, foundations for the volume of a polyhedron without using limits, and the construction of algebra. He is known for his principled rejection of the applicability of the law of excluded middle, especially in arguments about infinite sets.

## The Mathesis Publishing House

Odessa, then a provincial centre in southern Russia, became home to the country’s first publishing house devoted specifically to the natural sciences, which operated successfully from 1904 to 1925. It aimed to publish systematically the work of Russian mathematicians, physicists, astronomers and biologists and to translate the best foreign literature in those fields into Russian.

The series *Library of Elementary Mathematics* appeared in 1912–1913 under Shatunovsky’s general editorship. A substantial number of Mathesis books were recommended by the Ministry of Public Education’s scholarly commission for secondary-school libraries.

Shatunovsky was one of the teachers of G. M. Fichtenholz and S. A. Yanovskaya.
