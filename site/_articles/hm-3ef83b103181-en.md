*Marking the 115th anniversary of his birth*

A Soviet mathematician and founder of the Siberian school of algebra and logic, a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences (1958), and a Lenin Prize laureate for a series of works applying mathematical logic to algebra and model theory (1964).

He was born on 14 November 1909, or 27 November in the Gregorian calendar, at the Misheronsky glassworks in Moscow Governorate, now the settlement of Misheronsky in Moscow Oblast’s Shatura urban district.

He graduated from the mathematics division of Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mechanics in 1931 and worked at the Ivanovo State Pedagogical Institute, now Ivanovo State University, until 1960.

In 1937 he defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “Torsion-free Abelian Groups of Finite Rank”, supervised by Andrey Kolmogorov.

After defending his doctoral dissertation, “The Structure of Isomorphically Representable Infinite Algebras and Groups”, in 1941, he became a senior research associate at the Steklov Mathematical Institute.

He received the Stalin Prize, second class, for his work in group theory in 1946 and was elected a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1953.

In 1958, Academician Mikhail Lavrentyev invited Maltsev to work at the Siberian scientific centre. He was elected a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences that same year.

He moved to Novosibirsk’s Akademgorodok in 1959 and helped establish the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics at the newly founded Novosibirsk State University. In 1960–1967 he headed the Department of Algebra at the Institute of Mathematics of the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences and the University’s Department of Algebra and Mathematical Logic.

His well-known university textbooks include *Foundations of Linear Algebra* (1948), *Algorithms and Recursive Functions* (1965) and *Algebraic Systems* (1970).
