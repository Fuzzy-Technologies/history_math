Stefan Bergman (1895–1977) was a Polish-born American mathematician and a prominent figure in complex analysis. After the Nazis came to power, he emigrated to the Soviet Union and was a professor at Tomsk University in 1934–1936. He later worked in Tbilisi before leaving for the West and eventually settling in the United States.

Bergman is said to have made a point of speaking the language of every country in which he lived, though almost always with some small flaw. One such linguistic detail once baffled a cultivated secretary who had been found for his department in Tomsk.

Bergman invited her for a conversation and suddenly asked:

“Are you a respectable woman?”

The pause was so heavy that even the department’s folders seemed embarrassed.

It then emerged that he had meant something quite different: was she tidy, and would she keep the department’s affairs “in perfect order”? He had simply chosen a word with rather too many implications! The Russian word he used, poryadochnaya, means “respectable” or “decent”; he was thinking of poryadok, “order”.
