*Marking the 150th anniversary of his birth*

A German mathematician and distinguished historian of mathematics. He became a member of the German Academy of Natural Scientists Leopoldina in 1919 and a member of the International Academy of the History of Science in 1929.

## Education and Teaching

He was born on 31 October 1874 in Wasserburg am Inn, Germany.

He graduated from Munich University and earned his doctorate under Ferdinand von Lindemann.

He worked as a schoolteacher, beginning at a gymnasium in Speyer in 1900, and also taught in Augsburg and Pirmasens. In 1926 he became senior director of the New Realgymnasium in Munich (*Oberstudiendirektor des Neuen Realgymnasiums*).

Alongside his mathematical gifts, he had a talent for languages. He spoke and read Latin, Greek, French, Italian and English.

## Historian of Mathematics

He began researching the history of mathematics in 1905. His contemporaries regarded his studies, ranging from antiquity to the nineteenth century, as exceptionally reliable.

In 1928 Wieleitner completed his habilitation and began lecturing on the history of mathematics at Munich University; he became an honorary professor in 1930.

His books translated into Russian include *How Modern Mathematics Came into Being* (1933), *A Sourcebook in the History of Mathematics, Compiled from Original Sources* (1935), and *The History of Mathematics from Descartes to the Mid-Nineteenth Century* (1960 and 1966). These books continue to be cited today.
