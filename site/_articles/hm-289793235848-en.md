A problem from Henry E. Dudeney’s *The Canterbury Puzzles*.

Seven friends—Adams, Brooks, Cater, Dobson, Edwards, Fry and Green—were spending fifteen days together at the seaside.

At their hotel they had a round breakfast table all to themselves. They agreed that no man should sit twice with the same two neighbours. Since fifteen breakfasts can be arranged under this condition, the plan was feasible. But can the reader work out their seating for every breakfast? They asked the hotel owner to draw up a suitable plan, but he could not manage it.

Can you?

## Solution

Here is how to arrange the seven people:

| Day | 1   | 2   | 3   | 4   | 5   | 6   | 7   |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1   | A   | B   | C   | D   | E   | F   | G   |
| 2   | A   | C   | D   | B   | G   | E   | F   |
| 3   | A   | D   | B   | C   | F   | G   | E   |
| 4   | A   | G   | B   | F   | E   | C   | D   |
| 5   | A   | F   | C   | E   | G   | D   | B   |
| 6   | A   | E   | D   | G   | F   | B   | C   |
| 7   | A   | C   | E   | B   | G   | F   | D   |
| 8   | A   | D   | G   | C   | F   | E   | B   |
| 9   | A   | B   | F   | D   | E   | G   | C   |
| 10  | A   | E   | F   | D   | C   | G   | B   |
| 11  | A   | G   | E   | B   | D   | F   | C   |
| 12  | A   | F   | G   | C   | B   | E   | D   |
| 13  | A   | E   | B   | F   | C   | D   | G   |
| 14  | A   | G   | C   | E   | D   | B   | F   |
| 15  | A   | F   | D   | G   | B   | C   | E   |

At a circular table, A is next to the person at the end of the row.

It is easy to see that the number of arrangements for $n$ people cannot exceed $(n-1)(n-2)/2$. Can you see why?

Methods also exist for many other combinations of numbers of days and people, although they are not obvious.
