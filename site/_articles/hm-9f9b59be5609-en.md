Archimedes (287–212 BC) was one of antiquity’s greatest mathematicians and inventors. His contributions ranged from calculating areas and volumes by methods close to integral calculus to engineering mechanisms and the laws of hydrostatics.

In his treatise *On Floating Bodies*, he formulated the famous principle of the buoyant force exerted by a liquid, now known as Archimedes’s principle.

In the legend Vitruvius recounts, King Hiero asked Archimedes to find out whether his crown was made of pure gold. As Archimedes pondered the problem in the bath, he noticed the water level rising. The idea of displaced volume struck him so unexpectedly that he leapt out of the bath and ran naked through the streets of Syracuse, shouting:

“Eureka!”—“I’ve found it!”

## Notes

Source: Vitruvius, [*The Ten Books on Architecture*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/20239), Book IX, Introduction, §§ 9–12.
