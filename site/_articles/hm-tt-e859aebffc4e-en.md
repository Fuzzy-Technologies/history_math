The Greek cross (Latin *crux immissa quadrata*, “square cross”) is a cross with equal arms, consisting of two identical rectangular bars intersecting at right angles.

The ancient Greeks even marked bread with a cross, considering it a symbol of life.  
The Greek cross has also had other uses and meanings:

- A symbol of the sun god, rain and the four primary elements: air, earth, fire and water.
- An element of the coats of arms and flags of certain countries and cities: Greece, Malta, Switzerland and Rome.
- Together with the crescent, a symbol of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement.
- It was introduced into Christianity around the year 332.

In mathematics, it serves as the symbol for addition and is called “plus”.

Let us discuss some mathematical problems involving the cross.  
Geometry considers a regular, symmetrical cross-shaped figure made from five equal squares. This, too, is called a “Greek cross”.
