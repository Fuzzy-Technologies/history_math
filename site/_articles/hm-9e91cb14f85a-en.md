*Marking the 105th anniversary of her birth*

Isabella Grigoryevna Bashmakova (1921–2005) was an outstanding Soviet and Russian historian of science and mathematician. She received her Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1961. In 1962 she took up a professorship in the history of mathematics and mechanics at Moscow State University. She received the academic title of professor in 1967 and was named an Honoured Professor of the university in 1997.

She was born on 3 January 1921 in Nakhichevan-on-Don, now part of Rostov-on-Don.

## Mathematics, Poetry and the War Years

She moved to Moscow with her parents in 1932. Passionate about poetry since childhood, she knew many prominent poets personally, wrote poems herself and spent a long time choosing between mathematics and poetry. In 1938 she finally entered the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics at Moscow State University.

After the German invasion of the USSR, she was evacuated with the university to Ashkhabad. She worked as a nurse at a military hospital in Samarkand.

Soon after returning from evacuation, she met the mathematician Andrey Ivanovich Lapin, who became her husband and had a profound influence on her. From him she acquired a deep interest in antiquity. The couple investigated several problems together and published their results.

Around this time she also met S. A. Yanovskaya, under whose supervision she began studying the history of mathematics. Her principal interests became mathematics in Russia and ancient Greece and the history of algebra and number theory.

## The History of Mathematical Ideas

She graduated from Moscow State University in 1944 and began teaching there in 1948. In 1949 she became a docent, or associate professor, in its Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics. In 1948 she defended her Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “From the History of Divisibility Theory”, analysing the arithmetical books of Euclid’s *Elements*. She also examined the subsequent development of divisibility theory through the work of R. Dedekind, L. Kronecker and Y. I. Zolotaryov, paying particular attention to Zolotaryov’s methods.

Studying the development of mathematical ideas from the standpoint of modern mathematics became the guiding principle of her research.

She went on to investigate Archimedes’ works and the history of Diophantine analysis. With I. N. Veselovsky, she prepared the Russian edition of Diophantus’ *Arithmetica*, adding detailed commentary (1974).

Her findings became part of general courses on the history of mathematics.

Her studies of ancient mathematics formed the basis of the doctoral dissertation she defended in 1961.

## Recognition and Public Education

She was elected a corresponding member of the International Academy of the History of Science in 1966 and a full member in 1971. In 2001 she received its Alexandre Koyré Medal, a prestigious award recognizing outstanding contributions to the history of science over a scholarly career.

She worked in Moscow State University’s research seminar on the history and methodology of mathematics and eventually became one of its leaders, alongside S. A. Yanovskaya, A. P. Yushkevich and K. A. Rybnikov. Since the 1930s, this seminar had been a centre of historical research on mathematics in the Soviet Union.

Mathematics teachers and pupils will find much to interest them in her *Children’s Encyclopedia* articles on Archimedes, Gauss, Lobachevsky, Chebyshev, Newton, Fermat and Galois, and her articles “How Geometry Arose”, “How People Counted and Wrote Numerals in the Past” and “A Chronicle of Milestones in the Development of Mathematics”.

Her work on the history of algebra and number theory is widely known around the world.
