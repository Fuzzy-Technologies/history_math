*Written to mark the 350th anniversary of his birth*

Farquharson, a Scottish mathematician who made his career in Russia, was one of the first teachers of mathematics in Russia. He was also an astronomer and a specialist in navigation, and a professor at the University of Aberdeen.

Henry Farquharson, known in Russia as Andrey Danilovich Farvarson, was born in Aberdeenshire, Scotland, in 1674. From 1691 to 1695 he studied at Marischal College, which later became part of the University of Aberdeen. After graduating, he worked there as a tutor in mathematics.

In 1698 he was recommended to Tsar Peter I during the tsar’s visit to England. Peter invited him into Russian state service. Farquharson accepted and came to Moscow, where he became known as Andrey Danilovich.

His work in Russia began at the School of Mathematics and Navigation, which opened in Moscow in 1701. It trained specialists for military, naval and civil service. Farquharson and Leonty Magnitsky were its principal teachers, and the school occupied the Sukharev Tower. Farquharson helped draw up the plan for the school and taught navigation, nautical astronomy and geodesy, together with parts of mathematics. Mathematics teaching included arithmetic, geometry, trigonometry, and the use of logarithmic tables and slide rules.

At first Farquharson lectured in Latin through an interpreter, but he soon learned Russian. Alongside teaching, he examined foreign books in his fields and recommended those that would be useful to publish in Russian.

With his participation, Russia’s first *Tables of Logarithms and of Sines, Tangents and Secants* appeared in 1703, followed by Euclid’s *Elements* in 1739, in a Russian translation attributed here to Farquharson.

In 1716 he was transferred to the newly established Naval Academy in St Petersburg. In 1737 he received the rank of brigadier.
