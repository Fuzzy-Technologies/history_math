The well-known American physicist and mathematician Josiah Gibbs (1839–1903) was a man of few words. He generally remained silent at faculty meetings at Yale University, where he taught. On one occasion he broke his silence.

At one meeting, an argument arose over whether the new curricula should devote more attention to foreign languages or to mathematics. Unable to contain himself, Gibbs rose to his feet and delivered an entire speech: “Mathematics is a language!”

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Josiah Willard Gibbs](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%93%D0%B8%D0%B1%D0%B1%D1%81,_%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%BE%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%8F_%D0%A3%D0%B8%D0%BB%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B4) (1839–1903) was an American physicist, mathematician and researcher in mechanics, and one of the founders of vector analysis, statistical physics and the mathematical theory of thermodynamics. Gibbs is commemorated in the Hall of Fame for Great Americans.

Like many physicists of his time, Gibbs recognised how vector algebra could express complicated spatial relationships clearly. In 1881 and 1884 he privately printed two instalments of [*Elements of Vector Analysis*](https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/elementsvectora00gibb) for his students. In 1901 his student Edwin Bidwell Wilson prepared the textbook *Vector Analysis* from Gibbs’s lectures.
