*Marking the 140th anniversary of his birth*

An American mathematician and one of the most significant mathematicians of the twentieth century. He was president of the American Mathematical Society in 1925–1926, received his PhD in 1907 and became a member of the US National Academy of Sciences in 1918 and the Pontifical Academy of Sciences in 1936.

He was born on 21 March 1884 in Allegan County, Michigan, USA.

He graduated from Harvard University in 1905 and worked at Princeton, Harvard and the University of Wisconsin.

## Dynamical Systems and Algebra

He is best known for his work on statistical mechanics and ergodic theory. He laid the foundations of the general theory of dynamical systems. His research also addressed various aspects of relativity and quantum mechanics.

One book deserves particular mention in its Russian translation: G. D. Birkhoff, *Dynamical Systems*, Moscow: Gostekhizdat, 1941 (second edition, 1999). After this book appeared, dynamical systems became a distinct, rapidly developing field of mathematics. A dynamical system is a set of elements for which a functional relationship is specified between time and each element’s position in phase space. This mathematical abstraction makes it possible to study and describe how systems evolve over time.

Specialists in Lie algebras know the celebrated Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem, which gives an explicit description of the universal enveloping associative algebra of a Lie algebra.

## Geometry and Aesthetics

Birkhoff helped write a school textbook using a new axiom system: Birkhoff’s axioms, a system of four postulates for Euclidean geometry. These postulates are based on statements that can be tested by taking measurements with a protractor and ruler.

He proposed a mathematical theory of aesthetics in his 1933 work *Aesthetic Measure*.
