Richard Courant (1888–1972) was a prominent German and American mathematician and the founder of the Institute of Mathematical Sciences in New York.

Once he fell asleep in the middle of a lecture. When he was woken, Courant calmly replied:

“I wasn’t asleep. I was just solving a problem in my dreams.”

Later he did publish a solution — to the very problem he had “seen” in his dream.

Courant liked to say:

“Mathematics is the only science in which you can work even with your eyes closed.”
