There will always be people who dream of getting rich easily. Here is an old mathematical problem, a cautionary lesson wrapped in a fairy tale, from E. I. Ignatyev’s *V tsarstve smekalki* (In the Realm of Ingenuity).

A peasant walks along in tears. “Oh, what a bitter life! Poverty has worn me down completely. A few copper coins are all that rattle in my pocket, and I must soon pay those away too. How do other people manage to make more money out of whatever money they have? If only someone would help me!”

No sooner has he spoken than a devil appears before him.

“Well,” says the devil, “I can help you if you like. It is quite simple. Do you see that bridge over the river?”

“I do!” says the peasant, rather frightened.

“Just cross it and you will have twice as much money. Cross back and it will double again. Every time you cross, you will have exactly twice what you had before.”

“Really?” says the peasant.

“My word on it!” the devil assures him. “But we must strike a bargain. Every time I double your money, you must pay me 24 kopecks after crossing. Otherwise I will not agree.”

“That is no trouble!” says the peasant. “If my money keeps doubling, why should I not pay you 24 kopecks each time? Let us try!”

He crosses once and counts his money. It really has doubled. He tosses the devil 24 kopecks and crosses a second time. Again he has twice as much as before. He counts out 24 kopecks, pays the devil and crosses for the third time. His money doubles once more, but this time he has exactly 24 kopecks—and under the bargain he must give them all to the devil. He pays and is left without a penny.

The peasant has made a bad bargain! Clearly you need to use your own judgement, even when taking someone else’s advice.

How much did he have at the start? Try working it out.

## Solution

Work backwards, step by step.

1. After the last crossing he had 24 kopecks. Before that crossing he therefore had 12.

2. Those 12 kopecks remained after he had paid 24. He must therefore have had 36 after the second crossing and 18 before it.

3. Those 18 kopecks remained after he had paid 24. He must therefore have had 42 after the first crossing and 21 of his own before it.

Answer: 21 kopecks.

Now try solving the problem algebraically too.
