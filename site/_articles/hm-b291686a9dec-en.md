George Pólya (1887–1985) was a celebrated twentieth-century mathematician and teacher, the author of How to Solve It. But he could do more than teach problem-solving: he also had a precise eye for the comic habits of mathematicians.

In one chapter, he paints an almost perfect portrait of the “traditional mathematics professor”:

> The traditional mathematics professor of the popular legend is absentminded. He usually appears in public with a lost umbrella in each hand. He prefers to face the blackboard and to turn his back on the class. He writes $a$, he says $b$, he means $c$; but it should be $d$.

Pólya then adds a few more “hereditary” professorial traits. For example:

> In order to solve this differential equation you look at it till a solution occurs to you.

And this one too:

> Geometry is the art of correct reasoning on incorrect figures.

The funny thing is that anyone who has attended a good mathematics lecture will recognise the picture: the professor may mix up letters, lose an umbrella or look in the wrong direction, yet at some point still manage to explain what matters most.

Further information and sources:

- [George Pólya, Mathematical Discovery](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/bitstream/handle/123456789/194966/George_Polya_Mathematical_discovery.pdf) (in English, PDF), for biographical and teaching context.

- [George Pólya, How to Solve It](https://www.hlevkin.com/hlevkin/90MathPhysBioBooks/Math/Polya/George_Polya_How%20to%20Solve%20It.pdf) (in English, PDF), the passage about the “traditional mathematics professor” and “two lost umbrellas”.

Further sayings from the same passage in How to Solve It:

> Some of his sayings are handed down from generation to generation.

> “This principle is so perfectly general that no particular application of it is possible.”

> “My method to overcome a difficulty is to go round it.”

> “What is the difference between method and device? A method is a device which you use twice.”

> After all, you can learn something from this traditional mathematics professor. Let us hope that the mathematics teacher from whom you cannot learn anything will not become traditional.
