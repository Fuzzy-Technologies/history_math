(On the 76th anniversary of her birth, in the Year of the Teacher and Mentor)

*And so, her name was Tatyana …*

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-1" %}

Recently, I wrote about [women’s role in the history of mathematics](https://teletype.in/Rol-zhenshchin-v-matematike-09-06). Today I want to tell you about another woman mathematician: a wonderful university lecturer, a respected specialist in mathematics teaching, and an accomplished school mathematics teacher. She was also a loving mother and my dear wife, Tatyana Evgenyevna Novozhenina (1947–2013). On her birthday, I want to remember her “road of life”, including her path into mathematics and into teaching it.

## Childhood {#section-1}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Tatyana Novozhenina’s whole life was connected with these cities: childhood and school in Syzran, in the former Kuibyshev Region, now Samara Region; university in Kazan; and work in Syzran and Yelabuga. She regarded Syzran as her hometown, although she was born elsewhere: in Tyrma, Verkhnebureinsky District, Khabarovsk Territory, on 16 December 1947, into a military family.

Her parents, Valentina Egorova and Evgeny Novozhenin, came from Syzran. Most of the Novozhenin family, her relatives on her father’s side, lived there, along with the large Egorov family on her mother’s side. They were all very hardworking people.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

Tatyana also spent most of her youth in Syzran. She attended school there in 1955–1966 and graduated with a silver medal. Even at school, she was torn between mathematics, literature and English. She was good at all three.

Poetry accompanied her entire voyage along the river of life. She often wrote poems, turning happy and sad moments into rhyme.

*Here before me is the familiar building,*

*Where we studied together long ago.*

*Here we gained new knowledge,*

*And ran to class as soon as the bell rang.*

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

## Youth {#section-2}

From 1967 to 1972, Tatyana studied at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Kazan State University, now Kazan Federal University, KFU.

*I left then without tears,*

*To meet my dreams, full of hopes.*

*The train carried me into an unknown distance,*

*Its wheels sang: “Away with sorrow!”*

*And I kept saying: “Farewell, farewell,*

*My beloved city, farewell, farewell!”*

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

Kazan was the city of her youth, those wonderful young years with all the delights of student life. So many memories remained from that time.

*Off to the collective farm to harvest potatoes:*

*Stylish shoes were left at home,*

*Jersey and nylon, each newer than the other;*

*Instead, very dusty boots,*

*And headscarves down to the girls’ eyebrows …*

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-7" %}

Most importantly, here she met the one Tatar who would take her from her native Volga country around Syzran to the banks of the Kama. She and her future husband, Mansur Gilmullin, studied in the same group.

*And entering university,*

*We spent five years within these walls.*

*Youth is carefree, and so are its affairs,*

*But student friendship lasts forever!*

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-8" %}

***A limerick***

*I met a young woman from Syzran,*

*A beauty, and clever, it was clear.*

*Fate brought us together,*

*And gave me a wife.*

*For that, I thank the capital!*

## Mathematics teacher and lecturer {#section-3}

The rest of Tatyana’s life was devoted to teaching mathematics. She first worked as a mathematics teacher at her old school, No. 37 in Syzran, from 1972 to 1976.

She was a good mathematics teacher. Most of her pupils chose technical professions, and four also became mathematics teachers. Her years in school gave her room for poetry too. It was then that she found her closest lifelong friends.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-9" %}

*The digit One declared:*

*“I am queen of all the digits.”*

*But that is surely funny:*

*The queenly One has only*

*A long nose like a knitting needle—*

*A nose, and nothing else.*

She then wanted to change her life dramatically and studied foreign languages at Yelabuga Pedagogical Institute in Tatarstan. Her life nevertheless remained connected with mathematics in one way or another. Her subsequent service to mathematics education took place in Yelabuga, the city of her adult working life. She both studied and taught mathematics there. She taught at School No. 5 and lectured on mathematics teaching methods at Yelabuga State Pedagogical Institute from 1982 to 2013, now the Yelabuga Institute of Kazan Federal University.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-10" %}

> She became an associate professor in 2011.  
> She wrote more than 50 scholarly and methodological articles and teaching manuals on the theory and methodology of mathematics education:  
> *Trigonometric Equations and Inequalities* (1993);  
> *A Teaching Guide to the Theory and Methodology of Mathematics Education for Correspondence Students of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, Mathematics Specialisation* (2002);  
> *Methods for Teaching Mathematical Problem-Solving* (2005);  
> *Methods of Solving Equations through Examples* (2005).

Her research concerned the applied orientation of mathematics teaching. Here are the titles of some of her articles in Russian scholarly journals:

> “On the Applied Orientation of the Geometry Course in Lower Secondary School” (1994);  
> “Applied Problems as a Means of Implementing Different Approaches to Motivation” (2004);  
> “The Preparatory Stage in Teaching Applied Problem-Solving” (2005);  
> “Applied Problems in Pre-Specialisation Classes” (2006);  
> “Laboratory Work in Mathematics Lessons” (2007);  
> “The History of Mathematical Instruments and Their Use in Teaching Mathematics” (2007);  
> “Using Historical Problems to Support an Applied Orientation” (2007);  
> “Applied Mathematics Problems in the Methodological Training of Future Teachers” (2007);  
> “Applied Problems Based on Local History in Mathematics Teaching” (2008);  
> “The Elective Course ‘Applied Geometry’” (2009).

For many years, she was a member of the well-known International Scientific Seminar for Mathematics and Computer Science Lecturers at Universities and Pedagogical Universities, led by A. G. Mordkovich.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-11" %}

In the 1980s, regional professional competitions for future mathematics teachers were held for students at pedagogical universities. Yelabuga Pedagogical Institute belonged to the Volga zone, which included 12 institutions, among them well-known teacher-training institutes in Penza, Kazan, Ulyanovsk, Samara, Saratov, Volgograd and Astrakhan.

The students of the institute’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics were far from easy opponents. For example, in 1988–90, the student team took first place in the zone three years running, in Saratov, Astrakhan and Volgograd. Team members included A. V. Kostin and S. N. Matveev, now associate professors who successfully teach geometry. These results show that training in mathematics and its teaching at the Yelabuga institute was already of a high standard. T. E. Novozhenina trained the future mathematics teachers and these teams. Since then, the faculty has traditionally held such competitions every year between groups of students in years 4–5.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-12" %}

The most interesting parts were the prepared assignments, created on set themes but using the students’ own scripts. These were usually theatrical performances with costumes, music and artistic staging. Themes changed each year: “The Trial of the Curves”, “Solid Bodies”, “Equality, Fraternity, Freedom”—ample scope for mathematical creativity.

The “Story from a Painting” competition was always lively. Students described scenes in famous paintings using mathematical terms. They also enjoyed solving qualitative mathematics problems, completing teaching-method assignments, answering questions about etiquette and historical units of measurement, and performing humorous amateur acts with a mathematical theme.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-13" %}

The mathematics teachers of Tatarstan whom Tatyana Novozhenina trained over 30 years still remember her with gratitude.

## Family {#section-4}

Tatyana’s family began in Syzran in 1976. Yelabuga was the setting for a happy family life with her husband Mansur and the birth and upbringing of their beloved children, daughter Lada and son Timur.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-14" %}

*Your first cry tore through the silence.*

*My fear vanished; doubts receded.*

*You stepped into our beautiful world with hope,*

*A great creation of Mother Nature!*

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-15" %}

Tatyana Evgenyevna remains forever in the memories and hearts of her family and loved ones, friends and colleagues.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-16" %}

P. S. In memory of my wife and at her request, I published a biographical collection of her poems, [The Road of Life](https://ridero.ru/books/doroga_zhizni_3/), in 2018. Tatyana’s entire life was connected with teaching mathematics to schoolchildren and students. In her poems, she described the happy and sad moments of her life. Our children and I collected everything we could find in her archive.

Some of our readers may have known Tatyana Evgenyevna personally or even studied with her. They may find it doubly interesting to know her more closely through her poems, as a fascinating person as well as a woman mathematician, specialist in teaching methods and lecturer. This book is the road of life of my wife Tatyana.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-17" %}

*Where are you running, away from me?*

*Where has the road beckoned you again?*

*Where did you see signs of the coming day?*

*At whose threshold will you stop this time?*
