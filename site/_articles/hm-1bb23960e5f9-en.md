“A father named Nikolai and his son, and a father named Pyotr and his son, went fishing. Nikolai’s catch ends in 2, and his son’s in 3. Pyotr’s catch also ends in 3, and his son’s in 4. Their combined catch equals the square of a natural number. What is Nikolai’s son called?”

## Solution

### 1. Could there be four fishermen?

If all four were different people, their last digits would add to $2+3+3+4=12$, so the combined catch would end in 2. A square cannot end in 2, making this case impossible.

### 2. There must be three fishermen.

One of the sons is also another’s father: a chain of grandfather, father and son. Then the last digits add to $2+3+4=9$, and the total can be a square.

### 3. Work out who is whose son.

- Nikolai cannot be Pyotr’s son, because Nikolai’s catch ends in 2 rather than the 4 required for Pyotr’s son.

- Pyotr can be Nikolai’s son: both Pyotr’s catch and that of Nikolai’s son end in 3. There is no contradiction.

Only one possibility remains: Pyotr is Nikolai’s son.

Answer: Nikolai’s son is called Pyotr.

## Notes

“A problem from Bulgaria” is a conventional name. This classic logic puzzle often appears in Bulgarian problem collections and mathematics competition selections, which is how the name became established in retellings.
