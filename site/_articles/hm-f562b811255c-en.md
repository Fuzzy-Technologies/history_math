Ibn al-Haytham (965–1039), known as Alhazen, was an Arab polymath—a mathematician, physicist and astronomer—who worked in Egypt.

One day he decided to prove that light travels in a straight line and shut himself in a dark room with a small hole in the wall. Worried by his long absence, his servants burst in and found him overjoyed: “I’ve understood it! Light is lines, and shadows are proofs!”

Source: *Ibn al-Haytham: The Man Who Discovered How We See*, Bradley Steffens (2016).
