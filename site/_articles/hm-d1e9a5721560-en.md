[Daniel Bernoulli](https://teletype.in/@history_math/Daniil-Bernulli-17001782-01-07) (1700–1782) was a Swiss physicist, scholar of mechanics and mathematician, and a member of the celebrated Bernoulli family.

He became known for his work in hydrodynamics and probability theory, while his writings on mathematical expectation changed approaches to probability and statistics. He showed how mathematical laws could apply not only to physics but also to the social sciences, including economics.

Daniel Bernoulli formulated the idea from which expected utility theory grew:

“The determination of the value of an item must not be based on its price, but rather on the utility it yields. The price of the item is dependent only on the thing itself and is equal for everyone; the utility, however, is dependent on the particular circumstances of the person making the estimate. Thus there is no doubt that a gain of one thousand ducats is more significant to a pauper than to a rich man though both gain the same amount.”

P. S. In everyday terms, the same increase in wealth will bring more joy to a poor person than to a rich one. That is why Bernoulli proposed calculating expected utility rather than simply the average monetary value of a gain.

## Notes

Source: Daniel Bernoulli, [*Exposition of a New Theory on the Measurement of Risk*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1909829), translated from Latin by Louise Sommer, *Econometrica* 22(1), 1954, pp. 23–36. The Latin original was *Specimen theoriae novae de mensura sortis* (1738).
