One of the three classical problems of antiquity, dating to the fifth century BC, is the famous “squaring of the circle”: “Construct a square equal in area to a given circle.” Only a compass and straightedge may be used, as in elementary geometry. It was not until the nineteenth century that this was conclusively proved impossible.

Nevertheless, other tools can solve the problem — for example, a special curve called the quadratrix, used by Dinostratus in the fourth century BC.

We invite you to experiment with a sheet of paper and scissors: “How can you turn a given circle into a square?”

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

This is a joke problem. Cut a circle out of paper, divide it into four quadrants, then arrange them with their right angles facing outwards and their arcs facing inwards.

- For readers interested in unusual problems: W. Lietzmann, *Vesyoloye i zanimatelnoye o chislakh i figurakh* (Amusing and Entertaining Things about Numbers and Figures), 1963, [p. 208, problem 14 and fig. 75](https://retrolib.pdp-11.ru/books34/1963_litzman.pdf#page=208) (Russian edition).
