One of the earliest known scholars of Alexandria was [Euclid](https://t.me/history_math/55), c. 340–287 BCE. He was one of the most influential mathematicians of all time and is known as the “father of geometry”.

Euclid’s name is almost always associated with his best-known work, the *Elements*, one of the most remarkable works in the history of mathematics. It formed the basis of geometry teaching for more than 2,000 years. Much of our school geometry comes from its first six books, and Euclidean geometry also underpinned classical mechanics.

Ptolemy I, king of Egypt, had heard of Euclid’s extraordinary wisdom and wished to meet him. He asked Euclid to explain the contents of his books. After listening to proofs of the first theorems, he asked whether there was a shorter route to geometry than studying the *Elements*.

Euclid replied: “There is no royal road to geometry.” He meant that learning mathematics takes work, whatever one’s rank. His reply entered history.

Source: Proclus, *A Commentary on the First Book of Euclid’s Elements*.
