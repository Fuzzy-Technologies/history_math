For centuries, gold embroiderers decorated Russian churches, royal clothing and much else with gold thread. In the seventeenth century they were known as “gold craftswomen”.

A puzzle about their apprentices, “The Girls’ Clever Trick”, appears in S. N. Olekhnik, Yu. V. Nesterenko and M. K. Potapov’s [*Starinnye zanimatelnye zadachi* (Old Recreational Problems)](https://ru.djvu.online/file/riv97MIEzHlCn), 2nd ed., Moscow: Nauka, 1988, no. 146, pp. 56–57 and 131 (in Russian).

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

“A gold embroiderer took twenty girls as apprentices and lodged them in eight rooms of her house as shown. Every evening she walked round the house and checked that the rooms along each side contained seven girls.

One day four friends visited the apprentices. They talked until late and stayed the night. All twenty-four girls arranged themselves so that the mistress again counted seven girls along every side of the house.

The next day four apprentices accompanied their four friends away and did not return for the night. The sixteen who remained arranged themselves so that the mistress once more counted seven girls along every side.

How were the girls distributed among the rooms in the last two cases?”

This logic puzzle can be used in preparing primary-school children for mathematics competitions.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

### Answer

For 24 girls, put 1 in each corner room and 5 in each of the other rooms (Figure 2).

For 16 girls, put 3 in each corner room and 1 in each of the others (Figure 3).
