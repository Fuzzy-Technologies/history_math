In an exam, a student draws a right triangle. He labels the legs $1$ and $i$, and the hypotenuse $0$.

The examiner squints.<br>
“What kind of geometry is this?”<br>
“Complex.”<br>
“But there is a triangle!”<br>
“On the board, yes. According to the calculations, it has already… gone to zero.”<br>
“And what do you call that?”<br>
“A figure with a zero hypotenuse. Very convenient: nothing to prove, zero area, and Pythagoras is happy!”

The joke relies on $1^2+i^2=0$. In the complex plane, the distances from $0$ to $1$ and from $0$ to $i$ are both $1$, and the distance between $1$ and $i$ is $\sqrt{2}$.

P.S. The Pythagorean theorem is a rare example of a formula that has become part of humanity’s cultural vocabulary. We wrote about it earlier in [“The Geometry of the Pythagoreans”](https://teletype.in/@history_math/Geometriya-pifagorejcev-teorema-Pifagora-01-08).
