[Diophantus](https://t.me/history_math/142) (third century AD) was the last of the great mathematicians of antiquity and one of the founders of algebra. He lived and worked in Alexandria. Almost nothing is known about his life. His principal work is the thirteen-book *Arithmetica*. Six books survive in Greek and another four in Arabic translation.

An epigram in the *Greek Anthology* presents Diophantus’s life as a puzzle:

“Here lies Diophantus. One-sixth of his life was childhood, one-twelfth was youth, and one-seventh was spent as a bachelor. Five years after his marriage, a son was born who lived half as long as his father. Four years after his son’s death, Diophantus died.”

The conditions of the epigram imply that Diophantus lived for 84 years.

## Notes

Source: [*The Greek Anthology*, XIV.126](https://anthologiagraeca.org/passages/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg7000.tlg001.ag:14.126/).

The *Greek Anthology* (*Anthologia Graeca*) is a collection of ancient and medieval Greek epigrams whose core derives from the anthology compiled by the tenth-century Byzantine scholar Constantine Cephalas. The principal part of the *Palatine Anthology* (Cod. Pal. gr. 23, fols. 1–614) is held by Heidelberg University Library; its continuation (Supplément grec 384, fols. 615–662) is held by the Bibliothèque nationale de France.
