“All is number” sums up the Pythagorean view that numbers explain the world around us. Let's explore a more concrete idea: how square numbers emerge from a “Pythagorean circle”.

Here is an old puzzle from Yemelyan Ignatiev's *In the Realm of Ingenuity*, adapted from the Russian:

> Write consecutive numbers around a circle, starting at 1 and continuing up to a number of your choice: 1, 2, 3, 4, …, n. Once you reach n, continue around the circle in descending order until you reach 1 again: n − 1, n − 2, …, 2, 1. The sum of all the numbers in the circle is the square of n: that is, n multiplied by itself.

For example, to find the square of 7, arrange the numbers as shown:

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Adding them gives $49=7^2$, just as promised.

Try it yourself with 10. Then see if you can explain, or prove, why the method works.

## Proof {#section-35}

The sum of the integers from $1$ to $n-1$ is $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$. The circle contains two copies of this sequence and one additional number, $n$. Thus:

$$
n(n-1)+n=n^2
$$

## Notes {#notes}

Yemelyan Ignatyevich Ignatiev (1869–1923) was a Russian and Soviet mathematics educator and popular science writer. His best-known work, *In the Realm of Ingenuity*, appeared in three volumes between 1908 and 1911.
