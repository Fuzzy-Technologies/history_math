He was born on 15 December 1910 in Vologda.

Vladimir Vladimirovich Morozov was a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, a distinguished algebraist and a professor at Kazan State University.
He was one of the prominent scholars who profoundly shaped the University’s scientific reputation.

All the many facets of Morozov’s work were connected with Kazan and Kazan University. He studied at its Faculty of Physics and Mathematics in 1927–1930. A student of Nikolai Chebotaryov, he became an outstanding representative of the Kazan school of algebra.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Morozov initially worked at several higher education institutions in Kazan. In 1941 he joined the Department of Algebra at Kazan State University and became its head in 1947.

From 1930 to 1938 he worked intensively on classifying primitive groups. He defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “On Primitive Groups”, at Moscow State University in 1938. He then turned to the classification of all homogeneous primitive spaces. His doctoral dissertation, “On Non-semisimple Maximal Subgroups of Simple Lie Groups”, was defended at Kazan State University in 1943, in the midst of the war. This work culminated in a complete classification of maximal non-semisimple subgroups of semisimple Lie groups.

Many of his other investigations in the theory of groups and Lie algebras are also well known. He later became interested in classifying solvable and nilpotent Lie algebras. His papers “On a Nilpotent Element in a Semisimple Lie Algebra” and “On the Centralizer of a Semisimple Subalgebra in a Semisimple Lie Algebra” attracted the attention of leading contemporary algebraists and entered textbooks. At his initiative, research on modular Lie algebras began in the University’s Department of Algebra.

Many Kazan algebraists defended Candidate of Sciences and doctoral dissertations under his supervision: M. M. Arslanov, A. V. Suldin, Ya. I. Zabotin, I. I. Sakhayev, L. D. Eskin, Yu. B. Yermolayev and A. Kh. Dolotkazin.

Alongside his extensive research and teaching, Morozov devoted considerable energy to organizing scientific work. In 1944–1945 he was dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. From 1947 to 1953 he directed the N. G. Chebotaryov Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics at Kazan State University. From its foundation until the end of his life, he served on the editorial board of the national journal *Izvestiya Vysshikh Uchebnykh Zavedenii. Matematika*.

A new specialization in computational mathematics was established in the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics under Morozov’s leadership. He also took an active part in creating the University’s computing centre and Department of Computational Mathematics.

Morozov devoted several studies to the history of mathematics at Kazan State University.

He was brilliantly educated, fluent in French, German and English, and knew Latin. He left many translations from German and English, of both scientific books and fiction. He loved music and played the piano well.

P.S. I was one of Morozov’s last postgraduate students. I consider it my duty to write these words of gratitude about this remarkable man.
