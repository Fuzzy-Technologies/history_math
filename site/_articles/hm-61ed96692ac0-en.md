Mathematicians have joked for years in response to long arguments about drawing balls. 🤹

At the bottom of a deep vessel<br>
Lie $n$ balls, quietly at rest.<br>
Two fools take turns<br>
Drawing them out.

They find the occupation pleasant:<br>
For $t$ minutes they draw,<br>
And after taking out a ball,<br>
They immediately put it back.

Given this condition,<br>
How great is the probability<br>
That the first was a bigger fool than the second,<br>
If he drew $k$ balls?

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

These verses were probably written by V. P. Skitovich.

Viktor Pavlovich Skitovich (1921–1995) was a professor and Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences. From 1972 to 1982 he headed the Department of Mathematical Statistics, Reliability Theory and Queueing Theory in the Faculty of Applied Mathematics at Leningrad State University.

V. P. Skitovich was born in the village of Beryozovik in the Leningrad Region. After leaving school with a gold medal, he entered the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics at Leningrad State University in 1939. Just two months into his studies he was called up for military service. He fought in the Second World War on the Karelian Isthmus as part of Bondarev’s celebrated division, took part in fierce defensive fighting, experienced the bitterness of a forced retreat, and was wounded. After recovering he was sent to the Volkhov Front. His service was recognised with the Order of the Patriotic War, Second Class, and the medals “For Battle Merit”, “For the Defence of Leningrad” and “For Victory over Germany”.

After demobilisation in 1945, V. P. Skitovich returned to the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics and threw himself into his studies. His outstanding results earned him a P. L. Chebyshev Scholarship.

After graduating with honours in 1950, Viktor Pavlovich stayed on as an assistant in the Department of Probability Theory. For his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, he chose the difficult problem of characterising the multivariate normal distribution. His contribution to solving it was decisive: the Darmois–Skitovich theorem became a textbook result.

In 1973, V. P. Skitovich successfully defended his Doctor of Sciences dissertation and was awarded the title of professor. In 1969 he served on the founding council that organised the Educational and Research Centre for Applied Mathematics. From 1972 to 1982 he headed the Department of Mathematical Statistics, Reliability Theory and Queueing Theory in the Faculty of Applied Mathematics and Control Processes, now part of St Petersburg State University. For many years he also served as deputy dean for student affairs, both in the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics and in the Faculty of Applied Mathematics and Control Processes.
