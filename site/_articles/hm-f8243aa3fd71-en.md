*Marking the 320th anniversary of *Arithmetic*. The Year of the Teacher and Mentor in Russia*

“Arithmetic, or the art of reckoning, is an honourable art, free from envy and comprehensible to all, most useful and most praiseworthy, devised and set forth by the finest arithmeticians of ancient and more recent times.”

In the first quarter of the eighteenth century, mathematical education became a task of the state. Tsar Peter I undertook the urgent training of military and technical specialists.

Leonty Filippovich Magnitsky was one of the outstanding figures of Peter I’s era in education and mathematical knowledge.

He was born on 9 June 1669, or 19 June in the Gregorian calendar, in Ostashkov. Biographical accounts give Telyashin as his surname at birth; claims that he studied systematically at the Slavic-Greek-Latin Academy remain disputed.

According to later biographical tradition, Magnitsky impressed Peter I with his learning, and the tsar gave him the surname Magnitsky. In 1701, by Peter’s order, he was appointed a teacher at the School of Mathematics and Navigation in Moscow, where he worked until 1739.

In 1703 he printed his first textbook in Moscow, the encyclopedic *Arithmetic*, which immediately became Russia’s principal mathematics textbook for many years. It remained in use until the mid-eighteenth century and considerably influenced Russian authors’ teaching manuals. To meet practical needs, Magnitsky included information on natural science and technology alongside mathematics.

The full title is *Arithmetic, That Is, the Science of Numbers. Translated from Various Tongues into the Slavonic Language, Gathered into One Work, and Divided into Two Books*. It is a large volume in two books. The first covers arithmetic; the second includes algebra with geometrical applications, elementary trigonometry, geography and navigation.

Magnitsky did much to develop Russian mathematical terminology. His *Arithmetic* used and helped establish Russian terms for multiplier, product, divisor, denominator, square number, extraction of a root, million, billion, trillion, proportion and progression.

His book also frequently uses verse to express general conclusions and advice on teaching. For example, he describes the infinity of the natural numbers as follows:

> Number is infinite,\
> Beyond the reach of our minds.\
> No one knows its end\
> But God, creator of all.

In 1704 a royal decree granted Magnitsky nobility. From 1732 until the end of his life, he headed the School of Mathematics and Navigation.

Mikhail Lomonosov (1711–1765) called Magnitsky’s *Arithmetic* the gateway to his learning.
