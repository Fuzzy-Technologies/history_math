*Marking the 110th anniversary of his birth*

A Soviet physicist, physical chemist, astrophysicist and inventor. He became a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1958 and held the degree of Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences and the title of professor.

He was one of the creators of the Soviet atomic bomb (1949) and hydrogen bomb (1953).

His best-known work concerned combustion, explosions, detonation, nuclear physics, astrophysics and cosmology.

He was three times a Hero of Socialist Labour (1949, 1954 and 1956), received the Lenin Prize in 1956 and won four Stalin Prizes (1943, 1949, 1951 and 1953).

## Education and Scientific Work

He was born on 23 February 1914, or 8 March in the Gregorian calendar, in Minsk.

He studied externally in the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at Leningrad State University but did not graduate. He later attended lectures in the Faculty of Physics and Mechanics at Leningrad Polytechnic Institute. In 1934 he was admitted to postgraduate study at the USSR Academy of Sciences’ Institute of Chemical Physics in Leningrad.

In 1936 he defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “Questions of Adsorption”. In 1939 he defended his doctoral dissertation, “Oxidation of Nitrogen During Combustion”.

From 1938 he headed a laboratory at the Institute of Chemical Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences. In late August 1941 he was evacuated with the institute to Kazan. In 1943 he and his laboratory were transferred to Moscow. From 1946 to 1948 he headed the institute’s theoretical department. He was also a professor at the Moscow Engineering Physics Institute, MEPhI, until 1948.

## Contributions to Mathematics Education

Zeldovich contributed to mathematics education as the author of popular books on higher mathematics.

In 1963 the second, revised and expanded edition of his mathematics textbook *Higher Mathematics for Beginners and Its Applications to Physics* was published (the Russian title is translated here). Mathematicians criticized the book sharply, but it nevertheless went through numerous editions with additions and corrections.

His books on applied mathematics include the following Russian editions, whose titles are translated here:

- Ya. B. Zeldovich, *Higher Mathematics for Beginners and Its Applications to Physics*, 2nd rev. and expanded ed., 1963.
- Ya. B. Zeldovich and I. M. Yaglom, *Higher Mathematics for Beginning Physicists and Engineers*, 1982.
- Ya. B. Zeldovich and A. D. Myshkis, *Elements of Applied Mathematics*, 1967.
- Ya. B. Zeldovich and A. D. Myshkis, *Elements of Mathematical Physics*, 1973.
- Ya. B. Zeldovich, G. I. Barenblatt, V. B. Librovich and G. M. Makhviladze, *The Mathematical Theory of Combustion and Explosions*, 1980.

His scientific colleagues called Zeldovich the chief theorist of the atomic bomb. For his exceptional achievements across several sciences, many Soviet scientists called him the Soviet Leonardo da Vinci.

## Notes

*Higher Mathematics for Beginners and Its Applications to Physics*, written by the theoretical physicist and academician Ya. B. Zeldovich, is intended for senior school pupils, technical-college students and independent learners, and may also be useful to university students.

The book explains the basic concepts of differential and integral calculus as simply, visually and accessibly as possible, then provides the knowledge needed to apply higher mathematics to problems in physics and engineering. It was approved by the USSR Ministry of Education as a textbook for specialist physics and mathematics secondary schools and optional classes.

Zeldovich’s creativity was unique, ranging from the physics of combustion and explosions through nuclear weapons to the deepest questions of astrophysics and cosmology. Andrei Sakharov called him “a man of universal interests”. In Landau’s view, no physicist except Fermi possessed such a wealth of new ideas. Igor Kurchatov said of him: “And yet, Yashka is a genius!” In science, Yakov Zeldovich was a master of his craft.

Zeldovich was a member of the German National Academy Leopoldina, the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, the US National Academy of Sciences, the Royal Society of London and the Hungarian Academy of Sciences. He held honorary doctorates from Cambridge and Sussex universities and belonged to a number of physical societies.

Zeldovich’s scientific work was always classified “secret”. He is said to have been the Soviet academician whose work was most tightly classified.

His father, Boris Naumovich Zeldovich, was buried in 1943 at Kazan’s Arskoye Cemetery.

Zeldovich’s name is still surrounded by legends. It is said that the Leningrad Institute of Chemical Physics once acquired the young laboratory assistant Zeldovich from Mekhanobr, the Institute for the Mechanical Processing of Mineral Resources, in exchange for an oil diffusion pump. At the time, such a pump was worth its weight in gold.

The story goes that this laboratory assistant, who had no higher-education diploma, later decided to defend a Candidate of Sciences dissertation straight away.
