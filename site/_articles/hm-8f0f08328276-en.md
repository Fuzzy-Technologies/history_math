Mathematicians joke and take their revenge.

Felix Klein (1849–1925), the German mathematician known for his classification of geometries, was dean of the mathematics faculty at Göttingen University and ruled his subordinates with an iron hand. In retaliation, one of them devised a problem: “All mathematicians in Göttingen belong to two classes. Some do what they dislike but Klein likes. Others do what they like but Klein dislikes. To which class does Klein himself belong? ‘Neither’ is not an acceptable answer.” The intended answer was “Klein is not a mathematician.”
