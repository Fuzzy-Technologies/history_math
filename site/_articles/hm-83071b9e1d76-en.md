Three people started a game of billiards and agreed that the loser would pay for the use of the table. The first player, an expert, undertook to pocket as many balls as the second and third players combined. They had barely begun when a fourth person came in and joined them.

As an outsider, he received no handicap and played on equal terms with the three others. At the end of the game, they argued about who had lost and should pay under the agreement: the first player had pocketed 5 balls, the second 4, the third 2, and the fourth 4.

Which player should pay for the game, and why?

## Solution

This problem has no unique answer. As soon as the fourth player joined, the agreement should have been revised to define who would count as the loser.

Possible answers include:

1. The first player did not lose, since he beat the fourth. The fourth beat the third, so he did not lose. The second and third together beat the first, so they did not lose either.
2. The fourth player was not bound by the agreement. He pocketed 4 balls, beating the third player, and could leave without paying. Under the agreement, the first player therefore lost.
3. The second and third players were playing against the first under a special agreement. Since the first player beat the fourth, he is relieved of any liability. Since the second, third and fourth were playing on equal terms, the third player lost.
