What is the sum, in degrees, of all the marked angles in the triangles?

P.S. Do you know the sum of the interior angles of any triangle in the Euclidean plane? They add up to a straight angle, or $180^\circ$.

Euclid postulates separately that all right angles are equal. In [Proposition I.32 of the *Elements*](https://aleph0.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/propI32), he draws a parallel line and applies Proposition I.29, which depends on the fifth postulate. According to [Proclus](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/~dwilkins/Euclid/Proclus_Taylor1792/ProclusCommentaryOnEuclid_TTaylor_ElementsBk1Prop32_DraftTranscription.pdf), Eudemus credited the Pythagoreans with the discovery of this theorem.

In non-Euclidean geometries, however, a triangle’s angle sum differs from $180^\circ$. On a sphere, for example, it is easy to imagine a triangle whose angles are all right angles.

## Notes

Answer: $540^\circ$. All the angles of the four triangles add up to $720^\circ$. The four unmarked angles share a vertex and together form a straight angle. Subtracting their sum gives $720^\circ-180^\circ=540^\circ$.
