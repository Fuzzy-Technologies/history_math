*Marking the 215th anniversary of his birth*

A French mathematician. He became a member of the Paris Academy of Sciences in 1839, a foreign corresponding member of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences in 1840 and a Foreign Member of the Royal Society in 1850.

He was born on 24 March 1809 in Saint-Omer, France.

Liouville graduated from the École Polytechnique in 1827 and was invited back as a professor in 1838. In 1850 he obtained the chair of mathematics at the Collège de France; in 1857 he took the chair of mechanics at the Faculty of Sciences in Paris.

## Research

He carried out research in various branches of mathematical analysis.

He contributed to the theory of elliptic functions. In the 1830s he studied the boundary-value problem for second-order linear differential equations now known as the Sturm–Liouville problem.

He gave a rigorous definition of an elementary function and studied whether a given algebraic or transcendental function could be integrated in elementary terms.

He proved that a particular Riccati equation could be integrated by quadratures only in the cases already given by Bernoulli.

In number theory, he proved the existence of transcendental numbers in 1844 and was the first to construct specific classes of such numbers: Liouville numbers and Liouville’s constant.

He proposed an equation for the probability density function—Liouville’s equation—that plays an important role in statistical physics.

## The Journal and Galois’s Legacy

In addition to his scholarly achievements, he was a gifted organizer. Liouville founded the *Journal de mathématiques pures et appliquées*—the Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics—to promote mathematical work. The journal retains its reputation to this day.

In the 1840s Liouville studied Galois’s unpublished work, recognized its importance and published the papers in his journal in 1846, fourteen years after Galois’s death. This publication played an important part in the recognition of Galois’s work.

The Liouville surface and Liouville net, and the Liouville fractional integral, bear his name.

Altogether, Liouville’s publications comprise about four hundred memoirs, articles and notes.
