*Marking the 220th anniversary of his birth*

A Russian mathematician and the author of the first specialized doctoral dissertation in mathematics in Russia, in 1837. He became an Honoured Professor of Moscow University in 1859.

He was born on 7 November Old Style, 19 November New Style, 1804 in Yaroslavl.

## Education and Teaching

He received his early education at the Yaroslavl District School and then at the provincial gymnasium, graduating with distinction. In 1819 he entered the Demidov Lyceum.

A year later he moved to Moscow and entered the Division of Physical and Mathematical Sciences at Moscow University, studying there from 1820 to 1822. He worked as a teacher in Moscow educational institutions.

In 1827 he received a master’s degree for his dissertation *A Discourse on the Daily and Annual Motions of the Earth*. From 1827 to 1830 he taught arithmetic at Moscow University’s Noble Boarding School. In 1832 he was appointed a teacher at the Alexandrinsky Orphan Institute and an assistant to the observing astronomer at the University Observatory.

In 1834 he was elected an adjunct in Moscow University’s Department of Pure Mathematics, and in 1835 he was confirmed as an extraordinary professor. In 1837, after defending *A Discourse on the Integration of Equations with Partial Differentials*, he received the degree of Doctor of Philosophy. In 1842 he was elected and confirmed as an ordinary professor.

## Research and Writings

His research interests were analytic geometry, higher algebra, and differential and integral calculus. Zernov gave a systematic account of partial differential equations as a separate branch of mathematics. He improved and simplified methods of integration proposed by his predecessors and presented these results accessibly in Russian.

In 1842 he received the Academy of Sciences’ Demidov Prize for *Differential Calculus with Applications to Geometry*. His numerous articles appeared in *Scholarly Notes of Moscow University* and the *Magazine of Natural History*.

He is also known for an original design of periscope.

## Notes

Selected works by N. Y. Zernov:

- Zernov, N. Y. *A Discourse on the Daily and Annual Motion of the Earth*. Moscow: P. Kuznetsov Printing House, 1826. 77 pp.

- Zernov, N. Y. *Elementary Foundations of Arithmetic*. Moscow: University Printing House, 1827. 96 pp.

- Zernov, N. Y. *A Discourse on the Integration of Equations with Partial Differentials*. Moscow: University Printing House, 1837. 128 pp.

- Zernov, N. Y. *Differential Calculus with Applications to Geometry*. Moscow: University Printing House, 1842. 482 pp.

Nikolai Yefimovich founded a scholarly dynasty. His son Dmitry Nikolaevich Zernov (1843–1917) was a physician, an Honoured Professor, dean of the medical faculty and rector of Imperial Moscow University. His grandson Vladimir Dmitrievich Zernov (1878–1946) was a physicist, held a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree, and was one of the founding professors and a rector of Saratov University. His great-grandson Dmitry Vladimirovich Zernov (1907–1971) worked in electronics and was a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

Nikolai Yefimovich’s other son, Sergey Nikolaevich Zernov (1840–?), was a mathematician and Moscow University graduate. He taught mathematics at the Moscow Vocational Educational Establishment, as Bauman Moscow State Technical University was then called, and wrote several papers on integrating partial differential equations. Nikolai Yefimovich’s granddaughter Antonina Sergeyevna Zernova (1883–1964) was a bibliographer and historian of the book who worked at the V. I. Lenin Library.
