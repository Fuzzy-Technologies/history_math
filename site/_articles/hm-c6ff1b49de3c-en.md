*Marking the 130th anniversary of his birth*

A Dutch and American mathematician and historian of mathematics. He became a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences in 1930 and a professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1940; in 1972 he was appointed an honorary research associate in Harvard University’s Department of the History of Science.

His fields were mathematics, the history of mathematics, differential geometry and topology, and Marxism.

## From Rotterdam to MIT

He was born on 30 September 1894 in Rotterdam and graduated from the Higher Municipal School in The Hague.

He studied at Leiden University from 1912 to 1922. In 1922 he received his doctorate there under Willem van der Woude, with a dissertation entitled “The Foundations of Multidimensional Differential Geometry in Direct Representation” (the title translated here). From 1924 to 1926 a Rockefeller fellowship took him to Rome and Göttingen.

He was an assistant at the Technical University in Delft from 1917 to 1924.

In 1924 Struik went to Rome to collaborate with the Italian mathematician Tullio Levi-Civita. It was in Rome that he first became interested in the history of mathematics. In 1925 he moved to Göttingen to work with Richard Courant on compiling Felix Klein’s lectures on the history of nineteenth-century mathematics. At this time he also began studying Renaissance mathematics.

In 1926 Struik was offered a position at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where his career continued until 1960. He collaborated with Norbert Wiener on differential geometry and continued his research into the history of mathematics. In 1940 he became a professor at MIT.

In 1930 he was elected a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

## Persecution and Continuing Work

In 1919 he joined the Communist Party of the Netherlands and remained a party member for the rest of his life. He was persecuted during the McCarthy era and suspended from teaching for five years. He was reinstated in 1956 but left MIT in 1960.

His own experience of persecution for his political beliefs led him to lecture on freedom of speech. In 1963–1964 Struik served as a visiting professor at Utrecht University. In 1972 he became an honorary research associate in Harvard University’s Department of the History of Science; he also continued teaching in Mexico, Puerto Rico and Costa Rica.

In addition to textbooks on differential geometry, he wrote two works on the history of mathematics. He also published works by Simon Stevin.

He was a cofounder of the Marxist scholarly journal *Science & Society*. As a historian, Struik also studied the works of Karl Marx and republished some of them.

## Recognition and the Three Ms

In 1989 he received the first Kenneth O. May Prize in the history of mathematics from the International Commission on the History of Mathematics.

When asked on his hundredth birthday how he had managed to work for so long, the professor replied that he had the “three Ms” a man needs to sustain himself: “Marriage, Mathematics and Marxism”.
