*Marking the 282nd anniversary of his birth*

Nikolai Vasilyevich Vereshchagin (1744–1807) was an eighteenth-century Russian military educator and mathematician, and a major general of artillery. Sources disagree about his birth year: some reference works give 1741, while research publications and bibliographical records often give 1744. He belonged to the teachers who turned mathematics from book learning into a practical tool for engineering and artillery.

## Teaching at the Artillery School

Accounts of his early years read almost like an educational novel. The son of a non-commissioned officer, he reached St Petersburg as a teenager, entered an artillery school and then studied in the Artillery and Engineering Corps. He was already recognized as a strong geometry teacher while still young. A service assessment gives a brief but vivid description: “He teaches his class diligently. He is worthy of promotion.”

Contemporaries remembered Vereshchagin above all as a lecturer. Researchers describe a man “whom the educated society of St Petersburg gathered to hear”, whose style influenced pupils and fellow teachers alike. Another telling detail is that he held extra classes at home, in effect offering an additional course for those who wanted more mathematics than the timetable allowed.

## The Posthumous Edition and His Legacy

The principal monument to his teaching is a three-volume edition prepared after his death: *Mathematical Propositions on the Application of Algebra…* (1819), systematically applying algebra to geometry, logarithms, and plane and spherical trigonometry. Separate plates of diagrams were issued with the book, an unusual concern for visual explanation at the time.

Vereshchagin’s career shows that eighteenth-century Russian mathematics lived in the classrooms of military schools as well as in academic treatises, wherever theory had to withstand the test of practice.
