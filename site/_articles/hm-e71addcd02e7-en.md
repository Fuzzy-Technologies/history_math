He was born on 14 April 1629 in The Hague, Holland. He studied at Leiden University and worked in The Hague and Paris.

The outstanding Dutch physicist, astronomer, mathematician and inventor Christiaan Huygens was among the pioneers of the mathematical analysis emerging in his lifetime.

Many people know him primarily as an astronomer. He improved the telescope to give a magnification of 92 times and began studying the heavens. His first fame came when he discovered Saturn’s rings. He described the Orion Nebula and other nebulae in detail and observed double stars.

Huygens also investigated ways of measuring time, invented the pendulum clock (1657) and developed its theory in *Horologium Oscillatorium* (*The Pendulum Clock*, 1673). Besides the theory of clocks, the book contained many outstanding discoveries in analysis and theoretical mechanics. Huygens also carried out quadratures of several surfaces of revolution in it. This and his other works had an enormous influence on the young Isaac Newton. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz studied with Huygens in Paris.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

In 1675 Huygens developed a balance-spring regulator and obtained a French patent for it; Robert Hooke disputed the priority. The regulator made portable watches substantially more accurate and was especially important for navigation.

Huygens made applications of infinitesimal analysis in geometry and physics more rigorous. He found quadratures of the hyperbola, ellipse and circle. In applications of analysis, he investigated the evolutes and involutes of a plane curve (1654), the tractrix, the logarithmic spiral, the catenary and the cycloid. He developed a fairly complete theory of continued fractions and used their convergents as the best approximate values.

Alongside Fermat and Pascal, Huygens laid the foundations of probability theory. He wrote its first textbook, *On Reasoning in Games of Chance* (1657), and introduced the concept of mathematical expectation.

In 1666 Huygens was invited to Paris as one of the first salaried members of the new Royal Academy of Sciences. He was its leading scientist and worked in Paris, with interruptions, until 1681; the Academy had no office of president at that time.

Huygens worked on thermometry and investigated the theory of centrifugal force.

Huygens took part in contemporary debates about the nature of light. He presented his wave theory to the Academy in 1678 and published *Treatise on Light* in 1690.

His final book, *ΚΟΣΜΟΘΕΩΡΟΣ* (*Cosmotheoros*, published in Latin in 1698), is a philosophical and astronomical reflection on the universe. He believed that other planets were also inhabited by people. The book circulated widely in Europe and was translated into English (1698), Dutch (1699), French (1702), German (1703), Russian (1717) and Swedish (1774). By decree of Peter I, it was translated into Russian under the title *Book of the World View*. It was among the earliest printed books in Russian to introduce readers to Copernicus’s heliocentric system.
