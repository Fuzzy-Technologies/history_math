In 1802, Praskovya Alexandrovna Lobachevskaya brought her sons to Kazan: Alexander, the eldest, Nikolay, the middle son, and Alexey, the youngest. They passed the demanding entrance examinations to the gymnasium and were admitted at state expense. The gymnasium, and later the university, became a second home to the Lobachevsky brothers for many years.

The following incident, from Nikolay Lobachevsky’s entrance examination at Kazan Gymnasium in 1802, when he was nine, shows his remarkable mathematical ability even as a child.

The examiners were Grigory Ivanovich Kartashevsky, the teacher of pure mathematics, and Ilya Fyodorovich Yakovkin, the gymnasium’s inspector. Nikolay solved an ancient but difficult pool-filling problem quickly and in his head.

“A pool receives water through four pipes. The first fills it in 1 day, the second in 2 days, the third in 3 days, and the fourth in 4 days. How long will it take to fill the pool if all four pipes are opened at once?”

This was when Grigory Kartashevsky first singled Nikolay out. He realised that he was dealing with a highly gifted child.

How would you solve the problem?

## Solution

Answer: $12/25$ of a day. Use the usual method for pool-filling problems. If all four pipes are opened simultaneously, in one day they fill $1/1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4 = 25/12$ of the pool. They therefore need $12/25$ of a day to fill the entire pool.

## Notes

P.S. Pool-filling problems, which go back to Hero of Alexandria in the first century AD, have always appeared in arithmetic textbooks and mathematics curricula.

- M. S. Kolesnikov, [*Lobachevsky*](https://djvu.online/file/ZLWRekPLKQGnx) (Moscow: Molodaya Gvardiya, 1965), p. 13 (in Russian). This biographical account gives the four-pipe version with filling times of 1, 2, 3 and 4 days.
