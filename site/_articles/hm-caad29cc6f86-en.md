*Marking the 120th anniversary of his birth*

A mathematician, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences (1950), professor (1951) and Honoured Scientist of the Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (1967).

## Education and Early Textbooks

He was born on 4 October 1904 in Uralsk. In 1924 he moved to Kazan and entered the chemistry division of an industrial technical college. Through independent study, he completed his secondary education. In 1927–1929 he taught mathematics and physics in Kazan schools. In 1929–1931 he taught mathematics at the Technical College of Socialist Agriculture. In 1931–1932 he worked as an assistant in the Department of Descriptive Geometry at the Kazan Institute of Municipal Construction Engineers, and in 1932–1935 in the Department of Mathematics at the Kazan Agricultural Institute.

In the early 1930s he published textbooks in Tatar for secondary and higher educational institutions: *Technical Drawing* (1931), *Descriptive Geometry* (1932), *Mathematics for Adults* (1932), *A Physics Book for Practical Classes* (1931) and *A Book for Practical Classes in Mathematics* (1932). These are translations of the Russian titles given in the source.

He graduated from Kazan University in 1934. In 1931 he had been admitted to extramural postgraduate study at the university, working under Professor N. N. Parfentyev. He defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “Singular Points of Systems of Differential Equations”, in 1936 and his doctoral dissertation, “On Stable and Unstable Centres”, in 1949.

## Teaching and Research

He worked for many years at Kazan State Pedagogical Institute, from 1935 to 1971. He headed the Department of Mathematical Analysis in 1937–1971 and was dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics in 1943–1956. He also directed postgraduate studies in mathematics teaching methods at the institute.

His principal research concerned oscillation theory and problems of stability of motion. He formulated and solved inverse problems in the qualitative theory of differential equations.

In 1954–1962 he published a number of articles on teaching methods in the journal *Sovet mäktäbe* (“Soviet School”), including “On Solving a System of Equations”, “Methods of Calculation with an Abacus in Grade 5” and “On Calculating the Circumference of a Circle”.
