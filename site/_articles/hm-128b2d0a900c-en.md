*Marking the 110th anniversary of his birth*

An American amateur mathematician, writer, science popularizer and inventor of mathematical games and puzzles. He was elected a Fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences in 1997 and received the American Physical Society’s Forum Award for Promoting Public Understanding of the Relationships of Physics and Society that year.

## Life and Writing

He was born on 21 October 1914 in Tulsa, Oklahoma, USA.

He graduated from the College of the University of Chicago with a bachelor’s degree in philosophy.

During the Second World War he served in the US Navy. After the war he earned his living as a writer, publishing books and contributing articles to magazines and newspapers. In the early 1950s, Gardner edited *Humpty Dumpty* magazine and wrote features and stories for several children’s magazines.

## Recreational Mathematics

His paper-folding puzzles led to work for the American popular science magazine *Scientific American*, where he ran the column on mathematical games and recreations.

Gardner’s articles and books on recreational mathematics became especially popular. For him, recreation meant the excitement and interest of learning.

He is also known for several science-fiction stories, including “The Island of Five Colors” and “The No-Sided Professor”, and for his annotations of Lewis Carroll’s *Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland* and *Through the Looking-Glass*.

Gardner’s work includes philosophical essays, pieces on the history of mathematics, mathematical magic and “comics”, popular science essays and puzzles that test one’s ingenuity. The Gardner style combines clarity, vividness and persuasive exposition with sparkling, paradoxical thought and scientific ideas of novelty and depth. These are true masterpieces of popular writing about mathematics.

## Books Published in Russian

He published more than seventy books. Among the best known in Russian are the following editions; their Russian titles are translated here:

- *Mathematical Puzzles and Diversions* (1971)
- *Mathematical Pastimes* (1972)
- *Mathematical Novellas* (1974)
- *Mathematical Wonders and Mysteries* (1977)
- *I Have an Idea!* (1982)
- *Tic-Tac-Toe* (1988)
- *From Penrose Tilings to Reliable Ciphers* (1993)
- *Classic Puzzles* (2007)
- *New Mathematical Recreations* (2008)
- *Entertaining Mathematics* (2009)
