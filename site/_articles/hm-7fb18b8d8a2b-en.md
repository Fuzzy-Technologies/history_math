*Marking the 95th anniversary of his birth*

An outstanding Soviet and Russian algebraist. He was a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences (1976) and the Russian Academy of Sciences, and received the USSR State Prize in 1968.

He was born on 12 February 1929 in the village of Bolshoy Morets, Yelan District, now in Volgograd Region.

He entered Saratov University in 1947 and, as its best student, transferred to Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1951, graduating in 1952. He was a pupil of I. R. Shafarevich, an academician from 1991.

He received his doctorate in physical and mathematical sciences in 1960. He began teaching in Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1963 and held a professorship from 1976.

He was a researcher in the Department of Algebra at the Steklov Mathematical Institute and dean of Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1977–1980.

From 1972 until his death, he headed the Faculty’s Department of Higher Algebra.

Until 2000, he directed the Algebra School of Moscow University, a leading Russian research school founded by Otto Schmidt and Alexander Kurosh.

His principal research areas were finite groups, non-associative algebras, Lie algebras, cohomology groups, combinatorial group and algebra theory, representation theory and integral lattices. His most important achievements included solving the restricted Burnside problem for every prime exponent (1959) and establishing a connection between simple Lie algebras and Cartan’s infinite-dimensional algebras.

He wrote numerous papers, books and textbooks, including the well-known university text *Introduction to Algebra* in three parts, which went through several editions and was translated into many languages. He also co-authored *Linear Algebra and Geometry* with Yuri Manin.

Read about Kostrikin and other well-known scholars in [*History of Mathematics* (in Russian)](https://ridero.ru/books/istoriya_matematiki/).

## Notes

P.S. Alexei Kostrikin was a gifted university teacher who trained several generations of algebraists. I am grateful for my personal meetings with him at research seminars in Moscow State University’s Department of Algebra during my professional development course in 1979, and for our discussions of my work on a Candidate of Sciences dissertation on the cohomology of simple Lie algebras of Cartan type. He was very attentive to us, colleagues from other universities, and always engaged deeply with the scientific problems arising in our work.
