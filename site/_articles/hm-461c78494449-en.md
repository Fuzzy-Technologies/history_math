*Marking the 120th anniversary of his birth*

A Soviet mathematics educator and historian of mathematics, with a Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences degree. He wrote *The History of Mathematics at School*, among the finest teaching books on the subject. Unfortunately, we could not find authentic photographs of him for this biography—we would welcome any that readers could share—but his books have secured his place in the history of scholarship.

## Education and Teaching

He was born on 12 July 1904 in Sekuryany, Khotin District, Bessarabia Governorate.

He completed gymnasium in Chișinău, studied in the physics and mathematics division at the University of Vienna and graduated from the mathematics division of Rome’s La Sapienza University in 1929. He taught mathematics in Sokyriany, Mărculești and Bălți.

In 1940–1948 he headed the Mathematics Department at the Ion Creangă Chișinău State Pedagogical Institute and also taught at Chișinău State University. From 1948 he was a docent in the physics and mathematics division of the Tiraspol Pedagogical Institute, now the Taras Shevchenko Pridnestrovian State University. In 1952–1963 he headed the Department of Mathematics and Mathematics Teaching Methods that he had established there. He also organized a Department of Geometry, Elementary Mathematics and Methods of Teaching Them.

## Books on the History of Mathematics

He wrote teaching books and reference works on the history of mathematics that were translated into Bulgarian, Kazakh, Lithuanian, Croatian and Japanese.

The teaching book *The History of Mathematics at School* was originally published in three volumes in Moldavian in 1960, 1963 and 1966. Two single-volume Russian editions appeared in 1964 and 1970. The expanded three-volume Russian version prepared by the author was published posthumously in 1981–1983 and translated into languages of the USSR and other countries.

The books consist of short articles presenting the history of mathematics in a form accessible to teachers and pupils. They draw on existing historical scholarship and the author’s thirty years of personal experience teaching in secondary and higher education. Each has two parts: the first for classroom lessons and the second for extracurricular activities. The books conclude with historical arithmetic, algebra and geometry problems by notable mathematicians of earlier centuries, and a chronological reference section, “Centuries and Years”.
