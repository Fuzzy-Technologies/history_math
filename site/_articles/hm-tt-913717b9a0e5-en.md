> *Nature is simple and does not indulge in unnecessary causes.*

[Isaac Newton](https://teletype.in/%40history_math/Isaak-Nyuton-malchik-igrayushchij-na-morskom-beregu-10-18), one of the greatest minds in the history of science, is known not only for his laws of mechanics and theory of gravitation, but also for his love of mathematical puzzles.

One such problem, attributed to Newton, asks: *“Plant nine trees in ten rows, with three trees in each row”* (A farmer wants to plant 9 trees in such a way that there are 10 rows of 3 trees. How does he do this?). Seemingly simple at first glance, this problem has become a classic example of geometric combinatorics and still inspires mathematicians and puzzle enthusiasts.

The problem was expressed in verse:

*Your aid I want, nine trees to plant
In rows just half a score,
And let there be in each row three,
Solve this: I ask no more.*

The source gives this free Russian rendering, translated here into English:

*Help me, I ask: arrange nine trees
In rows, if you would be so kind.
Three in each row, and ten in all;
A solution will ease my troubled mind.*

(Note: in the original, “score” is an old word meaning 20, from the Old English *scoru*, a notch on a tally stick. Thus “half a score” means 10; the Russian equivalent is “a group of ten”.)

Since there are only nine trees, some must stand at the intersection of several rows. In this arrangement, some trees must stand at the intersection of four or more rows.

One solution, proposed by Newton himself (see the first illustration), places the trees at distances that allow 3 horizontal straight lines, 1 vertical straight line through the centre, and diagonal lines joining trees in rows of 3.

Can you think of other solutions? Then compare them with the ones below.

Newton’s nine-tree problem is more than a puzzle. It reminds us that mathematics is also about beauty, harmony and imagination!
