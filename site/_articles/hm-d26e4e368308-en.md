Today mathematicians are joking about mathematicians.

The Soviet mathematician Nikolai Maksimovich Günther (1871–1941), a professor and corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences, wrote in his article [On A. A. Markov’s Teaching](https://www.mathnet.ru/rus/im5679):

“Once, during a conversation about Russell’s definition of mathematics, Andrey Andreyevich was asked what mathematics was. He replied, ‘Mathematics is what Gauss, Chebyshev, Lyapunov, Steklov and I do!’”

P.S. Joking aside, defining the subject matter of mathematics is a serious question that still provokes much debate. We discussed it in [On the Subject of Mathematics: A Brief Account](https://teletype.in/@history_math/O-predmete-matematiki-kratkaya-harakteristika-06-09), and in more detail in [Что такое Математика? Нәрсә ул Математика? What is Math?](https://teletype.in/@history_math/What-is-Math) (both in Russian).

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Andrey Andreyevich Markov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Markov) (1856–1922) was a Russian mathematician, an academician and a member of the St Petersburg Mathematical Society.

A. A. Markov laid the foundations of the theory of the stochastic processes that bear his name by studying discrete-time chains. The theory was later extended to processes in continuous time. For a chain, the Markov property means that “the next state of the process depends probabilistically only on its present state”. In Venttsel’s more general formulation, the process’s “future” depends on its “past” only through its “present”.

When the theory was developed, it was considered abstract, but it is now applied throughout practical life. The theory of Markov chains grew into a vast and highly important field of research: the theory of Markov stochastic processes, which in turn underlies the general theory of stochastic processes.

A. A. Markov substantially advanced his predecessors’ classical work on the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem, extending those results to Markov chains. His discovery made an immense contribution to the theory of stochastic processes and to probability theory as a whole.
