Leonardo of Pisa (1170–1240), known as Fibonacci, once decided to calculate how many pairs of rabbits could be produced in a year if every adult pair produced a new pair each month and each newborn pair began breeding in its second month. But he became so absorbed in the problem that he forgot about the real rabbits he kept in his garden. When Leonardo finally went out to check on them, he found that they had escaped, leaving him with a purely theoretical result: the famous Fibonacci sequence.
