One of the founders of modern topology, Academician Pavel Sergeyevich Alexandrov (1896–1982), had the nickname “Dog”. It came from a witty inscription he wrote in a copy of his first book, which he gave to another outstanding topologist, his friend Pavel Samuilovich Urysohn: “P. S. U. from P. S. A.” In Russian the inscription reads «ПСУ от ПСА». Without the capitals, *psu ot psa* means “to a dog from a dog”.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Pavel Sergeyevich Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Alexandrov) (1896–1982) was a Soviet mathematician, professor at Moscow State University and member of the USSR Academy of Sciences. His principal work concerned topology, set theory, the theory of functions of a real variable, geometry, the calculus of variations, mathematical logic and the foundations of mathematics.

He introduced a new concept of compactness. Together with P. S. Urysohn, Alexandrov demonstrated its full importance. In particular, he proved the first general metrisation theorem and the celebrated theorem that every non-compact locally compact Hausdorff space admits a one-point compactification.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

[Pavel Samuilovich Urysohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Urysohn) (1898–1924) was a Soviet mathematician. His principal results were in topology, nonlinear differential equations and geometry. Urysohn also proved a theorem in convex geometry stating that the ball has the greatest volume for a fixed mean width. Together with Alexandrov, he founded the Soviet school of topology. He created a new field within topology: dimension theory. In 1921–1922, he delivered Russia’s first course in topology at Moscow University.
