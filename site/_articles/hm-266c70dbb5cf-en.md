*Marking the 105th anniversary of his birth*

A Soviet mathematics educator and specialist in teaching methods. He was a Doctor of Pedagogical Sciences and a professor at Belarusian State University. He conducted research into mathematics teaching and the history of mathematics education.

## Teacher and Historian of Education

He graduated from Belarusian State University in 1950.

He taught in schools in Minsk Region from 1946 to 1961. During those fifteen years he published many articles on improving classroom and extracurricular work in mathematics. In that period, Metelsky became interested in the rich legacy of the early twentieth-century Russian movement to reform mathematics teaching. He defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “The Progressive Legacy of the Movement to Reform Mathematics Teaching”, in 1967 and published the monograph *Essays on the History of Mathematics Teaching Methods: On the Reform of Mathematics Teaching in Secondary School* in 1968.

From 1961 he worked at Belarusian State University, remaining there until 1997. He was a professor in its Department of Geometry, Topology and Methods of Teaching Mathematics.

## Mathematics Teaching and Teacher Education

The reforms of school mathematics education in the 1960s and 1970s called for more effective teaching methods. Metelsky presented his approach to teaching mathematics as a means of developing pupils’ thinking in *Didactics of Mathematics* (1975; the Russian title translated here). This book became the first university course in mathematics teaching methods, as distinct from comparable courses for pedagogical institutes.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

The monograph *The Psychological and Pedagogical Foundations of Mathematics Teaching* (1977; the Russian title translated here) sets out the methodological foundations of this approach.

Metelsky’s doctoral dissertation addressed how to improve the preparation of mathematics teachers at universities by developing scholarly foundations for teaching methods and effective approaches to school instruction.

He wrote numerous works on current questions in mathematics teaching methods.

Nikolai Vladimirovich was awarded the Order of the Patriotic War and ten medals.
