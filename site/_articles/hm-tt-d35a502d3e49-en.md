In the 12th century, as Europe entered the age of the Crusades and Rus' experienced feudal fragmentation, the Novgorodian monk Kirik wrote a work now called the first mathematical treatise of ancient Rus'. His book, Teaching by Which a Person May Know the Numbers of All Years (1136), not only revealed the secrets of the church calendar but also demonstrated an understanding of fractions, time cycles and astronomy remarkable for its period.

His calculations include this problem:

*“How many months, weeks, days and hours has a person lived who turned 26 in the year 1136?”*

Try calculating it yourself before looking at the answer at the end of the article.

## Historical background {#section-1}

Here are Kirik's own old instructions for calculation, translated from the Teaching:

*1. In the beginning God created heaven and earth and every visible creature; from that time \[we count\] 6644 years to the present.*

*2. Knowing the number of months. From the beginning of the creation of this world to the present, 79 728 calendar months have passed. If you wish to count the months from Adam to the present, or to any time you choose, count 12 months in each year.*

*3. Teaching on counting weeks. From Adam, those same 6644 years contain 346 673 weeks and 3 days. Let anyone wishing to determine the number of weeks know that a year has 52 weeks and one day and a quarter of a day, and after four years this quarter becomes one day. First count the weeks in all the years, then the extra days and the quarters; calculate \[them\] at 7 days per week and add them to the total. Thus the desired result will be obtained correctly.*

*4. How to know the number of days. Let it be known that those same years contain 2 426 721 days. If you wish to know how many days have passed to the present day, or to any other day, first count 300, and 60, and 5 days per year. Having added this quantity, count how many leap days you have and add them to all the \[previously obtained\] days; in this way you can calculate correctly.*

*5. Investigation of the \[number of\] hours. From Adam, those same years contain 29 120 652 hours, excluding the night hours. Let those wise men (?) or lovers of calculation or rhetoricians who wish to grasp this know that there are 12 hours in a day. Thus weeks, months and years are formed. Just as a city is built little by little and becomes great, so knowledge grows little by little.*

Kirik is the first Russian mathematician known to us by name. He was a monk of the Antoniev Monastery in Novgorod who lived in the first half of the 12th century. His exact birth and death dates are unknown; they are usually placed at approximately 1110–1158. He combined religious service with scientific investigation, something rare in ancient Rus'. His work concerns the calculation of church festivals and chronology.

In his manuscript, Kirik calculates in detail how many years, months, weeks and days passed from the creation of the world to the year in which he wrote. The Bible gave church historians grounds to assert that, according to church chronology, the world was created 5508 years before the birth of Christ.

Kirik used the Byzantine era reckoned from the Creation. He calculated the length of a year as 365 days + 1/4 day, allowing for leap years.

He used the concept of the “Great Indiction”, the 532-year cycle adopted in the Byzantine tradition after which the dates of Easter repeat.

Scholars note that Kirik was ahead of his European contemporaries in his use of fractions. In the 12th century, for example, fractions had not yet been systematized in the West; this occurred only in the 13th century through Fibonacci.

His work is more than a set of calculations: it is an attempt to understand divine order through mathematics. As Kirik himself wrote: “Everything is counted and arranged in numbers, for number is the measure of time.”

## Solution {#section-2}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

According to an autobiographical addition to the Teaching, the author was 26 when he completed the work in 1136. The problem is therefore really about Kirik himself.

- 12 × 26 = 312 months: Kirik has this right.
- 365 × 26 + 7 = 9497 days: Kirik has this right.
- 9497 : 7 ≈ 1356.7: Kirik may have rounded or made an error.
- 24 × 9497 = 227928 hours: counting 24 hours a day and including the night hours; Kirik may have used a different principle.

**Answer:** 312 months; 1356 weeks; 9497 days; 227 928 hours.
