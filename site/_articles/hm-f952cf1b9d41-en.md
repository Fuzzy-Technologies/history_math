*Marking his 80th birthday and the 50th anniversary of Rubik’s Cube*

Ernő Rubik is a Hungarian inventor, engineer, sculptor and professor of architecture.

He is known worldwide for his three-dimensional puzzles and toys.

His well-known inventions include:

- Rubik’s Cube (1974).
- Rubik’s Snake (1981).
- Rubik’s Magic (1985).
- Rubik’s 360 (2009).

## Biography and Studio

He was born on 13 July 1944 in Budapest.

In 1967 he graduated from the Faculty of Architecture at the Technical University of Budapest with a degree in architectural engineering. He continued with postgraduate study in sculpture and interior design.

He worked as an architect in 1971–1975.

He then returned to the academy and became an associate professor at the Budapest Institute of Decorative and Applied Arts. He became a professor in 1987.

In the early 1980s he became editor of a games and puzzles magazine, *És játék* (“…and Play”). In 1983 he founded his own studio, Rubik Stúdió, which designed furniture and developed puzzles.

He cofounded the Hungarian Academy of Engineering, Magyar Mérnöki Akadémia, in 1990 and was its president until 1996. The international Rubik Foundation was established at the academy to support talented young inventors. Since 1996 he has headed Rubik’s studio.

## The Mathematics of the Cube

The idea for the “Magic Cube” emerged as Rubik developed a physical model to help design students think about geometry and the movement of parts in a three-dimensional structure.

The mathematics of Rubik’s Cube comprises methods for studying its properties from an abstract mathematical perspective. It studies algorithms for solving the cube and evaluates those algorithms, drawing on graph theory, group theory, computability theory and combinatorics.

The number of distinct reachable states of the $3\times3\times3$ Rubik’s Cube is $43\,252\,003\,274\,489\,856\,000$, more than 43 quintillion.

The Rubik’s Cube group is defined as a subgroup of the permutation group on the 48 sticker positions on the moving pieces, generated by six face turns: L, R, F, B, U and D (Left, Right, Front, Back, Up and Down).

In 2010 it was rigorously proved that any reachable configuration can be brought to the solved state in no more than twenty moves. A move is a 90° turn of one face in either direction or a 180° turn.

An interesting fact: only six per cent of the world’s population can solve Rubik’s Cube.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

In Hungary, Ernő is regarded as a national hero. He became the country’s first millionaire and donates part of his income to foundations supporting inventors.

More than 350 million authorized copies of Rubik’s Cube have been sold. That is one in every three puzzles worldwide.

In 1980 Rubik’s Cube received Hungary’s national prize for the best invention and won best-toy competitions in the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany and France.

In many restaurants the cube was an essential item on the table alongside the salt and pepper shakers.
