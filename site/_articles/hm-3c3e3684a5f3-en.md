During a storm, a captain ordered half of the thirty bales carried by two merchants to be thrown overboard. The merchants hesitated: neither wanted to lose his own goods. Seeing this, the captain said, “The sailors will arrange all thirty bales in a circle. We shall go round it, throwing out every ninth bale until half have gone.” One merchant bribed the sailors, who arranged the bales so that all fifteen left on deck belonged to him. How were they arranged?

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

## Solution

Draw a circle and mark thirty points on it to represent the bales.

Number them from 1 to 30.

Start counting at 1. When you reach 9, cross it out: bale 9 is thrown overboard.

Continue counting. The next ninth bale is number 18; cross it out.

Keep crossing out every ninth number among those not yet crossed out.

Fifteen numbers will be crossed out: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 16, 18, 19, 22, 23, 24, 26, 27, 30.

Place the other merchant’s bales in these positions and your own in the remaining positions: 1, 2, 3, 4, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 20, 21, 25, 28, 29. That is what the cunning merchant did.

He asked for this arrangement: 4 of his own, 5 of the other merchant’s, 2 of his own, 1 of the other’s, 3 of his own, 1 of the other’s, 1 of his own, 2 of the other’s, 2 of his own, 3 of the other’s, 1 of his own, 2 of the other’s, 2 of his own and 1 of the other’s.

## Notes

An old mnemonic encodes the sequence $4,5,2,1,3,1,1,2,2,3,1,2,2,1$. Memorise six Russian male names: Полуект (Poluekt), Аника (Anika), Павел (Pavel), Елизар (Yelizar), Евтех (Yevtekh), Влас (Vlas). Under each Cyrillic а write 1, under е write 2, under и write 3, under о write 4 and under у write 5. This gives:

По(4)лу(5)е(2)кт — А(1)ни(3)ка(1) — Па(1)ве(2)л — Е(2)ли(3)за(1)р — Е(2)вте(2)х — Вла(1)с
