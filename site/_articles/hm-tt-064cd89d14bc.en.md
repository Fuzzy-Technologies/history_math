> *History of Mathematics*

> As the French mathematician Poisson once said, “Life is adorned by two things: doing mathematics and teaching it.” That is true of my life too. I studied mathematics at school and then at university, and for more than fifty years now I have taught mathematics and its history at university to future teachers and scholars.

> I decided to write a book on the history of mathematics that would be interesting and useful to pupils and students as well as schoolteachers and university lecturers.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

## What the book is about {#section-1}

There are many books on the history of mathematics, but they are usually written for professional mathematicians. They all present the history thematically, solely as a part of science. I have presented it chronologically, returning to certain areas of mathematical history several times. This approach is called concentric.

For example, when discussing Lobachevsky's non-Euclidean geometry, I show it in relation to world history, Russian history and the Kazan mathematical school: in different historical circles, or “concentres”. The book can be classed as popular science.

## How the book can help you {#section-2}

The book introduces the history of mathematics as part of world culture, connecting it with the development of civilisations. Its richly illustrated material will bring you closer to the people who made mathematical history. This makes it interesting to everyone concerned with the history of science, as well as to specialists.

These are things you will not hear about in school lessons or university lectures. Yet without knowing a science's past, one cannot understand its present or see its future.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

The book has six chapters:

Chapter 1. Introduction to the History of Mathematics

Chapter 2. The Origins of Mathematics

Chapter 3. The Period of Elementary Mathematics

Chapter 4. The Period in Which the Mathematics of Variable Quantities Was Created

Chapter 5. The Period of Modern Mathematics

Chapter 6. The History of Mathematics in Russia

There are also exercises for independent study and a glossary of terms.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

## Who the book is for {#section-3}

- School pupils: it broadens their horizons and gives them a better understanding of mathematics' role in contemporary society.

- Students: studying mathematical history through the experiences of great scholars can help them reach new heights in science without repeating old mistakes. As the great mathematician Leibniz said, “Learning the method from outstanding examples develops the art of discovery.”

- Schoolteachers: under the new standards for secondary education, mathematics should be taught within a cultural and historical context.

- University lecturers who use the history of mathematics in their work: the book has been approved by the Educational and Methodological Association, reviewed, and recommended as a university study guide.

## About the author {#section-4}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

[Mansur Fayzrakhmanovich Gilmullin](https://kpfu.ru/mansur.gilmullin) is an associate professor in the Department of Mathematics and Applied Informatics at Kazan Federal University.

He is a mathematician and algebraist, specialising in the history of mathematics, mathematics education, and methods of teaching mathematics and its history. He has more than 45 years of experience teaching mathematics and the history of mathematics.

He is the [author](https://scholar.google.ru/citations?hl=ru&user=r5KtCyEAAAAJ&pagesize=1000&view_op=list_works&sortby=pubdate) of two hundred scholarly and popular-science articles and books.

He defended a dissertation entitled “Developing the Historical Component of Students' Mathematical and Methodological Culture through Teaching the History of Mathematics at a Teacher-Training University”.
