Paul Erdős (1913–1996) was one of the most prolific mathematicians of the twentieth century. Beyond theorems, he had another talent: turning serious matters into jokes you never forget.

Once, someone asked how old he was. Erdős answered without blinking:

“Two and a half billion. When I was very young, scientists thought the Earth was two billion years old; now they say it is four and a half billion years old.”

Then the joke took on a life of its own. He recalled once giving a lecture entitled “My First Two and a Half Billion Years in Mathematics”, for which students drew a timeline showing him… riding a dinosaur!

The moral: when you are a mathematician, age is merely the difference between two estimates.
