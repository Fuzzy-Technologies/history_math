*Marking the 165th anniversary of his birth*

Nikolai Alexandrovich Rybkin, born on 15 April 1861 Old Style, or 28 April in the Gregorian calendar, and deceased in 1919, was a Russian mathematics educator. His school geometry and trigonometry textbooks and problem books remained in use as standard texts into the 1970s.

He graduated from the Vladimir Gymnasium with a gold medal in 1879 and from Moscow University in 1883. His long teaching career in Moscow began immediately afterwards, first at K. K. Mazing’s private real school in 1883–1887, then at the Lazarev Institute of Oriental Languages in 1887–1906. He also taught part-time at the Moscow Practical Academy of Commercial Sciences in 1901–1902.

His name became firmly associated with school mathematics because he gave teachers and pupils something useful every day: a clear course and extensive, well-organized practice, rather than general observations on teaching methods. His geometry and trigonometry problem books were reissued for decades in large print runs familiar across the country, not as rare specialist volumes.

## Books for Everyday Study

Particularly well known were:

- *Geometry Problems*, covering plane and solid geometry, with construction, calculation and proof problems;
- *Plane Trigonometry*, a textbook for senior pupils;
- *Trigonometry Problems*, whose exercises used trigonometry as a tool, including in geometrical problems.

Readers valued these books for their consistency and care. The exposition was steady and unhurried, and the exercises built skills step by step. Rybkin’s textbooks long remained a reliable support in schools, while teachers continued using his problem books well after curricula changed.

Nikolai Alexandrovich died in 1919. Today he is remembered above all for classic school books that shaped the mathematical culture of several generations.
