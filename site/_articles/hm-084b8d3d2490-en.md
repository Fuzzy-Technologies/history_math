*Marking the 170th anniversary of his birth*

A Russian mathematics educator and General of Artillery (1917).

## Education and Military Service

He was born on 3 August 1854, or 15 August in the Gregorian calendar, in Kherson Governorate.

He received his initial education at the Petrovsko-Poltava Military Gymnasium.

In 1872 Popruzhenko entered military service at the Konstantinovsky Military School but was soon transferred to the Mikhailovsky Artillery School. After graduating in 1875, he was commissioned as a second lieutenant and assigned to an artillery brigade.

He took part in the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878, including the Battle of Nikopol and the siege of Plevna. After the war he continued his education at the Mikhailovsky Artillery Academy, graduating in 1881.

## Teaching and Educational Leadership

From December 1881 he spent nine years at the Voronezh Gymnasium, first as a residential educator and then as a full-time teacher of mathematics and cosmography, or astronomy.

From Voronezh he was appointed inspector of classes at the Orenburg Neplyuev Cadet Corps. He later became director of the Vladimir-Kiev Cadet Corps, serving from 1898 to 1905.

In 1905 he received a new appointment at the Main Directorate of Military Educational Institutions. He worked on the Pedagogical Committee and the Educational Committee of the Pedagogical Museum and chaired an examination board and committees reviewing publications in mathematics, physics, natural science and geography.

## Textbooks and Congresses

He contributed to the journals *Bulletin of Experimental Physics and Elementary Mathematics* and *Pedagogical Collection* (their Russian titles translated here). He wrote school textbooks on geometry, elementary mathematical analysis and cosmography.

His textbooks went through many editions before the Revolution: *Elements of Cosmography*, *Elements of Analysis*, the first Russian secondary-school textbook on that subject, and a *Geometry Problem Book*. The last was also issued twice in the Soviet period, in 1936 and 1939. Many problems from it were incorporated into Kiselev’s *Elementary Geometry*, which became the principal Soviet geometry textbook for many years. The Russian book titles are translated here.

Popruzhenko helped organize the First All-Russian Congress of Mathematics Teachers, held in St Petersburg from 27 December 1911 to 3 January 1912, and served as deputy chair of its organizing committee. He was elected chairman of the Second Congress, held in Moscow from 27 December 1913 to 3 January 1914.
