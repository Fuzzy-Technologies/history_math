Pythagoras of Samos (c. 570–490 BC) was an ancient Greek mathematician and the founder of the philosophical school of the Pythagoreans.

He was known both for his famous theorem and for unusual dietary rules. Pythagoras forbade his pupils to eat beans, claiming that they were “connected with the souls of the dead”.

Once, fleeing from pursuers, he found himself before a bean field. Unwilling to break his prohibition, he preferred capture to stepping across the beans. According to the legend, his last words were:

“Better to die than to eat a bean!”

P.S. For Pythagoras, mathematics was sacred. He believed that numbers were the foundation of the cosmos and that a philosopher’s mind should be as pure as a geometric proof. This purity extended to everyday life: the Pythagoreans observed many prohibitions — not eating beans, not breaking bread, not picking up what had fallen, not leaving marks in ashes, and even not touching a white cockerel.

These strict rules may seem strange, but for the Pythagoreans they were a form of inner discipline. As Pythagoras himself said, “all is number”… so even lunch should have harmony.
