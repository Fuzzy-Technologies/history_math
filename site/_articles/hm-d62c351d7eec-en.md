“How Much Land Does a Man Need?” is one of Leo Tolstoy’s moral tales, written in 1886.

In it he vividly reworks a custom described by Herodotus. Among the Scythians, land was given or sold according to how much a person could encircle on foot or horseback in a single day, from sunrise to sunset.

The story’s main character, the peasant Pakhom, is consumed by greed. It leads to his death.

“And what will the price be?” Pakhom asks.<br>
“We have just one price: 1,000 roubles a day.”<br>
Pakhom does not understand.<br>
“What kind of measure is a day? How many desyatinas is that?”<br>
“We cannot work that out,” comes the reply. “We sell by the day: as much as you can walk round in a day is yours, and the price is 1,000 roubles. …”<br>
“There is just one condition: if you do not get back to the place you started from, you lose your money.”

Yakov Perelman uses this story in *Practical Lessons in Geometry* and *Entertaining Geometry* to discuss the relationship between a figure’s shape, perimeter and area. By his calculations, Pakhom walked 40 versts in a day and marked out about 8,000 desyatinas of land, or 78 square versts. Perelman also showed that an optimal route along the sides of a square would have enclosed an even larger area.

The calculations begin with a quadrilateral, using the fact that a square has a greater area than any other rectangle with the same perimeter. … Had Pakhom followed a circle, the same 40 versts would have enclosed 127 square versts. No other figure, whether bounded by straight or curved lines, can have a greater area for a given perimeter.

Try solving the problems yourself.

## The problems

1. Perelman asks: which route would have been best for Pakhom—a square, a regular hexagon, or a circle?

2. What is the ratio of their areas?

## Solution

Answer: $3:3.46:3.82 \approx 79:90:100$.

The problem comes down to finding which of these figures has the greatest area when their perimeters are equal.

Taking the perimeter as one unit, the areas of the square, regular hexagon and circle are in the approximate ratio $3:3.46:3.82 \approx 79:90:100$.

Clearly the circular route is best: for the same perimeter, its area is about 27.3% greater than that of the square and about 10.3% greater than that of the regular hexagon.
