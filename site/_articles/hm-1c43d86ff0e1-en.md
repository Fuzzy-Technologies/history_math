*Marking the 120th anniversary of his birth*

An outstanding French mathematician and winner of the Wolf Prize. He was a member of the French Academy of Sciences, a foreign member of the Royal Society of London, the Russian Academy of Sciences, the US National Academy of Sciences, the Royal Danish and Royal Swedish academies of sciences, the Polish Academy of Sciences and the Finnish Academy of Science and Letters, and an honorary member of the German Mathematical Society. He became a professor at the University of Paris in 1940.

He is considered to have played a key role in reviving the French mathematical school after the Second World War.

He was one of the founders and most active members of the [Bourbaki group](https://history-math.org/publications/hm-tt-9c6b66f64c63-en/).

## Life and Teaching

He was born on 8 July 1904 in Nancy. He was the son of Élie Cartan, the well-known French mathematician who laid the foundations of the algebraic theory of Lie groups.

He graduated from the celebrated École Normale Supérieure in 1926. He taught at the Lycée Malherbe in Caen in 1928–1929 and at the universities of Lille (1929–1931), Strasbourg (1931–1940), Paris (1940–1969) and Orsay (1970–1975).

## Research and a Book

His principal work concerned homological algebra, algebraic topology, complex analysis and general topology.

The book he wrote with Samuel Eilenberg became famous:

H. Cartan and S. Eilenberg, *Homological Algebra*. Russian translation. Moscow: Foreign Languages Publishing House, 1960. 510 pp.
