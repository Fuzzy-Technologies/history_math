When someone in an argument tells you, “That is my point of view”, it is tempting to ask: what is its radius?

David Hilbert (1862–1943) was one of the foremost mathematicians of the twentieth century, a professor in Göttingen and the author of the famous [Hilbert problems](https://t.me/history_math/62). He also had a joke about people reducing their horizons to a minimum.

In a lecture, he put it this way:

“Sometimes a person’s circle of horizon grows smaller and smaller, and as the radius tends to zero, it contracts to a single point. And then that becomes their point of view.”

The moral, in Hilbert’s spirit, is simple: before proudly announcing a “point of view”, it is worth checking that you still have some breadth of vision.

## Notes

The post also supplies this abridged English wording: “Sometimes it happens that a man's circle of horizon becomes smaller and smaller… And then that becomes his point of view.”

Source: Constance Reid, *Hilbert–Courant* (1984), p. 174.
