Isaac Newton (1643–1727) could explain the motion of the planets, yet sometimes seemed to struggle with the smallest everyday matters. University stories about him abound: some have support in early memoirs, while others live on as folklore.

## Close the window!

In 1689, Newton was elected to Parliament for Cambridge University. One famous story says that, amid a heated debate, he rose… and delivered his only parliamentary contribution: “I would ask that the window be closed. There is a terrible draught.”

## The cat and the kittens

Here is a classic legend: Newton asked a carpenter to cut a hole in a door for the cat—and a separate little hole for the kittens. They could, of course, have gone through their mother’s hole.

## Lunch without Newton

Memoirs about Newton at Trinity College describe him coming into hall, becoming so absorbed in thought that he never began eating, and having the food cleared away while he solved a problem in his head.

## A surplice in the wrong place

Another story of the same kind has him setting off in the wrong direction on ceremonial days: towards St Mary’s Church instead of the college chapel—or, even better, turning up for dinner in hall wearing his surplice, a long garment worn in church services. A little thing, perhaps, but his reputation as an absent-minded professor was secure.

## Newton and the counterfeiters

Then comes a twist few expect from the author of the law of universal gravitation. In the 1690s, Newton worked at the Royal Mint. There he proved himself a detective, gathering testimony, looking for evidence and bringing the notorious counterfeiter William Chaloner to trial. Newton challenged falsification in real life as well as in proofs.

Further information, primary sources and testimony:

- [Isaac Newton at MacTutor](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Newton/): a short biography and the date of his election to Parliament.

- [“Isaac Newton’s Life”](https://www.newton.ac.uk/about/isaac-newton/isaac-newtons-life/) at the Isaac Newton Institute, University of Cambridge, on his parliamentary service in 1689 and 1701–1702.

- G. E. Christianson, [*Isaac Newton*](https://pyrkov-professor.ru/Portals/0/Mediateka/XVII%20vek/christianson_g_e_isaac_newton.pdf) (PDF): a biographical account containing the often-repeated window story.

- William Stukeley, [“Revised Memoir of Newton”](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/OTHE00001) at the Newton Project, on Newton’s character and stories about him.

- J. M. F. Wright, [*Alma Mater; or, Seven Years at the University of Cambridge*](https://archive.org/details/almamaterorseven01wrig/page/n17/mode/2up) (1827): folklore and stories about Newton, including the two cat and kitten holes on pp. 17–18.

- [Pet door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_door#:~:text=In%20an%20apparent,hole%20as%20well) at Wikipedia: discussion of the cat-hole legend.

- J. Craig, [“Isaac Newton and the counterfeiters”](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsnr/article-pdf/18/2/136/350720/rsnr.1963.0017.pdf), *Notes and Records of the Royal Society*, 1963 (PDF): Newton’s work at the Mint and his pursuit of counterfeiters.

- [“March 16, 1699: William Chaloner, Counterfeiter, Hanged”](https://www.aps.org/publications/apsnews/201103/physicshistory.cfm) at APS Physics: an accessible account of Chaloner’s case and Newton’s role.
