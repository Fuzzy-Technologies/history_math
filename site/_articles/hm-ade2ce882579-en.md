Feeling death approaching, an Arab man summoned his three sons and said, “When I die, divide my herd of camels among yourselves. Let the eldest take $1/2$ of the whole herd, the middle son $1/4$, and the youngest $1/5$.” After he died, the sons tried to divide the herd as he had instructed, but could not: there were 19 camels.

Luckily, a mullah known for his wisdom happened to pass by. On hearing the difficulty, he suggested borrowing one camel from a neighbour for a short time. When it arrived, the mullah asked, “How many camels do you have now?” “Twenty,” the sons replied. He then told the eldest to take half the herd, or 10 camels; the middle son a quarter, or 5; and the youngest a fifth, or 4. “How many have you taken?” the mullah asked. The brothers counted and answered, “Nineteen.” “Then return the remaining camel to your neighbour,” said the mullah.

Did everyone reason correctly about the division, or was someone mistaken?

## Solution

The father was mistaken. He overlooked the fact that the shares $1/2$, $1/4$ and $1/5$ do not add up to one, but only to $19/20$ of the whole herd. The mullah understood this, so he had one camel added to the herd: the missing portion.
