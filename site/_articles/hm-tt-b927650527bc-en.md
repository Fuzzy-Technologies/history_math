In 1773, the German poet and scholar Gotthold Ephraim Lessing discovered an ancient Greek manuscript containing an unusual problem in the library at Wolfenbüttel.

In a letter addressed to his critic Eratosthenes of Cyrene, the great Archimedes challenged the mathematicians of Alexandria to solve it. The task was to find the number of bulls and cows in four herds belonging to Helios, the Sun god, which is why it is usually called *The Cattle Problem of Archimedes*. The number had to satisfy a set of puzzling arithmetic conditions. So began the centuries-long journey of one of history’s hardest Diophantine problems, whose solution took more than 2000 years.

The problem was written in verse:

*“… If, stranger, you can tell precisely how many cattle the Sun has,
And name separately the number of well-fed bulls,
And separately the cows, how many of each colour there were,
No one will call you ignorant of numbers,
Yet you will not be numbered among the wise …”*

In the first part, Archimedes asks for the numbers of bulls and cows of different colours, white, dark, yellow and spotted, under these conditions:

- Helios had four herds, each distinguished by its colour;
- the number of white bulls equals (1/2 + 1/3) of the dark bulls plus all the yellow bulls;
- the number of dark bulls equals (1/4 + 1/5) of the spotted bulls plus all the yellow bulls;
- the number of spotted bulls equals (1/6 + 1/7) of the white bulls plus all the yellow bulls;
- the number of white cows equals (1/3 + 1/4) of the dark herd;
- the number of dark cows equals (1/4 + 1/5) of the spotted herd;
- the number of spotted cows equals (1/5 + 1/6) of the yellow herd;
- the number of yellow cows equals (1/6 + 1/7) of the white herd.

The second part adds two conditions:

- the total number of white and dark bulls is a square number;
- the total number of spotted and yellow bulls is a triangular number.

## Solution {#section-1}

However difficult it may seem, you can first try accepting Archimedes’ challenge and solving the problem yourself.

The first part reduces to a system of seven linear equations in eight unknowns. Denote the unknowns by the colours of the herds, retaining the source notation: bulls Б, Т, Р, П, and cows б, т, р, п, for white, dark, yellow and spotted respectively.

Set up the system of linear equations:

Б = (1/2 + 1/3)Т + Р  
Т = (1/4 + 1/5)П + Р  
П = (1/6 + 1/7)Б + Р  
б = (1/3 + 1/4)(Т + т)  
т = (1/4 + 1/5)(П + п)  
п = (1/5 + 1/6)(Р + р)  
р = (1/6 + 1/7)(Б + б)

The additional conditions in the second part lead to a quadratic Diophantine equation in two unknowns:

x² – 4729494y² = 1 (Pell’s equation)

But reaching that equation still takes work! We have only supplied a checkpoint. The problem reduces to this: *“Find a square number which, when multiplied by 51 285 802 909 803, is equal to a triangular number.”*

> Note

> Triangular numbers are 1, 3, 6, 10, …; in general, they are the sums of the arithmetic progression 1, 2, 3, … . They have the form n(n + 1)/2.

> Square numbers are 1, 4, 9, 16, …; in general, they are the sums of the arithmetic progression 1, 3, 5, … . They have the form n².

The eight unknowns are numbers with more than 200 000 digits. A solution was published in 1880. The total number of bulls is approximately 7.76 × 10²⁰⁶⁵⁴⁴. Writing all 206 545 digits would require 660 pages with 2500 characters on each.

The exact numerical value of the cattle problem’s solution was first printed in 1965 using computers.

The numbers in the solution are so large that they exceed the number of particles in the universe, underlining the problem’s difficulty. Archimedes posed it as an intellectual challenge to his contemporaries, knowing that it had no practical application but demonstrated the beauty of mathematics.

The problem could not physically have been solved at that time. Only in 1965 was Archimedes’ cattle problem finally solved in the United States by a team using a computer: S. Williams, R. A. German and C. R. Zarnke. It required 7 hours and 49 minutes of computation on an IBM 7040.

The lesson: even simple arithmetic can pose problems that are practically impossible to compute.

## Historical background {#section-2}

Here is the complete text of Archimedes’ problem in verse, translated from the Russian rendering:

*Find for me, stranger, how many cattle the Sun has.
(Think and count them, if wisdom is not foreign to you.)
On the fertile plains of Sicily, the island of Thrinacia,
A great multitude once grazed in four herds.
The herds differed in colour: one shone milk-white,
Another had the colour of a dark sea wave,
The third was yellow, the last was spotted.
In every herd the males were a mighty multitude,
Yet they maintained these proportions: imagine, stranger,
The number of white bulls was precisely equal
To a half and a third of the dark bulls, plus all the yellow;
The number of dark bulls equalled a quarter
Of the spotted, with a fifth added, and all the yellow too;
Consider the number of spotted bulls in this way:
Make it equal to a sixth and a seventh
Of the silver-haired bulls, together with all the yellow.
In the same herds there were these numbers of cows:
The white-haired cows numbered precisely
A quarter and a third of the entire dark herd,
If you add those two parts together;
The dark cows again equalled a quarter
Of the spotted herd, with a fifth added,
Counting the bulls as part of that whole herd too.
The cows with spotted coats formed a multitude equal
To a fifth and a sixth of the yellow herd.
The yellow cows numbered “half a third”
Of the entire white herd, with a seventh part added.
If, stranger, you can tell precisely how many cattle the Sun has,
And name separately the number of well-fed bulls,
And separately the cows, how many of each colour there were,
No one will call you ignorant of numbers,
Yet you will not be numbered among the wise.
Consider, then,
What further properties the numbers of the Sun’s cattle have.
If you mix the silver-haired bulls with the dark,
So that they stand close together, equal in width and length,
Then on the vast Sicilian fields
They will occupy a great area as a solid square.
If you mix the yellow and spotted in a single herd,
They will stand in steps, beginning the count at one,
So that they form a triangular figure for us;
There is no need to add bulls of another colour.
If you find this, stranger, by applying your mind,
And can state precisely the size of every herd,
Then depart proud of your victory, and it shall be said
That in this wisdom you have surpassed all others completely.*
