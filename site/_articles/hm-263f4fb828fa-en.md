*Marking the 140th anniversary of his birth*

A Russian and Soviet mathematics educator, historian of mathematics education and author of mathematics textbooks and problem books for primary and secondary schools and higher education. He wrote on methodology, teaching methods and the history of mathematics and became a professor in 1933.

Professor Alexander Vasilyevich Lankov played a leading role in school and university mathematics education in the first half of the twentieth century.

## Education and Early Teaching

He was born on 30 April 1884 in Vorontsovo, Yakovlevskaya Volost, Korcheva District, Tver Governorate.

He completed primary school and a two-class village school. In 1903 he graduated from the Novotorzhsk Teacher Training Seminary and began teaching at a primary school in Maryino, then at the two-class school in Kimry, Tver Governorate.

He attended the Moscow Teacher Training Institute in 1907–1910, specializing in mathematics teaching methods and graduating with a gold medal. Three periods can be distinguished in his subsequent teaching career:

- educational institutions in Tver Governorate, now Tver Region, 1910–1930;
- higher education institutions in the Uzbek SSR, 1930–1934;
- the Perm, or Molotov, Pedagogical Institute, 1934–1953.

## Textbooks and Practical Teaching

From 1917 Lankov became actively involved in writing textbooks and teaching books. His Russian works included:

- *Elements of Algebra: A Collection of Algebraic Exercises and Problems for Higher Primary Schools* (1918);
- *Mental Arithmetic: Essays on the Theory and Practice of Mental Calculation* (1922);
- *Mathematics in the Labour School* (1923).

The last of these was reissued seven times. Lankov proposed using problems with practical content and emphasized the value of historical material, games and excursions in school mathematics teaching.

- *Mathematics in the Service of Labour* (1925–1926);
- *An Algebra Problem Book Based on Technology and Economics*, Parts I and II (1925).

## Fergana and Perm

At the end of 1930 a new period began in Lankov’s teaching career. In September he was elected head of the Mathematics Department at the Fergana Pedagogical Institute in the Uzbek SSR, leading it for four years. He also taught in Bukhara, Andijan and Margilan.

From 1934 the remainder of his life was connected with the Perm, or Molotov, Pedagogical Institute. He headed the Mathematics Department in its Faculty of Physics and Mathematics and also served as deputy director for research and teaching in 1939–1947. From 1938 he led the Department of Higher Algebra and Geometry for fifteen years.

Lankov lectured on analytic geometry, number theory and mathematics teaching methods. He developed and taught for many years a specialist course on the history of mathematics, introducing students to the history of mathematics teaching methods and mathematics education in Russia.

Under his direction, the Department of Algebra and Geometry began offering postgraduate study in mathematics teaching methods.

## Teaching Methods and Educational History

In 1937 the Institute began regularly publishing its *Scholarly Notes*, with Lankov on the editorial board. His articles in the series included “Nikolai Lobachevsky in Elementary Geometry” (1938) and “On the Theoretical Foundations of a Systematic Arithmetic Course” (1949). His scholarly legacy also includes work on teaching geometry, algebra and trigonometry.

In “Scientific Research on Mathematics Teaching Methods”, Lankov surveyed the major work undertaken in the country in 1934–1949. In Sverdlovsk in 1952 he presented “On Teaching Mathematics Teaching Methods at a Pedagogical Institute”, tracing curricula from 1933 through the draft of 1952 and outlining the principal requirements for presenting the course material.

He wrote several historical works that also illuminate questions of teaching. In “Towards a History of the Reform of Mathematics Teaching”, Lankov argued that Felix Klein, widely recognized as a leader of the international reform movement, often played down Russia’s role and place in it. Russian journals, he maintained, had raised and developed questions of teaching methods considerably earlier than those in Germany. A larger and more remarkable work was his 1951 book *Towards a History of Progressive Ideas in Russian Mathematics Teaching*.
