*Marking the centenary of his birth*

A Soviet and Russian mathematician. He received his Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1955 and became a professor at Moscow State University in 1959. He was a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences from 1968 and the Russian Academy of Education from 1993, and a member of the Mexican Academy of Sciences.

He received the 1962 Lenin Prize, jointly with Pontryagin, Gamkrelidze and Mishchenko, for work applying ordinary differential equations to optimal control and oscillation theory.

## Education and Research

He was born on 26 April 1925 in Moscow.

During the Great Patriotic War he was mobilized into the Red Army while in his third year at Moscow University and served on the Second Belorussian Front. He was awarded the Order of the Patriotic War, Second Class.

He graduated from Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1948 and subsequently completed postgraduate studies. He was a student and later a colleague of Lev Semyonovich Pontryagin. Under Pontryagin’s supervision, he defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “Vector Fields on a Manifold”, in 1951.

He taught in Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics from 1951.

He joined the Steklov Mathematical Institute of the Academy of Sciences in 1951. From 1956 he worked at the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences.

After 1994 he worked as a researcher at the Centre for Research in Mathematics, CIMAT, in Guanajuato, Mexico.

## Geometry, Control and Teaching

Boltyansky’s principal work concerned combinatorial geometry, particularly questions connected with Hilbert’s third problem, and topology. His work in cybernetics concerned optimal control theory. He wrote more than 220 papers.

He was also widely known for his work on mathematics teaching and popular mathematics. He authored and co-authored geometry textbooks for grades 6–9 and 10–11.

From the channel’s author, Mansur Gilmullin:

In my university teaching, I enjoyed using the following book when setting students’ course papers and graduation projects on mathematics education:

Boltyansky, V. G., and Vilenkin, N. Ya. *Symmetry in Algebra*. Russian edition. Moscow: Nauka, 1967. 284 pp.

The book explains how to use symmetry to solve equations, irrational equations, inequalities and systems of equations, and to prove identities and inequalities. All these problems are solved by a single method based on the theory of symmetric polynomials.
