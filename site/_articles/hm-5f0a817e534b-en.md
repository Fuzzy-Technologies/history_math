A Central Asian scholar, mathematician, astronomer, geographer and historian.

Born around 780, al-Khwarizmi took his name from his homeland, the Central Asian state of Khwarazm. Under Caliph al-Ma’mun, he was attached to Baghdad’s House of Wisdom (Bayt al-Hikma), the caliphal library.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

He was well acquainted with Indian mathematics and the scholarship of the Hellenistic world. He wrote several books on mathematics and astronomy. In a treatise on Indian arithmetic, he described the Indian place-value numeral system. The Arabic original is lost; the work is known through a substantially reworked twelfth-century Latin version, one of the sources through which Europe became acquainted with decimal positional notation. Latin versions of his name, including Algorizmi and Algorithmus, gave rise to the word “algorithm”.

In another book, *Hisab al-jabr wa-l-muqabala* (*Calculation by Completion and Balancing*), he discussed methods for solving linear and quadratic equations. Al-jabr, or “completion”, referred to removing a subtracted term by adding it to both sides of the equation. This algebra became known through a Latin translation, and the word al-jabr, rendered as algebra, came to denote the whole subject of algebra, which until the nineteenth century was the science of solving equations.
