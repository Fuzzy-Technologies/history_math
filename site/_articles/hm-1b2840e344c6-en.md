*Marking the 125th anniversary of his birth*

Ivan Georgiyevich Petrovsky (1901–1973) was a Soviet mathematician and a leading figure in education. He was Moscow State University’s legendary rector from 1951 to 1973, received his Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1935 and became a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1946. He was named a Hero of Socialist Labour in 1969, received Stalin Prizes in 1946 and 1952 and was awarded the Order of Lenin five times, in 1953, 1961, 1967, 1969 and 1971.

He was born in Sevsk, Oryol Province, now in Bryansk Region, on 5 January 1901 Old Style, or 18 January in the Gregorian calendar.

## Education and Research

He completed the town’s real school in 1917. An interesting detail: his marks were excellent in almost every subject, but he received fours — good rather than excellent — in mathematics and drawing, although he loved to draw.

Petrovsky initially entered the natural sciences division of Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. After the revolution and the outbreak of civil war, however, he left and rejoined his family, who had moved to Yelisavetgrad, now Kropyvnytskyi, Ukraine. There he worked as a clerk and studied at the mechanical engineering technical college.

In 1922 he returned to Moscow and joined the mathematics division of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. As a fifth-year student in 1927, he delivered a greeting at the First All-Russian Congress of Mathematicians on behalf of the faculty’s young people.

After graduating in 1927, he undertook postgraduate study, completing it in 1930. His supervisor, Professor D. F. Yegorov, had a profound influence on him. He began working at Moscow State University in 1929, first as an assistant and then as a docent, or associate professor.

He became a professor at Moscow State University in 1933. In 1935 he was awarded a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree without defending a dissertation. From 1930 to 1941 he also headed a department in the evening faculty of the Moscow Mechanical Engineering Institute, as the Bauman institution was then known.

## The War Years and the Academy of Sciences

He was elected dean of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1940. During the war against Nazi Germany he directed the evacuation of part of Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics to Ashkhabad and then Sverdlovsk, and its return to Moscow in May 1943. At his own request he stepped down as dean in 1944.

During the war he carried out profound research on lacunas and wave diffusion.

He was elected a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1943 and a full member in 1946. From 1949 to 1951 he served as academic secretary of its Division of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, and from 1953 as a member of the Academy’s Presidium.

## Leading the University

In 1951 he became head of the differential equations department in the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, remaining until 1973. That same year he was appointed rector of Moscow State University. He took office while the new university complex on the Lenin Hills was still under construction (1949–1953).

Petrovsky was a gifted teacher. A. N. Kolmogorov wrote that “his research seminars were always centres of lively scientific thought”. Participants later became leaders of mathematical schools and fields. Generations of students have learned from his textbooks.

His principal research fields were partial differential equations, the qualitative theory of ordinary differential equations, algebraic geometry, probability and mathematical physics. He laid the foundations of a general theory of systems of partial differential equations, identifying elliptic, hyperbolic and parabolic classes.

Taking an interest in education before university, he helped initiate continuing education courses for teachers, a correspondence mathematics school and a boarding school attached to Moscow State University.

Bryansk State University bears his name: Academician I. G. Petrovsky.

He was buried at Novodevichy Cemetery in Moscow in 1973.

## Notes

Petrovsky’s years as rector profoundly shaped the life of a university community numbering many thousands. More than seventy departments and two hundred laboratories were established in emerging fields. His scholarly standing attracted leading researchers, including more than a hundred members of the USSR Academy of Sciences. Departments became stronger research centres, faculties and institutes received modern experimental equipment, and contacts with research and educational institutions abroad expanded.

In 1955 Moscow State University’s Computing Centre was established on the basis of the computing research division attached to its computational mathematics department. In December 1956 the university’s first electronic computer, a Strela — one of the first in the USSR — was installed and put into operation. It performed complex calculations for the Soviet space programme in 1958–1961. By the early 1970s the university had a powerful collection of computers.
