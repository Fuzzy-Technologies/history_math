Some definitions hinge on just a couple of words. Put them the wrong way round, and mathematical analysis turns into a magic spell.

At an exam, a student confidently gives the definition of the limit of a sequence:

“A number $a$ is called the limit of a sequence if, for any epsilon given backwards…”

The examiner gives a start:

“Wait! Where did you get that peculiar definition?”

The student is absolutely certain:

“But that was in your lectures! You said it yourself!”

The professor laughs:

“Oh, my dear, I put it slightly differently: ‘…for any epsilon given in advance…’”

What can one say? In analysis, the order of words can sometimes matter more than the order of thoughts!

## Notes

Source of the joke. In Russian, *zadom naperyod* (“backwards”) sounds similar to *zadannogo naperyod* (“given in advance”).

- S. N. Fedin, [*Mathematicians Joke Too*](https://www.docdroid.net/ytC3FTI/fedin-sn-matematiki-toze-sutiat-pdf.pdf) (in Russian), anecdote no. 21, “Backwards” (PDF).
