Let us try an exercise in comparative logic, mathematical statistics and snake-oil salesmanship: “how to prove anything with statistics”, inspired by a humorous piece in the spirit of *The Journal of Irreproducible Results*.

Cucumbers will be the death of you! Every cucumber you eat brings you closer to death. It is astonishing that thinking people have still not recognised the harmfulness of this vegetable, and even use the Russian expression “as fresh as a cucumber” to describe someone full of life!

The evidence is watertight:

1. Practically everyone with a chronic illness has eaten cucumbers. The effect is clearly cumulative.

2. 99.9% of all people who died of cancer had eaten cucumbers during their lifetime.

3. 100% of all soldiers have eaten cucumbers.

4. 99.7% of all victims of car and aircraft accidents ate cucumbers within two weeks of their accident.

5. 93.1% of all juvenile offenders come from families where cucumbers were eaten regularly.

There are long-term data too: among people born in 1839 who subsequently ate cucumbers, mortality is 100%.

And the guinea-pig experiment clinches it: those force-fed 20 pounds of cucumbers a day for a month lost their appetite completely.

The only way to avoid the harmful effects of cucumbers is to change your diet. Try marsh-orchid soup, for example. As far as we know, nobody has ever died from it.

To bring the joke right up to date: this is much the way certain confident “experts” sell “services” in the guise of science. You have probably heard absurd claims like these:

“99% of serial killers drink coffee, so draw your conclusions and drink our healthy juice!”<br>
“95% of people who have ever made a mistake used words. Conclusion: language is the main source of error.”<br>
“97% of people who suffered burnout had deadlines in their diaries. So deadlines are inherently toxic.”<br>
“83.3% of women unhappy in marriage had a man beside them. The expert’s conclusion is obvious: get a divorce and go on a retreat immediately!”

Correlation plus the right tone of voice, and any “proof” is ready to go!
