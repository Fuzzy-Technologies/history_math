Mathematics, like every other science, was created over many millennia by different peoples and their outstanding representatives. No decree can prohibit or repeal mathematical laws.

The best commentary is a passage from the great German mathematician David Hilbert’s address to the International Congress of Mathematicians in Bologna in 1928: *“...all boundaries, especially national ones, contradict the spirit of mathematics. Only a complete failure to understand our science can lead people to distinguish between individuals and races, and the reasons for doing so are utterly trivial. Mathematics knows no races... For mathematics, the entire civilized world is a single country.”*

To illustrate this, let us name a few outstanding mathematicians of different nationalities who made substantial contributions to mathematics as a whole.

## Greece {#section-1}

Pythagoras (c. 570–473 BCE), Euclid (c. 340–287 BCE), Archimedes (287–212 BCE), Apollonius (262–190 BCE).

Different styles of mathematical thought undoubtedly exist, and the role of each mathematical school’s traditions is also evident. A small number of major research centres play a large part in mathematics’ development. In the first third of the XX century, for example, Göttingen University in Germany was such a centre, a “Mathematical Mecca” for all mathematicians.

International congresses and the organization of mathematical reviewing worldwide help preserve the organizational unity of an increasingly branching science.
