(On the 115th anniversary of his birth)

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-1" %}

Many generations of teachers who graduated from the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Yelabuga State Pedagogical Institute gratefully remember their mentor, master and teacher Alfred Maksovich Lustig. His was a truly unique life.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Alfred Maksovich Lustig was born on 10 October 1908 in Vienna, Austria's capital. His father was a master cabinetmaker and his mother a homemaker. His schooling comprised five years of elementary school and eight years of gymnasium. In 1927 he entered the University of Vienna's Faculty of Philosophy to specialize in physics, graduating in 1932 with a gold medal.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

For four years, the young Alfred Lustig studied physics and mathematics. On the basis of his dissertation on Brownian motion and his doctoral examination, he received a Doctor of Philosophy degree in February 1932.

For the following six years he worked as a research assistant at the University of Vienna's Institute of Physics. His duties were to supervise students working in the institute and assist its director, Professor F. Ehrenhaft, in research and physical and technical expert assessments. He had personal contact with the famous physicists Einstein, Bohr and Pauli. He also published eight research papers in German-language physics journals. In 1908–1939 he lived continuously in Vienna.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

In June 1938, following the German occupation of Austria, Lustig, like all Jews, lost his position in a state institution and became unemployed. In October 1939 the German authorities forced thousands of Jews, including him, to leave for the occupied part of former Poland, between the San and Bug rivers. With the Soviet border guards' permission, he crossed the Soviet border on 30 October 1939.

From that day the Soviet Union became his second homeland. He then lived for two months in Stanislav, now Ivano-Frankivsk, in western Ukraine. At the beginning of 1940 he was sent to Kazan. Tatspirttrust, the organization for which he worked, sent him to Yelabuga, where he worked from 24 January 1940 until December as a shift chemist in the distillery laboratory.

His knowledge qualified him to work at the recently opened Yelabuga State Teacher Training Institute, established in 1939.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

Following his personal application, Lustig was employed there from 20 December 1940, initially as a senior laboratory assistant in the physics teaching laboratory. He was then assigned to teach German to students. In his application he wrote: *“I have a command of the entire field of mathematics and physics, although I do not yet feel capable of teaching these subjects in Russian. My native language, however, is German, and I believe I am able to teach German.”* He approached his work conscientiously, completed the German syllabus and achieved positive results.

During the war he took up arms to defend his new homeland. He was called into the Soviet Army in January 1942 and wounded in November 1942. From then until April 1943, he again taught German at the teacher training institute. In April 1943 he was called to the front once more, serving as a private rifleman on the Second Baltic Front. Severely wounded a second time, he returned to Yelabuga again.

Lustig received military decorations, including the Order of Glory, third class. In 1947 the Supreme Soviet of the USSR awarded him the Medal for Valiant Labour in the Great Patriotic War. He never wore his decorations, however.

From May 1945, from Victory Day, Lustig returned to his beloved scientific and teaching work. He was appointed senior lecturer in the teacher training institute's Department of Physics and Mathematics. He taught elementary and higher mathematics, courses that academician V. I. Smirnov had taught during the war. From then on, his entire life was devoted to teaching mathematical subjects to students.

He spent a long time restoring the documents confirming his Austrian education and degree. In 1958 he received confirmation of his University of Vienna diploma through the Austrian consulate. By a decision of the Higher Attestation Commission dated 10 October 1964, he was confirmed in the academic title of associate professor in the Department of Mathematics.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

His high standing as a scholar had been recognized earlier. On 18 September 1961 he was appointed head of the Department of Mathematics. He held the post until 16 June 1967, when he was released at his own request. His contribution to developing the mathematics departments was enormous. He devoted considerable attention to improving the lecturers' theoretical preparation and teaching skills. Many faculty lecturers had studied under him, then worked under his leadership and enjoyed his fullest support.

The research papers he wrote in Yelabuga concerned various problems in mathematical analysis. For example, the first volume of Scholarly Notes of Yelabuga State Pedagogical Institute, published in 1956, included his article “Some Questions in the Classification of Infinitely Differentiable Functions”. He served on the editorial board of subsequent volumes devoted to physics and mathematics.

Both the institution's leadership and his students recognized his teaching mastery and high scientific qualifications. Lustig was entered in the institute's Book of Honour in 1965. For his achievements in developing education, he received the Order of the Badge of Honour by decree of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR on 30 September 1966.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-7" %}

Mathematics teachers in our region gratefully recall his classes, clear and accessible lectures, and consultations. Everyone was struck by his erudition, cultivated manner, tact and courtesy. He was a profoundly decent person: scrupulously honest, fair and responsible. He could be demanding in class, but always supported students during examinations. His students' achievement was consistently high because he made mathematics accessible.

Lustig is known to have learned Russian quickly, becoming able not only to converse but to teach at a very high level. His Russian was impeccably correct. His knowledge of Russian grammar became legendary. He also learned Tatar: at least, he invited Tatar students to explain the material in Tatar when they struggled to express their thoughts in Russian. The Department of Foreign Languages made very successful use of the unique opportunity to converse with a native German speaker. He also had a good command of English and Hungarian.

At his own request, Lustig was released from his position on 12 July 1969 on retiring at the statutory age. Despite his well-earned retirement, he remained active in teaching and the institute's public life. Each year from 1971 to 1982, he chaired the State Examination Commission of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-8" %}

In Yelabuga, Lustig found not only a second homeland but a family. He married Zinaida Grigoryevna Zamoreva. His second marriage was to Lidiya Alekseyevna Belyayeva, with whom he had two children, Mikhail and Zinaida. He loved his children dearly and brought them up according to his principles. They too became university lecturers: his son a mathematician and his daughter a foreign-language teacher.

Lustig lived in Yelabuga for more than 40 years. He loved the town, its surroundings and the Kama. After being wounded and recovering, he was once offered the chance to return to Austria. He returned to Yelabuga instead, to remain there forever. He died on 21 March 1985 and was buried in Yelabuga. An Austrian anti-fascist, Soviet Army soldier, physicist and mathematician, outstanding educator and teacher of Tatarstan's teachers: such a person deserves to be remembered forever.
