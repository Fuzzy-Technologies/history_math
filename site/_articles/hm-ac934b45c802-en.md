“A means of healthy mental exercise.”<br>
—Sergey A. Rachinsky

The distinguished Russian teacher Sergey Alexandrovich Rachinsky (1833–1902) was a master of mental arithmetic and problem solving. His distinctive *1001 zadacha dlya umstvennogo scheta* (1,001 Problems for Mental Arithmetic), published in 1899, became a valuable aid for teachers using mental calculation in their lessons.

For your mathematical leisure, here is an old problem from that book. Try to solve it in your head.

“Jerusalem is 3,100 versts away. A pilgrim went there, walking as many sazhen as the number of arshin he travelled by rail and the number of feet he travelled by steamship. How many versts did he cover by each means?”

Please explain an arithmetic solution.

## Historical background

Here is how the units mentioned in the problem were related in Rachinsky’s day:

$1\text{ verst}=500\text{ sazhen}=1500\text{ arshin}=3500\text{ feet}$.

## Solution

Answer: 2,100, 700 and 300 versts.

Divide the 3,100 versts into three parts inversely proportional to 500, 1,500 and 3,500:

- The distance on foot in versts is $1/500$ of the numerical count of sazhen.
- The rail distance in versts is $1/1500$ of the numerical count of arshin.
- The steamship distance in versts is $1/3500$ of the numerical count of feet.

Let $N$ be their common numerical count. Then

$N\left(\frac1{500}+\frac1{1500}+\frac1{3500}\right)=3100.$

The sum in parentheses is $31/10500$, so

$N=3100\div\frac{31}{10500}=1050000.$

Dividing this common count by 500 gives 2,100 versts travelled on foot.

Similarly, dividing by 1,500 gives 700 versts by rail; dividing by 3,500 gives 300 versts by steamship.
