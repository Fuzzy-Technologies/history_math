*Marking the centenary of her birth*

Maria Ignatyevna Moro (21 April 1926–23 May 2018) was a Soviet and Russian mathematics educator and specialist in primary teaching. A student of N. A. Menchinskaya, she received her Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences degree in 1965. For generations, her name has above all meant mathematics textbooks for grades 1–4, one of the most familiar school textbook series for decades.

## From Arithmetic to Mathematics

An important milestone in her work was the primary school’s move from arithmetic to a broader mathematics course: calculation and rules joined by understanding, reasoning and the language of diagrams and models. In the early 1960s the new series underwent extensive trials in schools, including those in Moscow and Leningrad, before gradually entering general use. Later editions were revised and reissued many times while retaining their recognizable style and carefully graded progression.

## Research and Classroom Practice

She graduated from the Potemkin Moscow City Pedagogical Institute in 1950, then taught mathematics and served as a deputy head for academic work in Moscow schools from 1951 to 1957. That classroom experience shows in her writing: she seems always to remember how a child actually reads a problem, where mistakes arise and at what point the meaning can be lost.

From 1956 she was a researcher in the primary teaching methods section of the Institute of Teaching Methods of the RSFSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences. Her Candidate of Sciences dissertation concerned a system of independent work for younger pupils in arithmetic lessons, a theme that still feels current: the aim is to help a child act independently, check their work and understand what they are doing, rather than simply hand down a rule.

Her subsequent work brought research and practice ever closer, ranging from experimental teaching materials and exercise cards to guidance that supports a lesson from within. In the 1970s and 1980s she served as an academic editor and mentor to younger researchers, developing primary teaching methods through books for children and guidance for teachers and education specialists.

## Textbooks and Teaching Resources

The coauthored primary school *Mathematics* textbooks in the *School of Russia* teaching programme became widely known. In these books, pupils are asked to explain, compare, find patterns and read information from pictures or diagrams as well as calculate. Hence their characteristic structure: many small steps, mental arithmetic, work with quantities, simple models and a carefully developed sequence of increasingly demanding problems.

At different times her coauthors included M. A. Bantova, G. V. Beltyukova, S. I. Volkova and S. V. Stepanova. Collaboration matters here: a textbook is built as a system, each detail tested against classroom experience and sound teaching methods.

The official recognition of this work is also characteristic of Soviet education. In the 1970s the series’ textbooks and teaching materials received professional awards, including at VDNKh, the Exhibition of Achievements of the National Economy in Moscow. This was a rare instance of honours going to quietly and conscientiously developed classroom practice rather than a grand theory.

Another very practical part of her legacy consists of teachers’ guides and supplementary teaching materials: exercise cards, independent work and practice activities. Such books seldom attract the spotlight, yet they help make lessons engaging and systematic.

Even people who cannot recall the authors of their schoolbooks know Moro’s textbooks: parents studied from them, and later their children did too. That is a rare measure of lasting educational work, when a teaching approach becomes part of school culture.
