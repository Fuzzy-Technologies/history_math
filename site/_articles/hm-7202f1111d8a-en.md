*Marking the 190th anniversary of his birth*

Vasily Yakovlevich Zinger (1836–1907) was a Russian mathematician, botanist, philosopher and educator, a Doctor of Pure Mathematics and an Honoured Professor of Imperial Moscow University from 1888. He helped found the Moscow Mathematical Society in 1864 and served as its president from 1886 to 1891.

He was born in Moscow on 30 January 1836 Old Style, or 11 February in the Gregorian calendar. His father died early, and much of his childhood was spent in the home of his grandfather, the administrator of the Golitsyn Hospital. Zinger later recalled finding his early gymnasium lessons uninteresting and excessively formal. In the senior years, however, he awakened intellectually, astonishing teachers with his memory and independent thinking. He graduated from Moscow University in 1857 and remained to prepare for a professorship, supplementing his income with private lessons.

## Geometry and Teaching

In 1862 he defended his master’s dissertation, “The Method of Least Squares”, and became a docent in pure mathematics. In 1867 he defended his doctoral dissertation, “On the Motion of a Free Fluid Mass”. His interests lay where geometry, mechanics and hydrodynamics meet, and his university courses covered analytic geometry, higher algebra, theoretical mechanics, mathematical physics and the theory of light. His works included the university text *Higher Algebra* (1874).

He gave particular importance to projective geometry, also known as synthetic or new geometry, introducing it as a separate subject at Moscow University and effectively establishing a school of geometry there. His lectures were original and visual. N. E. Zhukovsky, later a leading figure in mechanics, especially valued his geometrically clear explanations. His teaching followed the same principle: understanding why a proof is needed and how its idea works matters more than reproducing it mechanically.

## Organizing Science and Education

Zinger was also a major organizer of science and education. He served twice as dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, in 1876–1878 and 1880–1885, and as vice-rector of Moscow University in 1878–1880. He continued teaching until 1899 and directed the Alexander Commercial School from 1892 to 1898. In 1900 he was elected a corresponding member of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences.

His work with the Moscow Mathematical Society spanned an entire era: he helped organize it and served as secretary, vice-president and then president. Its statutes expressed the ambitious aim of promoting the development of mathematical sciences in Russia. The society published *Matematicheskii Sbornik*, a journal he worked hard to support. He also translated Michel Chasles’ *Historical Survey of the Origin and Development of Geometrical Methods* into Russian, a book that became an important landmark in Russian mathematical culture.

## Botany and Philosophy

Botany began as a pastime in the countryside. After marrying and visiting his wife’s estate in Tula Province, he became interested in field observation and plant classification and maintained close contact with the botanist N. N. Kaufmann. He went on to publish substantial studies of the flora of central Russia, including a major *Collection of Information on the Flora of Central Russia* (1885). His botanical work earned him an honorary doctorate in botany. Botanical taxa and the genus *Zingeria* commemorate him; his botanical author abbreviation is W.J.Zinger.

Zinger also wrote on science and philosophy, defending the independence of mathematical thought and discussing the foundations of geometry. He is rightly associated with the Moscow philosophical and mathematical school. He also wrote school textbooks on physics and elementary mathematics. He died in Moscow on 16 February 1907 Old Style, or 1 March in the Gregorian calendar, and was buried at Vagankovo Cemetery.
