*Marking the 190th anniversary of his birth*

An English logician and philosopher, and a Fellow of the Royal Society from 1883.

Nearly every schoolchild and student knows Euler–Venn diagrams, which are used in set theory, probability, logic, statistics and computer science.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

## Life and Teaching

John Venn was born on 4 August 1834 in Kingston upon Hull, Yorkshire, England.

He began his education in London. After attending Highgate School, he entered Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, in 1853. He graduated with a Bachelor of Arts degree in 1857 and was soon elected a Fellow of the College, remaining one throughout his life. He initially served as a clergyman.

In 1862 he returned to Cambridge University as a lecturer in moral sciences, studying and teaching logic and probability.

In 1883 Venn was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and received a Doctor of Science degree from Cambridge. That same year he left the clergy.

## Books and Diagrams

Venn’s principal research field was logic. He published three works on the subject:

1. *The Logic of Chance* (1866), which introduces the frequency interpretation, or frequency theory, of probability.

2. *Symbolic Logic* (1881), which introduced Venn diagrams.

3. *The Principles of Empirical or Inductive Logic* (1889), which gives justifications for inverse operations in Boolean logic.

Geometric representations of syllogistic logic did not originate with Venn; Gottfried Leibniz had often used them. Venn diagrams are used to derive logical consequences from premises expressible in classical propositional calculus and the calculus of one-place predicates.

## University History

In 1888 Venn turned to history. He donated his large collection of logic books to Cambridge University Library. With his son, he took on the task of compiling a history of Cambridge graduates. The first edition appeared in 1922.

You may also be interested in the logical puzzle associated with Venn’s name that he presented in *Symbolic Logic*.

## Notes

The puzzle concerns a club whose membership rules we are asked to simplify.

The club’s rules state:

- The finance committee is elected from the members of the general committee.
- No one may belong to both the general committee and the library committee unless they also belong to the finance committee.
- No member of the library committee may belong to the finance committee.
