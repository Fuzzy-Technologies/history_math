> Whatever they do, nothing goes right;  
> Their mother must have given birth to them on a Monday.  
> (The song “Island of Bad Luck” from the film *The Diamond Arm*.)

Almost everyone would like to know which day of the week they were born on. You can find it from your date of birth. More generally, algorithms exist for determining the day of the week from any date. This mathematical problem can be solved using modular arithmetic: arithmetic with remainders. Of course, perpetual calendars are also available, including ones you can carry in your pocket, even with a voice interface, as well as online calculators. Here we will explain the mathematics behind these calendars.

Here is one formula for doing the calculation. You can work it out by hand or write a program. It applies to the Gregorian calendar (New Style, introduced in 1582).

**The calculation uses the following formula:**

**ДН = Д + Г + \[Г/4\] + \[С/4\] – 2С + К (mod 7)**

Here:

• ДН is the day of the week to be determined (0 = Sunday, 1 = Monday, ..., 6 = Saturday);

• Д is the day of the month (a number from 1 to 31);

• Г is the year within the century (the last two digits of the four-digit year);

• С is the century (the first two digits of the year);

• К is the month coefficient (January = 6, or 5 in a leap year; February = 2, or 1 in a leap year; March = 2; April = 5; May = 0; June = 3; July = 5; August = 1; September = 4; October = 6; November = 2; December = 4);

• \[ \] denotes the integer part of a number (the greatest integer not exceeding that number);

• (mod 7) means taking the remainder when the right-hand side of the expression is divided by 7.

The calculation rests on these principles:

• every seventh day of the month has the same name as the previous one;

• leap years must be taken into account.

## Example {#section-1}

13 November 2023 was a Monday.

Here: Д = 13, Г = 23, С = 20, К = 2.

Then: \[Г/4\] = 5, \[С/4\] = 5.

ДН = 13 + 23 + 5 + 5 – 40 + 2 = 8 = 1 (mod 7). It really was a Monday.

P. S. Mental calendar calculation is one of the events at the Mental Calculation World Cup, held every two years since 2004. Competitors must identify as many weekdays as possible for Gregorian dates between 1600 and 2100 within one minute. The world record of 140 calculations per minute was set in 2018.
