*Marking the 115th anniversary of his birth*

Alexei Nikolayevich Bogolyubov (1911–2004) was a Soviet and Ukrainian mathematician, specialist in mechanics and historian of mathematics and mechanics. He became a corresponding member of the Ukrainian SSR Academy of Sciences in 1969, was a professor and received Ukraine’s title of Honoured Worker in Science and Technology in 2001. He authored and edited encyclopedias and reference works on the history of science and wrote scholarly biographies of engineers and scientists.

## Education and Adversity

He was born in Nezhin on 12 March 1911 Old Style, or 25 March in the Gregorian calendar, into the family of a professor of theology. His family background long obstructed his education and academic career, but also strengthened his character and habit of working across disciplines. There were three sons: the eldest, Nikolai, became a leading academician, while the youngest, Mikhail, became a philologist.

During the 1930s Alexei lived and worked in Kharkov, teaching and holding engineering posts while studying mechanics at Kharkov University in 1931–1936 and machine dynamics at the mechanical engineering institute in 1936–1938. His biography also includes an unusual international episode: in 1937 he worked at a home for evacuated children of Spanish Republicans, teaching mathematics and physics.

The war radically changed his life. After escaping occupied territory in 1944, he was arrested and sent to labour camps near Norilsk. Released under the 1953 amnesty, he returned to engineering and soon resumed research and teaching in Kyiv.

## The History of Mechanics and Scientific Biography

From the mid-1950s he combined work organizing higher education with research on the history of science. He worked in the Ukrainian SSR’s Ministry of Higher and Secondary Specialized Education in 1955–1962, then at the Academy’s institutes of mathematics and history. He defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation on the history of machine mechanics under I. I. Artobolevsky in 1963 and a doctoral dissertation in technical sciences in 1965.

His interests brought working mechanics together with its cultural memory: how ideas, schools, textbooks and research centres arise, and how mathematics takes material form in mechanical engineering. He is regarded as a founder of the Ukrainian school of historians of mathematics and mechanics.

Major reference works appeared under his editorship and with his participation, from the multi-volume *History of Mathematics in Russia* (1966–1970) to *Mathematicians and Mechanicians: A Biographical Reference Book* (Kyiv, 1983), a standard reference for teachers and researchers. Another part of his legacy comprises biographies of engineers and scientists, including Agustín de Betancourt, Gaspard Monge and Robert Hooke. In 1990 Betancourt and Lanz’s *Treatise on the Construction of Machines*, one of the world’s earliest books on the theory of machines and mechanisms, was reissued in Madrid with Bogolyubov’s commentary. His extensive writing on cultural relations between Spain and Russia earned him Spain’s Order of Civil Merit in 1996. In 1997 he received the Ukrainian National Academy of Sciences’ Krylov Prize.

His writing is recognizable for rigorous source work, close attention to detail and respect for the mathematician’s and engineer’s craft. His reference books and biographies still help readers assemble the history of science from facts, names and ideas without reducing it to a dry chronicle. For many teachers and students, such books bridge university courses and the real history of their discipline.

## Selected Books

Selected books:

- *The History of Machine Mechanics* (1964), in Russian;
- *The Development of Problems in Machine Mechanics* (1967), in Russian;
- *Essays on the History of Mechanics* (1974), in Ukrainian;
- *Doctor Robert Hooke* (1980), in Russian;
- *Mathematicians and Mechanicians: A Biographical Reference Book* (1983), in Russian.

He died in Kyiv on 2 November 2004.

Readers interested in mathematical history may find Bogolyubov’s *Mathematicians and Mechanicians* (1983) useful. It gives biographical and scholarly information on more than fifteen hundred mathematicians and specialists in mechanics, past and present, together with a chronology of major events in mathematics and mechanics and important collected works.
