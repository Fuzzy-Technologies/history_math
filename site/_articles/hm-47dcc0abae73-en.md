They say that practical classes in differential geometry at Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics were once taught by a very young Doctor of Sciences, A. T. Fomenko, a future academician and one of the faculty’s best-known geometers.

One of his groups included a student we shall call M. She hardly ever came to classes, happily skipped the whole semester and was, to put it mildly, unprepared for the exam.

And Fomenko was the examiner she got.

Apparently deciding to end the conversation firmly and quickly, he asked her to prove a nontrivial result in differential geometry. M. had nothing left to lose. She barely understood what he was asking and blurted out in desperation:

“But Anatoly Timofeyevich, that is obvious!”

For most examiners, that would have ended the conversation. Here, however, something unexpected happened.

Fomenko was astonished: if it seemed obvious to the student, perhaps he himself had overlooked a short solution? The possibility that she was bluffing apparently never crossed his mind.

Greatly excited, he went to the far end of the room and began thinking. He thought for a long time. About ten minutes later, he returned, thoroughly dishevelled but happy.

He announced joyfully:

“You know, it really is obvious!”

He then immediately gave the astonished student an “excellent”.

Sometimes “it is obvious” can be more dangerous than complete ignorance. Sometimes, though, it saves your exams.

Further information and sources:

- S. N. Fedin, [*Mathematicians Joke Too*](https://www.docdroid.net/ytC3FTI/fedin-sn-matematiki-toze-sutiat-pdf.pdf) (in Russian), the story “Obviously Extraordinary”, presented in the book as a faculty tale about A. Fomenko.

- [Anatoly Timofeyevich Fomenko](https://www.mathnet.ru/rus/person8368): biographical information on MSU’s Mechanics and Mathematics Faculty, the Department of Differential Geometry and his 1972 doctoral dissertation.

- [Anatoly Timofeyevich Fomenko at ISTINA, MSU](https://istina.msu.ru/workers/1089091/).

No earlier reliable source for this particular exam story has been found, so it is best regarded as a characteristic faculty tale rather than a strictly documented episode.

A similar problem in the spirit of Fomenko’s differential geometry course appears on p. 81 of the [lecture notes](https://teach-in.ru/file/synopsis/pdf/difgeom-and-topology-fomenko-M.pdf) (in Russian, PDF). A meridian of the sphere is shown to be a geodesic, with the explicit remark, “Obviously, this is a solution of equation (*) for the sphere.” Rotating the sphere then shows that any equator is a geodesic too.
