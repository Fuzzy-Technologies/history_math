Inaccessible points already occur in geometrical problems from ancient Greece. Euclid’s well-known problem asks, “Construct the bisector of an angle whose vertex is inaccessible.”

Such problems surely arose from practical needs. In 2,300 years, a whole collection has accumulated. We might call it a mathematical monument to Euclid, deserving a few flowers!

Consider the following related historical and teaching questions:

1. Study Euclid’s method of solution. What property of angles does it use?
2. Suggest other ways to solve the problem—there are about ten. Try to remember these methods, or heuristics; they are useful for other problems of this kind too.

## Notes

For more problems involving inaccessible points, see the following article (in Russian):

- G. Filippovsky, “Euclid and Problems with Inaccessible Points”, *Matematika*, 2010, no. 18, pp. 27–30.

- Teachers may also be interested in Filippovsky’s [*Avtorskaya shkolnaya geometriya*, parts 1 and 2](https://filippovsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/avtorska-shkilna-geometriya-2012-2.pdf) (an author’s approach to school geometry, in Russian, PDF).
