At the beginning of the twentieth century, Dedekind (1831–1916), the renowned German mathematician who laid the foundations of modern algebra, opened a calendar and read: “Richard Dedekind. Died in Brunswick on 4 September 1899.” He wrote to its publisher: “My esteemed colleague! May I draw your attention to the fact that, in the date of my death, at least the year is incorrect.”
