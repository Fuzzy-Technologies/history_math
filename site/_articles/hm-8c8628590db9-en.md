At an entrance examination, an applicant cheerfully recites the parallel axiom:

“Through any point not on a given straight line, one can draw one line parallel to it, provided one draws it *rovno*.”

The examiner freezes for a moment:

“Rovno? Why rovno? Where did you get that from?”

“From the school textbook,” the applicant replies calmly. “Shall I show you?”

A minute later, he brings the book and points to the passage. There it is, in black and white:

“…one can draw *rovno* one straight line parallel to the given line…”

That is how it happens: put the emphasis in the wrong place, on *rovno*, and the whole of geometry wobbles for a moment. The Russian word can mean both “exactly” and “evenly” or “straight”: the applicant has mistaken an exact count for advice on drawing.

It is also a fine way to remember the difference between [Euclid](https://t.me/history_math/55) and [Lobachevsky](https://t.me/history_math/39). In Euclidean geometry, exactly one parallel passes through a point outside a line. Change the axiom, and the world becomes something quite different.
