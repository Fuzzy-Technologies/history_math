*Marking the 75th anniversary of her birth*

A Soviet and Russian mathematics educator, historian of mathematics education, bibliographer and local historian.

## Education and Teaching

She was born on 17 May 1950 in Leningrad into the family of the distinguished mathematician and educator Zalman Alterovich Skopets (1917–1984), who held a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree.

She attended School No. 4 in Yaroslavl, which offered advanced English teaching. She then studied in the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at Yaroslavl State Pedagogical Institute, now K. D. Ushinsky Yaroslavl State Pedagogical University, from 1967 to 1972. After graduating, she pursued postgraduate studies there and prepared a dissertation in constructive geometry.

After completing her postgraduate studies in 1976, she was assigned to Vologda State Pedagogical Institute, now a university. She worked for three years as an assistant in its Department of Algebra and Geometry.

In 1979 she returned to Yaroslavl as an assistant in the Department of Elementary Mathematics at Yaroslavl State Pedagogical Institute. In 1985 she moved to the Department of Geometry, where she worked until the end of 2012, becoming a senior lecturer in 1993. She taught practical classes in various branches of geometry and the history of mathematics.

## The History of Mathematics Education

On beginning her teaching work in Yaroslavl, Revekka Zalmanovna began studying the history of mathematics teaching in higher and secondary education in Yaroslavl and Russia.

In 2014–2015 she was a researcher in the Department of the History of Mathematics and Mathematics Education at the Mathematical Enlightenment research and practice centre in Moscow. She worked closely with the History of Mathematics Section of the Russian Academy of Sciences’ Institute for the History of Science and Technology.

Drawing on considerable experience in the history of mathematics, she developed a new course in the subject, introduced in 1985 and assigned to the institute’s Department of Geometry. She devoted sustained, detailed work to compiling bibliographies on the history of mathematics and of mathematics education in Russia.

For more than twenty years she studied the history of Russian secondary schools before 1917. More than seventy publications, including papers in major journals, were devoted to this subject.

## The Kolmogorov Readings and Bibliography

For many years she organized and inspired the annual international Kolmogorov Readings conference at Yaroslavl State Pedagogical University. In 2003, the centenary of A. N. Kolmogorov’s birth, a memorial plaque was placed on his grandfather’s house in Yaroslavl. Gushel made a substantial contribution to compiling the mathematician’s biography.

With her active involvement, the All-Russian School on the History of Mathematics was convened in Yaroslavl in 2003 after a long interruption, marking Kolmogorov’s centenary. The Kolmogorov Readings were held annually at Yaroslavl State Pedagogical University from 2004 to 2015. Counting the 2003 school, there were thirteen meetings. They included a large mathematics teaching section and a section on mathematics and its applications.

Gushel worked closely and fruitfully with the editors of *Mathematical Education* for many years. She compiled anniversary calendars for different years, covering notable people in mathematics and mathematics education as well as important events in these fields.

Revekka Zalmanovna assembled an enormous card index on the history of mathematics and mathematics education. Among her publications was the well-known *From the History of Mathematics and Mathematics Education: A Guide to the Literature* (1999).
