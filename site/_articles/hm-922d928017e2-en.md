*Marking the 130th anniversary of his birth*

An outstanding Soviet educator and specialist in mathematics teaching, a Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences, professor and historian of mathematics and mathematics education. He became a corresponding member of the RSFSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences in 1957 and was named an Honoured Scientist of the Russian SFSR in 1964.

## Life and Teaching

He was born on 21 May 1894, or 2 June in the Gregorian calendar, in Korsakovo, Novosil District, Oryol Governorate.

He graduated from the P. G. Shelaputin Higher Pedagogical Institute in Moscow in 1918 and took an active part in the work of the RSFSR People’s Commissariat of Education.

He worked as a village schoolteacher and taught at the Poretsk Teacher Training Seminary and the Tver Pedagogical Institute.

From 1923 he held posts as a docent and professor of mathematics at several Moscow institutes. In 1933 he was appointed head of the Department of Higher Algebra, Elementary Mathematics and Mathematics Teaching Methods at the Krupskaya Moscow Regional Pedagogical Institute, known as MOPI. He held this post for more than forty years.

Dozens of dissertations were completed under his supervision. His pupils included Yu. M. Kolyagin, a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences and full member of the Russian Academy of Education, and G. L. Lukankin, a corresponding member of the Russian Academy of Education.

## Curricula and Textbooks

He wrote the first programme for training the new generation of mathematics teachers, adopted at the First All-Russian Congress of Teachers in August 1918, and subsequently directed the development of mathematics-teaching curricula until 1965.

He wrote more than forty textbooks and teaching books, more than 135 publications and more than ten curricula covering mathematics for schools and higher education, teaching methods and the history of mathematics.

He wrote arithmetic textbooks for secondary schools, including:

*Arithmetic of Natural Numbers*. Moscow, 1954 (in Russian).

I. K. Andronov and V. M. Bradis, *Arithmetic: A Book for Secondary Schools*, 1957 (in Russian).

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

*Arithmetic: The Development of the Concept of Number and Operations on Numbers*. Moscow, 1959 (in Russian).

## History of Education and the Seminar

He studied questions of methodology and the history of mathematics. His well-known works include:

*Half a Century of School Mathematics Education in the USSR*, 1967 (in Russian).

*A Trilogy of the Subject and Method of Mathematics*, a teaching book edited by Professor I. I. Bavrin. Moscow: Moscow State Regional University. Part I, 2004; Part II, 2003; Part III, 2004 (in Russian). The edition of the *Trilogy* marked Andronov’s 110th birth anniversary and the 45th anniversary of his seminar.

In 1959, under Andronov’s direction, the RSFSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences established a seminar entitled “Contemporary Ideas in Mathematics Teaching in the USSR and Abroad”; meetings were held mainly at MOPI. After the academy was reorganized in 1966, the seminar continued under the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences. After Andronov’s death it became known as the Andronov Seminar.

Andronov served on the editorial board of the journal *Mathematics at School* for more than thirty years.
