*Marking the 130th anniversary of his birth*

One of the founders of the Soviet school of probability theory, a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences (1939), a full member of the RSFSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences (1944), a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences (1935) and a professor at Moscow State University.

## Life and Teaching

He was born on 7 July 1894, or 19 July in the Gregorian calendar, in Kondrovo, then a village and later a town, in Kaluga Governorate.

He studied at Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics in 1911–1916 and became one of Nikolai Luzin’s first pupils. His teaching career began in 1918 in Ivanovo-Voznesensk, where he moved with Luzin. He soon became the first dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at the Ivanovo-Voznesensk Pedagogical Institute. In 1922 he returned to Moscow, joining the Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics established at Moscow University.

In 1926 he was invited to head the Department of Mathematical Analysis at the Second Moscow State University. He was confirmed as a professor at Moscow State University in 1927. In 1935 he was awarded a doctorate in physical and mathematical sciences without defending a dissertation.

From 1943 to 1957, he headed the Department of Mathematical Analysis in Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics.

## Research and Teaching

Khinchin produced outstanding work on functions of a real variable and number theory. He applied methods from the metric theory of functions to problems in probability. One major result that brought him worldwide recognition was the Lévy–Khintchine formula, which characterizes the characteristic functions of infinitely divisible distributions. Together with Andrey Kolmogorov, he laid the foundations of the general theory of stochastic processes. From 1930 onward, he worked on creating queueing theory.

He made a substantial contribution to mathematics education in universities and secondary schools and was active in the movement to reform school mathematics. He was one of the founders of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences in 1943 and wrote on teaching mathematics, including *Pedagogical Articles*, *On the Educational Effect of Mathematics Lessons* and *Basic Mathematical Concepts in Secondary School*.

He wrote *A Short Course of Mathematical Analysis*, *Asymptotic Laws of Probability Theory*, *Studies in the Mathematical Theory of Queueing* and *Mathematical Foundations of Statistical Mechanics*, as well as the well-known books for a general audience *Fermat’s Last Theorem*, *Three Pearls of Number Theory*, *Continued Fractions* and *An Elementary Introduction to Probability Theory*.

He received the Stalin Prize, second class, for his work on probability theory in 1941.

## Early Poetry

P.S. As an adolescent, Khinchin was passionate about poetry. Four small collections of his poems appeared in Kaluga in 1908–1914.

> But to all the abandoned idols,\
> You will cry out that love is alive.\
> And, in a feast of many colours,\
> Mere words will blaze in an empty soul.
