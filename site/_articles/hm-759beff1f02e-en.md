*Marking the 105th anniversary of his birth*

Isaak Moiseyevich Yaglom (1921–1988) was a Soviet geometer, educator and popularizer of mathematics. He received his Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1965 and was a professor.

He could discuss profound geometry in a way that let the central idea emerge from formulas and axioms: geometry is the study of transformations, symmetries and invariants.

## Study and Teaching

Born in Kharkov on 6 March 1921, he moved to Moscow with his family in 1925. He finished school there and entered Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1938. His health prevented him from serving at the front during the war, and he was evacuated with his family to Sverdlovsk, where he graduated from university in 1942. In 1945 he defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation in geometry under V. F. Kagan. His twin brother, Akiva Moiseyevich Yaglom, became a distinguished mathematician and physicist.

Isaak’s subsequent life centred on teaching and mathematical education. He worked at universities and teacher training institutes, supported school mathematics clubs and participated in mathematical olympiads. He taught at Yaroslavl State University in 1974–1983 and served as a technical consultant to the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences in 1984–1988.

## The Geometry of Transformations

His research interests included classical and projective geometry, conic sections, transformations of the plane and space, and connections between geometry, algebra and analysis. To most readers, however, he was known through his books. His two-volume Russian *Geometric Transformations* (1955–1956) became a standard reference for mathematics clubs, teaching readers to think through isometries, similarities, inversions, circle transformations and linear transformations. Geometry ceased to be just about triangles and became a language of symmetries and projections.

He favoured an approach in which a proof begins by asking what is preserved at each step: angles, ratios of lengths, tangencies or relative positions. This brought clarity. A problem was solved through a well-chosen transformation and attention to an invariant, rather than sheer calculation. He trained generations of mathematics club pupils and future professionals in this way.

Favourite themes of classical geometry recur throughout his writing: similarities and rotations, inversion, transformations of circles and lines, and projective correspondences. With complex numbers, geometry also becomes a convenient computational language for rotations and dilations.

## Books and Mathematical Education

He wrote for both pupils and teachers, co-authoring upper-secondary geometry teaching materials with V. G. Boltyansky and publishing popular articles, including in *Kvant*. His books were translated into many languages, and his approach to transformation geometry became part of international mathematical culture.

He created the Mathematics Club Library series, which published many books. He also wrote historical studies exploring mathematics’ connections with the natural sciences and humanities and its role in society.

Isaak Moiseyevich wrote more than forty books and numerous articles.

He died in Moscow on 17 April 1988 and was buried at Vostryakovo Cemetery.

His books still open a door to geometry for readers who want to see an idea as well as solve a problem. His rigorous yet humane style became a model for teaching mathematics without oversimplifying it or keeping it at a reverential distance.
