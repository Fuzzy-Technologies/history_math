Mathematicians enjoy some dark humour.

Shortly before his death, Abraham de Moivre (1667–1754), the French-born mathematician working in England, announced that he needed to sleep 10–15 minutes longer each day than the day before. The day after he passed the twenty-three-hour mark, he slept a full twenty-four hours and died in his sleep.
