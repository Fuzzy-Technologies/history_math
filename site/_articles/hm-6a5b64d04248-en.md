*Marking the 80th anniversary of his birth*

Valery Vasilyevich Vavilov (30 June 1946–21 October 2016) was a Soviet and Russian mathematics educator, a Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences and an Honoured Teacher of the Russian Federation. Almost his entire career was linked with Moscow State University’s Specialized Educational and Scientific Centre, SUNC, the Kolmogorov School. There he taught, built mathematics into a coherent course and sustained the tradition established in the school’s early years.

## The Kolmogorov School

He was born in Komsomolsk-on-Amur and died in Moscow. He graduated from Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1968 and joined the school that same year at Andrei Nikolayevich Kolmogorov’s personal invitation.

Over time he became a leading organizer of mathematical life at SUNC. From 15 April 1990 Vavilov served as acting head of its mathematics department, becoming head in July 1992; from September 2013 he was deputy director for research, and from September 2014 a professor at SUNC. He also taught at the university as an associate professor in the Department of Mathematical Analysis in 1987–2016. Obituaries from the school and the faculty emphasize both the scale of his work and his rare combination of demanding standards with respect for the pupil.

## Learning to Think and Check One’s Work

For him, school mathematics meant more than solving problems: learning to think and check one’s own work mattered most. He wrote extensively about the experience of Physics and Mathematics School No. 18 and how to foster pupils’ interest in research, from posing questions carefully to writing up a solution or presenting a report.

His problem books and works on teaching form another prominent part of his legacy. They range from the *Problems in Mathematics* series, covering algebra, equations and inequalities, and introductory analysis, to shorter but lively books such as *Polygons on Lattices* and *A School of Mathematical Creativity*.

He also took part in the olympiad movement, including preparing USSR teams, and initiated the international school pupils’ conference *Kolmogorov Readings* in 2000. He served on the editorial boards of *Kvant* and *Potentsial*, editing the latter’s mathematics section for many years.

## Recognition and Remembrance

His work received official recognition. His awards included the Lomonosov Prize for teaching in 2006, the Honorary Worker of General Education of the Russian Federation badge in 2009, the title Honoured Lecturer of Moscow University in 2005 and Honoured Teacher of the Russian Federation in 2015. He was also a winner of the Dynasty Foundation’s school teacher competitions in several years.

He is remembered for more than dates and appointments: he taught a way of thinking, with clear questions, honest reasoning, a taste for a good problem and the habit of seeing work through to completion. In that sense, his work continues wherever someone once heard and understood him.
