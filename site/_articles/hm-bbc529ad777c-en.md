He was born on 17 August 1601 in Beaumont-de-Lomagne, France.

A self-taught French mathematician and a lawyer from Toulouse, and one of the greatest mathematicians of all time.

He knew many ancient and modern languages. He became a versatile mathematician: together with Descartes he created analytic geometry, together with Pascal he laid the foundations of probability theory, and he devised a new method for tangents and extrema. Fermat is regarded as one of the founders of early modern number theory.

His results reached us in scattered form. He wrote little and concisely; during his lifetime many of his results circulated in manuscripts and letters rather than in print. He corresponded actively with members of Marin Mersenne’s circle of mathematicians.

Some number-theoretic results survived only as assertions without proofs. Fermat conjectured that every number $F_n=2^{2^n}+1$ was prime; Euler disproved this already for $n=5$. Whether infinitely many Fermat primes exist remains an open question.

Fermat’s little theorem is a fundamental result in number theory: if $p$ is prime and $a$ is not divisible by $p$, then $a^{p-1}-1$ is divisible by $p$. It is widely used in cryptography, coding theory and finite fields.

Fermat’s last theorem states that the equation $x^n+y^n=z^n$ has no solution in positive integers $x$, $y$, $z$ when $n$ is an integer greater than 2. Efforts to prove it lasted 300 years, and it was officially recognised as proved only in 1995. It is regarded as “the most famous mathematical puzzle of all time”.

His treatise *Introduction to Plane and Solid Loci* (1636) contains the beginnings of Fermat’s analytic geometry.

In *Method for Finding Maxima and Minima* (1638), Fermat devised a procedure for finding extrema and tangents.

Although Fermat left only fragmentary notes and manuscript versions of his works, most of his brilliant investigations nevertheless reached later generations. He inspired mathematicians who came after him.
