Let us describe a unique phenomenon in the scientific and educational life of the USSR and Russia: Mordkovich's Seminar.

Since 1987, Russia has hosted the International Scientific Seminar for Teachers of Mathematics and Computer Science at Universities and Pedagogical Higher-Education Institutions. It acquired the unofficial name “Mordkovich's Seminar” in honour of its organizer and continuing scientific director, Aleksandr Grigoryevich Mordkovich. As a mark of respect and recognition of this phenomenon's uniqueness, I will write “Seminar” with a capital letter.

Aleksandr Grigoryevich holds a Doctorate in Pedagogical Sciences and a Candidate degree in Physical and Mathematical Sciences. He is a professor, an Honoured Scientist of the Russian Federation and a laureate of the President of the Russian Federation's Prize in Education. He has received the K. D. Ushinsky Medal and the Excellence in Public Education distinction.

In 1987–2023, 42 seminars were held in different Russian cities. It began as a scientific Seminar for mathematics lecturers at the country's pedagogical institutes, primarily addressing the problems of preparing mathematics teachers. Very soon it became a meeting place for like-minded people concerned about the future of all Russian mathematics education, and a creative laboratory for developing and testing new ideas in mathematics teaching. It has always discussed the most pressing issues facing mathematics education in schools and universities.

Many research ideas originated at these meetings. Scholars pursuing Doctor and Candidate degrees in pedagogical sciences tested their results at the Seminar before defending their dissertations. It is also very important that beginning researchers, almost always attending in large numbers, presented their first research results here and received constructive, friendly criticism and useful advice.

The Seminar has the scientific capacity to tackle urgent problems in mathematics education. Its Doctors of Sciences and professors have founded new research directions and schools. Such schools operate throughout Russia: in Barnaul, Bryansk, Vologda, Yekaterinburg, Kazan, Kaluga, Kirov, Krasnoyarsk, Moscow, Orsk, Perm, Ryazan, Samara, St Petersburg, Saratov, Tolyatti, Ulyanovsk, Cheboksary, Chelyabinsk, Yaroslavl and other cities and universities.

Our Seminar is unique: tied to no particular city or university, it is a traveller. Its participants have had opportunities to visit many parts of Russia.

The Seminar's two or three days of work usually include meetings of two sections: Teaching Mathematics at University and Teaching Mathematics at School. Proceedings are published by the opening of the Seminar. Colleagues from Belarus and Kazakhstan, and formerly from Ukraine, often take part with their research.

As a regular participant and an organizer of some of these Seminars, I will focus for now on those held in Yelabuga. The city hosted it twice, in 1994 and 2011.

13\. 1994, Yelabuga. “Preparing Mathematics Teachers for Subject-Profile and Attainment-Level Differentiation in School Teaching”.

30\. 2011, Yelabuga. “Innovative Technologies for Teaching Mathematics at School and University”.

The organizer of the 1994 Seminar in Yelabuga was the Yelabuga State Pedagogical Institute. Well-known scholars and figures in mathematics teaching methods took part: S. L. Atanasyan, G. L. Lukankin, N. I. Merlina, Ye. S. Petrova and S. L. Soloveychik.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

The 2011 Seminar in Yelabuga was organized by Kazan (Volga Region) Federal University and its Yelabuga branch, formerly Yelabuga State Pedagogical University. It brought together well-known scholars in the theory and methods of mathematics teaching — Ye. M. Vechtomov, Yu. A. Drobyshev, I. V. Drobysheva, A. L. Zhokhov, I. G. Lipatnikova, I. Ye. Malova, V. V. Orlov, S. S. Salavatova, I. S. Safuanov, V. A. Testov and L. V. Shkerina — as well as lecturers from higher- and secondary-vocational institutions and general-education schoolteachers. Delegations came to Yelabuga from 23 cities.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

The programme included a teachers' section on “Teaching Mathematics in General-Education Institutions Using A. G. Mordkovich's Teaching and Learning Materials”, and masterclasses by A. G. Mordkovich of Moscow on teaching trigonometry and T. G. Khodot of St Petersburg on school geometry.

The cultural programme in every host city was always interesting and memorable.

***The Seminar Anthem***

*Autumn comes again, and so does our Seminar.*

*We have gathered here from all across Russia.*

*Again we have defined the range of problems,*

*And discussed them with great warmth.*

*As before, we cannot forget*

*The many papers and presentations.*

*However many tasks confront us,*

*We will always find their solutions.*

*Mordkovich is our compass in our work,*

*And our students' knowledge our reward.*

*Let them call a gathering in the mountains —*

*We will rush there if we must.*

(Collective authorship, to the melody of “Nadezhda” (“Hope”), performed by Anna German and the Melodiya ensemble.)
