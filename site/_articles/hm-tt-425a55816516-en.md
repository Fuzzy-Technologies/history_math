The Bernoulli family is a unique phenomenon in mathematics and physics. Nine members of this famous Swiss dynasty became major mathematicians and physicists, and three are regarded as great scientists.

The Bernoullis made substantial scientific contributions in the second half of the XVII century and during the XVIII century, at the dawn of the mathematics of variable quantities: differential and integral calculus, differential equations, series, calculus of variations and probability theory. Leibniz’s work exerted enormous influence on his contemporaries. He created a mathematical school that included the Bernoulli brothers, l’Hôpital and Euler. Before 1700, the triumvirate of Leibniz, Jacob and Johann Bernoulli had already discovered much of the basic course of mathematical analysis.

The dynasty also includes well-known historians, art historians, architects and lawyers. Historians have counted at least 30 famous Bernoullis in science and culture. Five family members were academicians of the Saint Petersburg Academy of Sciences. Bernoullis held the University of Basel’s mathematics chair almost continuously for 105 years. This article briefly traces the family’s history in Basel.

In 1622, Jacob Bernoulli (1598–1634) settled in Basel, Switzerland, and became a Swiss citizen. His son, whom historians call “Nicholas the Elder” (1623–1708), established a trade in pharmaceutical goods and soon became a wealthy citizen and member of Basel’s city council. His 11 children founded the Bernoulli dynasty.

Certain names recur across generations of Bernoullis, so, as with kings, a numeral is added to distinguish them.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Three generations produced nine major mathematicians and physicists, the best known being:

• Jacob Bernoulli (1654–1705).

• Johann Bernoulli (1667–1748), Jacob’s younger brother.

• Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782), Johann’s son.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

Jacob Bernoulli (1654–1705), son of Nicholas the Elder, was a pupil of his brother Johann, his nephew Nicholas I and Euler’s father, Paul. He was a founder of probability theory and mathematical analysis. Together with his brother Johann, he initiated the calculus of variations. He proved a special case of the law of large numbers, Bernoulli’s theorem. He was professor of mathematics at Basel and a foreign member of the Paris (1699) and Berlin (1702) academies of sciences.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

Johann Bernoulli (1667–1748), son of Nicholas the Elder, was a mathematician, mechanician, physician and philologist, and the family’s most famous member. An early developer of mathematical analysis, he was Europe’s foremost mathematician after Newton’s death and Euler’s teacher. He was a foreign member of the Paris (1699) and Berlin (1701) academies, a foreign honorary member of the Saint Petersburg Academy (1725), and a member of the Royal Society of London (1712).

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782), Johann’s son, was a physicist, mechanician and mathematician, and a founder of the kinetic theory of gases, hydrodynamics and mathematical physics. He was an academician and foreign honorary member (1733) of the Saint Petersburg Academy of Sciences, a member of the academies of Bologna (1724), Berlin (1747) and Paris (1748), and of the Royal Society of London (1750).

Let us briefly consider the other Bernoullis’ activities.

> Nicholas Bernoulli (1662–1716), son of Nicholas the Elder, was a painter.

> Nicholas I Bernoulli (1687–1759), Nicholas’s son, taught mathematics in Padua and later law and logic in Basel.

> Nicholas II Bernoulli (1695–1726), Johann’s son, moved to Russia and taught mathematics in Saint Petersburg.

> Johann II (1710–1790), Johann’s youngest son, taught mathematics in Basel.

> Johann III (1744–1807), Johann II’s eldest son, was an astronomer at the Berlin Academy of Sciences and later head of its mathematics class.

> Daniel II (1751–1834), Johann II’s middle son, qualified as a physician and subsequently rose to professor of eloquence in Basel.

> Jacob II Bernoulli (1759–1789), Johann II’s son, was a researcher in mechanics and an ordinary academician of the Saint Petersburg Academy of Sciences (1787). He worked at the academy from 1786 and also at the Cadet Corps from 1787.

Today, members of the Bernoulli family live mainly in Basel.

Named after the Bernoullis are:

• Bernoulli’s differential equation, lemniscate, polynomial, inequality, distribution, scheme in probability theory, shift, formula and numbers — after Jacob.

• Bernoulli’s law, or Bernoulli integral, in hydrodynamics — after Daniel.

• Rue Bernoulli, a street in Paris.

• The Bernoulli Laboratory at Moscow State University’s Institute for Mathematical Research of Complex Systems — after Daniel.
