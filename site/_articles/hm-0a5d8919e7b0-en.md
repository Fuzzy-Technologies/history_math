Rejoice, everyone! We have entered a year of “Great Joy”!

The Indian mathematician Dattatreya Ramchandra Kaprekar (1905–1986) called natural numbers divisible by the sum of their digits “Harshad numbers”. The word comes from the Sanskrit *harṣa*, “great joy”. (They are also called Niven numbers. Ivan Niven (1915–1999) was a Canadian-American mathematician specialising in number theory.)

Let us see:

$2024\div(2+0+2+4)=253$.

It divides exactly! Harshad!

We are in luck: next year will be the same, since $2025\div9=225$. We were lucky in 2023 and 2022 as well.

I am lucky too: I was born in a year of Great Joy — 1950.

We will have to wait almost a thousand years for the next run of four Harshad years, until 3030.

These numbers have many remarkable properties. We can prove them rigorously and also experiment with them.

1. All one-digit natural numbers are Harshad numbers.

2. All numbers whose digit sum is 3 or 9 are Harshad numbers.

3. For every natural number $N$, there is a Harshad number with digit sum $N$.

4. There are infinitely many Harshad numbers.

5. There are arbitrarily long gaps in the natural numbers containing no Harshad numbers.

6. A run of Harshad numbers cannot be longer than 20, although runs of length 20 do occur.
