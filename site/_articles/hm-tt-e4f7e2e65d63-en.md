(For the 80th anniversary of the Great Victory)

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-1" %}

Yelabuga's cultural life during the war was enlivened by the creative people evacuated there. Scholars from the Leningrad University branch also contributed to the cultural education of the local population. They were people with many talents. The branch had its own amateur arts activities, described in the memoirs of the Leningrad scholars V. V. Sobolev and N. A. Nikiforovskaya.

Perhaps some older Yelabuga residents attended concerts at the Teachers' Institute given by staff of the Leningrad State University branch. A major event in the life of the branch, and perhaps of Yelabuga itself, was the enthusiasts' creation of the Drambalop, an amateur theatre of drama, ballet and opera. Ballet never materialized, but the other two did. The mathematician and academician V. I. Smirnov, for instance, was known to play the piano beautifully, while the astronomer Professor V. V. Sharonov, the son of a celebrated Mariinsky Theatre singer, performed operatic arias.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Among the Drambalop's most prominent participants were Professor V. N. Tsvetkov, a chemist who later became a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences and a laureate of the 1952 State Prize; Professor S. F. Rodionov, a physicist who created the first photon counter; Professor B. P. Nikolsky, a chemist and future academician; Professor B. N. Dolgov, a chemist; S. A. Shchukarev; E. V. Frisman; and A. I. Anselm. Productions were staged in the packed assembly hall of the Teachers' Institute. N. A. Nikiforovskaya wrote that she remembered the performance of A. N. Ostrovsky's *Guilty Without Guilt*. Its premiere took place on 9 May 1942 — a symbolic date! The production was a great success.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

> “Mother and I saw *Guilty Without Guilt* on 17 May. The hall was still full; this was the third performance. I went with strong reservations. I still vividly remembered the magnificent production of *Guilty Without Guilt* by Moscow's Maly Theatre, which had toured Leningrad in the autumn of 1940. It featured such remarkable performers as V. N. Pashennaya, M. M. Klimov, E. D. Turchaninova, M. I. Tsaryov and I. V. Ilyinsky… So I had something to compare it with… I enjoyed the production much more than I had expected. The sets were modest and tasteful, as were the costumes. In the fourth act, set in a public garden, there was a real fountain on stage. On the whole, the acting was quite good. I liked A. I. Anselm best, as Milovzorov. He played simply and naturally, with a subtle sense of humour and no exaggeration. I thought he could have played this part in a professional theatre. B. P. Nikolsky gave a refined and sensitive performance as Dudukin. B. N. Dolgov also played the inn servant well. V. N. Tsvetkov handled the unrewarding role of Murov quite creditably.

> At the performance Mother and I attended, the overwhelming majority of the audience were local residents. They followed the action on stage with unwavering attention. The hall was completely silent. Afterwards, the audience warmly applauded the ‘actors’. For many spectators, this production was probably their first encounter with Ostrovsky's play. All the proceeds were donated to the Defence Fund. A considerable amount was raised: tickets were quite expensive for those days, at 5 and 6 roubles, and the hall held a large audience.

> In the summer of 1942, the Leningrad Regional Drama Theatre came to Yelabuga on tour. I saw its productions and found that its actors were weaker than some of the amateur performers from the Leningrad State University branch. Apparently, this was because the Leningrad scholars had greater cultural refinement and subtler artistic taste than the actors of the regional theatre.”

This is another vivid mark left on our town's history by the remarkable scholars from Leningrad.
