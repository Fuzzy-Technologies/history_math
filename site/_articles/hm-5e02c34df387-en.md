*Marking the 205th anniversary of his birth*

A French mathematician and astronomer. He became a member of the Paris Academy of Sciences in 1860.

## Education and Teaching

He was born in Paris on 30 August 1819.

He graduated from the École Polytechnique in 1840 and worked there as an examiner of applicants.

He taught theoretical astronomy at the Collège de France from 1856 and took the chair of celestial mechanics in 1861.

He was a professor in the chair of analysis at the Sorbonne from 1863 to 1871.

## Research and Textbooks

He conducted research in differential geometry, number theory, analysis and mechanics.

He helped develop Galois’s work. He edited the fourteen-volume collection of Lagrange’s works (1867–1892; Gaston Darboux and Ludovic Lalanne also contributed to the edition) and the fifth edition of Monge’s *Application de l’analyse à la géométrie* (1850). Camille Jordan was a notable student of his.

He wrote textbooks that circulated widely in France and other countries, including courses in higher algebra (*Cours d’algèbre supérieure*), analysis (*Cours de calcul différentiel et intégral*) and trigonometry (*Traité de trigonométrie*).

His textbooks went through several editions and were translated into other languages. The following appeared in Russian (titles translated here):

- *A Course in Arithmetic* (1871).
- *Plane Trigonometry* (1878).
- *A Course in Differential and Integral Calculus*, vols. 1–2 (1883–1884).
- *A Course in Higher Algebra* (1897).
