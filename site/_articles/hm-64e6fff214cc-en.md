*Marking the 175th anniversary of his birth*

A Russian scholar, the first historian of mathematics in Russia, a bibliographer and mathematics educator.

He became a professor at Moscow University in 1917. He was one of the authors of the Brockhaus and Efron Encyclopedic Dictionary, contributing more than three hundred articles on mathematics.

## Life and Scholarship

He was born on 8 November 1849, or 20 November in the Gregorian calendar, in the village of Shili, Roslavl District, Smolensk Governorate.

After graduating from Tula Gymnasium with a gold medal in 1867, Viktor Bobynin entered the natural sciences division of Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. He later transferred to the mathematics division, graduating in 1872 with the university degree of candidate.

For the next ten years, Bobynin taught mathematics, physics and cosmography at various educational institutions of the Russian Empire, beginning in Nizhny Novgorod at its military gymnasium.

Bobynin chose the Rhind papyrus as the subject of his dissertation. After defending his magister dissertation, “Mathematics among the Ancient Egyptians (from the Rhind Papyrus)”, in March 1882, he began teaching an optional course on the history of mathematics at Moscow University in October 1882 as a Privatdozent. No one in Russia had previously taught such a course.

He was a scholar devoted wholeheartedly to the history of science and a popularizer of the history of mathematical knowledge and its use in teaching. At his own expense, he published the journal *The Physical and Mathematical Sciences in Their Present and Past* from 1885 to 1898.

After the revolution of 1917, Bobynin moved to his estate near Tula. From 1918 he lived in Tula, where he taught mathematics at Tula Gymnasium.

## History in Mathematics Teaching

Viktor Viktorovich also developed the pedagogical foundations of mathematics. In late 1911 and early 1912, he addressed the First All-Russian Congress of Mathematics Teachers with a paper entitled “Aims, Forms and Means of Introducing Historical Elements into the Secondary-School Mathematics Course”. He developed the idea of a historical mathematics reader that, in his view, “should include, alongside articles on the history of mathematics, extracts from works of ancient mathematical literature that are suitable for their purpose and accessible to pupils”.

In his 1886 work “The Philosophical, Scientific and Pedagogical Significance of the History of Mathematics”, he wrote: “The teaching of each science should follow the same path that the science itself followed in its development.” Furthermore, the history of mathematics should provide the theoretical foundation for mathematics teaching: “The history of mathematics should draw up a detailed programme for the art of teaching mathematics and, together with the philosophy of mathematics, indicate the techniques and methods for carrying out this programme.”

## Manuscripts and Library

Many handwritten mathematics textbooks in Russian survive from the seventeenth century. Bobynin was the first to study them, describing early records of Russian popular mathematics, manuscripts and rare mathematics books. He bequeathed his unique library of more than 5,000 books, including about one hundred mathematical manuscripts from the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, to Moscow University.
