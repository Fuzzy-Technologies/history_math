*Marking the 175th anniversary of his birth*

A well-known German mathematician. He became a member of the Prussian Academy of Sciences in 1893.

He was born on 26 October 1849 in Charlottenburg, a suburb of Berlin.

## Education and Teaching

In 1860 he entered the Joachimsthal Gymnasium. In 1867 he attended Göttingen University for one semester, then continued his studies at Humboldt University in Berlin. He attended lectures by Leopold Kronecker, Ernst Eduard Kummer and Karl Weierstrass.

In 1870 he defended his dissertation there under the supervision of Weierstrass and Kummer.

In 1874 he was appointed a professor at the University of Berlin. He spent seventeen years in Zurich, from 1875 to 1892, at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology. In 1892 he returned to the University of Berlin to take the position left vacant by Leopold Kronecker’s death.

## Research

His research concerned elliptic functions, differential equations and finite groups, particularly representation theory.

Frobenius and Weierstrass laid the foundations of matrix theory as an algebraic discipline.

Alongside Hamilton, Frobenius is considered one of the creators of the algebra of hypercomplex numbers.

## The Coin Problem

The coin problem, also known as the Frobenius problem, is sometimes used as a research project even for school pupils:

“For specified positive integer denominations with greatest common divisor 1, find the largest monetary amount that cannot be made using coins of those denominations.”

For example, the largest amount that cannot be made using only coins of denominations 3 and 5 is 7.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Frobenius was the first to prove that finite-dimensional associative division algebras over the real numbers exist only in dimensions one, the field of real numbers; two, the field of complex numbers; and four, the division ring of quaternions. This is the famous Frobenius theorem in the theory of number systems (1877). If associativity is dropped, Cayley’s alternative eight-dimensional algebra of octonions is another example.

There are no other finite-dimensional associative real division algebras.

The following mathematical concepts are named after Frobenius:

- The Frobenius endomorphism of a commutative ring of characteristic p.
- The Frobenius integrability criterion for distributions.
- Frobenius algebra.
- Frobenius matrix.
- The Frobenius formula for inverting a block matrix.
- Frobenius norm.
- Frobenius group.
- Frobenius inequality.
- Frobenius theorem.
- The Cauchy–Frobenius lemma.
- Frobenius polynomial.
- The Perron–Frobenius operator.
- The Perron–Frobenius theorem.
- Frobenius factorization.
- The Frobenius reciprocity theorem.
- Frobenius pseudoprime.
- The Frobenius primality test.
- The Frobenius coin problem.
