The well-known German mathematician Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet (1805–1859) liked formulas far more than words and was therefore a man of few words. He managed without words even when he sent his father a telegram announcing the birth of his son. What was probably the world’s shortest telegram read:

$$
2 + 1 = 3
$$

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Johann Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gustav_Lejeune_Dirichlet) (1805–1859) was a German mathematician who made substantial contributions to mathematical analysis, the theory of functions and number theory. He was a member of the academies of sciences in Berlin, Paris and St Petersburg.

In analysis and mathematical physics, he introduced the concept of conditional convergence of a series and gave a convergence test. He proved that, for a piecewise monotone periodic function, its Fourier series converges to the function’s value at points of continuity and to the average of the one-sided limits at points of discontinuity. He formulated Dirichlet’s principle in mathematical physics and substantially advanced potential theory.

In number theory, he proved the theorem on arithmetic progressions: the sequence $\{a+nb\}_{n=0}^{\infty}$, where $a$ and $b$ are coprime positive integers, contains infinitely many primes.

One of Dirichlet’s students was the German mathematician [Leopold Kronecker](https://t.me/history_math/124) (in Russian) (1823–1891). He believed that only arithmetic possessed true reality. Kronecker famously declared, “God made the integers; all else is the work of man.”
