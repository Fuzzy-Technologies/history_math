The well-known German mathematician Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet (1805–1859) liked formulas far more than words and was therefore a man of few words. He managed without words even when he sent his father a telegram announcing the birth of his son. What was probably the world’s shortest telegram read:

$$
2 + 1 = 3
$$

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Johann Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%85%D0%BB%D0%B5,_%D0%9F%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%80_%D0%93%D1%83%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%B2_%D0%9B%D0%B5%D0%B6%D1%91%D0%BD) (1805–1859) was a German mathematician who made substantial contributions to mathematical analysis, the theory of functions and number theory. He was a member of the academies of sciences in Berlin, Paris and St Petersburg.

In analysis and mathematical physics, he introduced the concept of conditional convergence of a series and gave a convergence test. He proved that every monotone piecewise-continuous function can be expanded in a Fourier series. He formulated Dirichlet’s principle in mathematical physics and substantially advanced potential theory.

In number theory, he proved the theorem on arithmetic progressions: the sequence $\{a+nb\}_{n=0}^{\infty}$, where $a$ and $b$ are coprime positive integers, contains infinitely many primes.

One of Dirichlet’s students was the German mathematician [Leopold Kronecker](https://t.me/history_math/124) (1823–1891). He believed that only arithmetic possessed true reality. Kronecker famously declared, “God made the integers; all else is the work of man.”
