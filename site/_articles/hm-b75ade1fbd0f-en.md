A French mathematician and researcher in mechanics. He became a member of the Paris Academy of Sciences in 1688, the Royal Society in 1714 and the Berlin Academy in 1713.

## Education and Teaching

He was born in 1654 in Caen, Normandy, France.

He studied at the Jesuit college and university in Caen, receiving a master’s degree in 1682.

In 1688 he became professor of mathematics at the Collège Mazarin, also known as the Collège des Quatre-Nations. In 1706 he took the chair of Greek and Latin philosophy at the Collège Royal, now the Collège de France.

Varignon corresponded with Newton, Leibniz and the Bernoulli brothers. He was one of the first advocates of differential calculus in France.

## Mechanics

His works cover mathematical analysis, geometry, physics, mechanics, statics, kinematics and fluid mechanics.

In *A Project for a New Mechanics…* (1687), Varignon gave a precise statement of the parallelogram law of forces, developed the concept of the moment of a force and derived the theorem that bears his name.

The parallelogram law of forces states that the resultant force vector is the diagonal of the parallelogram whose sides are the two component force vectors.

Varignon’s theorem in mechanics states: “If a system of forces acting on a perfectly rigid body has a resultant, the moment of that resultant about any point or axis equals the sum of the moments of all the forces in the system about the same point or axis.”

In *New Mechanics, or Statics, the Project for Which Was Given in 1687* (1725), Varignon systematically presented the composition and resolution of forces, their moments and the rules for working with them.

A work applying differential calculus to liquid flow and water clocks appeared in 1699. In 1690 he devised a mechanical explanation of gravity. In a memoir for 1705, Varignon described his invention of the manometer, an instrument for measuring pressure.

## Varignon’s Parallelogram

Varignon’s theorem in geometry states: “The midpoints of the sides of any quadrilateral form a parallelogram. If the diagonals of the original quadrilateral have nonzero length, the sides of this parallelogram are parallel to the corresponding diagonals.”
