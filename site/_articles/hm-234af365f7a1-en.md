The late 1940s: computers still fill whole rooms, the word “programming” sounds almost like “sorcery”, and John von Neumann (1903–1957) takes the stage — one of the people who helped make computation part of everyday life.

The story goes that, in a talk on the future of electronic computers, he decided to tease an audience made up largely of mathematicians:

“…mathematics was only a small part of life, and a very simple part at that.”

The audience, of course, reacted. Von Neumann then added:

“If people do not believe that mathematics is simple, it is only because they do not realize how complicated life is.”

The irony is that life is so tangled and complicated that mathematics looks remarkably simple and tidy beside it.

## Notes

Source: Franz L. Alt, “Archaeology of computers: Reminiscences, 1945–1947”, *Communications of the ACM* (1972), recalling von Neumann’s address at the first national ACM meeting in 1947.
