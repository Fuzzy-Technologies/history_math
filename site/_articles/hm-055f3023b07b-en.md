A Russian and Soviet mathematics educator and specialist in teaching methods. He was also a writer, historian of science and professor.

He was born on 27 December 1879 in Zhytomyr into a Polish noble family.

He studied in the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at St Petersburg University in 1897–1904.

## Teaching and Research

- In 1905–1912 he taught mathematics and physics at a Realschule and a gymnasium.
- In 1912–1918 he taught higher mathematics on polytechnic courses.
- In 1918–1923 he taught on higher cavalry courses.
- In 1920–1930 he was a professor in the Department of Technical Mathematics of the Higher Pedagogical Courses.
- From 1930 he was a senior researcher at the P. F. Lesgaft Research Institute.
- From 1920 he taught courses entitled “The History of Schools and Educational Systems” and “The History and Methodology of the Exact Sciences”.
- In the 1930s he lectured on the history of technology, mathematics teaching methods and technical mathematics at various higher technical institutions in Leningrad and at the A. I. Herzen Pedagogical Institute.
- In 1930–1937 he was on the staff of the Department of Applied Astronomy at the P. F. Lesgaft Scientific Institute, carrying out historical research commissioned by N. A. Morozov.
- In 1930–1931 he served on the presidium of the Society of Materialist Mathematicians at the Communist Academy.
- In 1931 he organized and headed a section on the Marxist history of technology at the House of Engineering and Technical Workers.
- In 1934 he was appointed deputy director for education at the newly established University of the History of Science and Technology.
- In 1933–1934 he was deputy chairman of the Academy of Sciences’ commission on technical mathematics.

In 1937 he was arrested, convicted and executed by shooting in Leningrad.

## Publications

His publications include the following (Russian titles translated here):

- *Plane Trigonometry and the Elements of the Theory of Goniometric Functions* (1908, 1913).
- *Pedagogy of Mathematics* (1910).
- *Arithmetic Past and Present* (1912).
- *Three Periods of School Physics* (1913).
- *School Mathematics Rooms* (1913).
- *Materials on the Reform of Vocational School* (1924).
- *Training Technical Educators*.
- *Technical Mathematics* (1931).
- *The Emergence and Development of the Theory of Probability* (1934).

## Notes

Mrochek was arrested in 1901 and 1903 for taking part in student strikes. He belonged to the Socialist Revolutionary Party from 1904 to 1917 and to the Bolshevik Party from 1918 to 1924.

Mrochek was one of N. A. Morozov’s closest colleagues at the P. F. Lesgaft Institute and an active assistant in his research into the history of science.

Nikolai Alexandrovich Morozov (1854–1946) was active in the Russian revolutionary movement as a Narodnik and a member of Narodnaya Volya, or People’s Will. Altogether he spent about thirty years in tsarist prisons. He was a science popularizer, writer, memoirist and poet. From 1918 he directed the P. F. Lesgaft State Institute of Natural Science. In 1932 he became an honorary member of the USSR Academy of Sciences.
