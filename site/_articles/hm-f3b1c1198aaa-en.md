*Marking the 810th anniversary of his birth*

> “Mathematics is the gate and key of the sciences.”

Roger Bacon was an English philosopher and scholar of nature who worked on physics, mathematics and invention. He was also a writer, music theorist, theologian, astrologer and alchemist, and a professor of theology at Oxford.

## Education and Teaching

He was born in 1214 in Ilchester, Somerset, England. He was educated at Oxford and Paris universities and received his Master of Arts degree in 1241.

Until 1247 he taught at the University of Paris, lecturing on Aristotle in the Faculty of Arts. During his time in Paris, Bacon became known for his disputes with the scholastics. He also received a doctorate in philosophy and earned the honorary title *Doctor Mirabilis*, “the Wonderful Doctor”.

Bacon did not create a fundamentally new philosophical doctrine, but he offered a reasoned critique of the methods and theories of his time. He was the first to oppose scholasticism.

In 1250 Bacon returned to Oxford, where he taught at the university.

## The Order and the Treatises

He joined the Franciscan order in 1257. His reputation soon spread through Oxford.

Suspected of practising black magic and departing from Catholic dogma, he was banned from lecturing at Oxford and placed under the order’s supervision in a Franciscan monastery in Paris.

Only the patronage of Pope Clement IV enabled Bacon to publish three major treatises: *Opus majus* (“The Major Work”), *Opus minus* (“The Minor Work”) and *Opus tertium* (“The Third Work”). A separate treatise, *De multiplicatione specierum*, which reflects Bacon’s views on natural philosophy, was also sent to the pope as an appendix to *Opus majus* and *Opus minus*.

In 1268 Bacon received permission to return to Oxford, where he continued his scientific studies.

In the late 1260s he completed *Communia mathematica* (“General Principles of Mathematics”), *Communia naturalium* and, later, *Compendium studii philosophiae*. The first two treatises set out and examine the most general and fundamental questions of mathematics and physics respectively.

His criticism of the clergy’s lax morals led to persecution by ecclesiastical authorities and twelve years’ imprisonment, beginning in 1278.

## Mathematics, Experience and Natural Science

Throughout his life he studied mathematics, chemistry and physics. He regarded mathematics as the most certain and unquestionable science, one that could be used to test the findings of every other science. He also maintained that mathematics was the easiest science and accessible to everyone.

Bacon worked actively on alchemy, astrology and optics. In optics he developed new theories of magnifying glasses, the refraction of light, perspective and the apparent size of objects.

He is known for a commitment to empirical knowledge unusual in medieval philosophy. Although familiar with Aristotle’s writings, Bacon did not accord logic a particularly high place. He gave precedence to mathematics, which he considered innate to the human mind. Mathematical study without experimental illustrations does not provide proper satisfaction.

Mathematical knowledge could help even theologians, for example in determining the dates of movable feasts or correctly interpreting passages of Scripture that mention numbers, geometric figures or musical instruments.

Several of Bacon’s ideas on applying mathematical principles to natural philosophy and other sciences played an important part in the development of scientific knowledge.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Famous quotations from Roger Bacon:

- “A person who does not know mathematics is incapable of any other science.”

- “There are four great obstacles to understanding the truth: the example of a weak and unworthy authority, the persistence of habit, the opinion of the ignorant crowd, and the concealment of one’s own ignorance through a display of wisdom.”

- “Experimental science is the mistress of the speculative sciences.”

- “Mathematics is the gate and key of the sciences. ... Neglect of mathematics works injury to all knowledge, since he who is ignorant of it cannot know the other sciences or the things of this world. And what is worse, men who are thus Ignorant are unable to perceive their own ignorance and so do not seek a remedy”.

Roger Bacon devoted all his energy and substantial sums of money to scientific experiments, acquired old manuscripts and created new instruments and equipment. He was the first European to describe the manufacture of gunpowder in detail (1242) and predict its future military use. He described the use of spectacles and used a camera obscura to study a solar eclipse. His scientific achievements were known throughout Europe and even earned him a reputation as a miracle-worker.
