You may already have enjoyed the intricate patterns in pictures by the celebrated Dutch artist Maurits Escher (1898–1972). Many of his pictures show figures covering the entire plane of the drawing according to interesting rules. Such coverings are called tessellations. Think about the mathematical laws that govern them. Create your own “little theory” of tessellations.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

(1) We are given a set of basic statements:

• (a) When polygons fill the plane, any two polygons either share a whole side, share a vertex, or have no points in common.

• (b) A regular tessellation consists of congruent regular polygons.

• (c) The plane can be tiled with congruent equilateral triangles.

• (d) The plane can be tiled with congruent squares.

(2) Statements to investigate:

• (a) There is a regular tessellation made of hexagons.

• (b) There are finitely many ways to tile the plane with regular polygons.

• (c) Not every regular polygon can form a tessellation.

• (d) For every quadrilateral there is a tessellation consisting of quadrilaterals congruent to it: a quadrilateral of arbitrary shape can fill the entire plane.

## Tasks {#section-1}

1\. Can you identify foundational statements among the basic statements from which the others, or at least some of them, follow? If so, use your intuition to select a minimal set of foundational statements.

2\. Prove some of the statements under investigation using your chosen minimal set of foundational statements.

3\. Using reference works or books on the history of mathematics, determine when, by whom and how the mathematical object under consideration — a “theory of tessellations” — was introduced. Who formulated and proved the statements?

## Answers and historical background {#section-2}

As is customary in a mathematical theory, define “tessellation,” “regular tessellation” and “semiregular tessellation.” What other concepts will you need to present such a theory?

We will give answers straight away so that you can see how this is done.

An elementary theory of tessellations is presented in various books, for example: Smirnova, I. M., and V. A. Smirnov. *Polygons: An Elective Course for Grade 9. A Study Guide for General Education Schools*. Moscow: Mnemozina, 2007. 64 pp. [In Russian.]

Tessellations have attracted people's attention since ancient times. The earliest theoretical knowledge of them is known to have developed among the Pythagoreans. They realized that only three regular polygons could each cover the plane without gaps or overlaps: triangles, squares and hexagons. From then on, tessellations became a subject for mathematicians as well as artists. Russian scholars A. D. Alexandrov, B. N. Delaunay, E. S. Fedorov and A. N. Kolmogorov did much to develop their theory.

1\. A tessellation, or tiling, is a partition of the plane into polygons without gaps or overlaps. Tessellations made from identical regular polygons are called regular tessellations. Tessellations consisting of two or more types of regular polygon, such that for any two vertices there is a symmetry of the tessellation taking one to the other, are called semiregular tessellations.

2\. There are three regular tessellations of the plane: triangular, square and hexagonal.
