*Marking the 120th anniversary of his birth*

Boris Pavlovich Demidovich (1906–1977) was a Soviet mathematician and university educator specializing in ordinary differential equations, functional analysis and mathematical physics. He wrote the celebrated mathematical analysis problem book that became almost synonymous with the subject for generations of students.

## University and Research

He was born on 2 March 1906 in Novogrudok, then in the Russian Empire, now in Belarus. After graduating from Belarusian State University in 1927, he taught school mathematics for several years. In the early 1930s he moved to Moscow and made Moscow University the centre of his research and teaching. He received his Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1936 and doctorate in 1963, became a professor in 1965 and was named an Honoured Worker in Science and Technology of the RSFSR in 1968.

In 1963 he defended his doctoral dissertation, “Bounded Solutions of Differential Equations”, at Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics.

His research grew out of the Moscow school of qualitative differential equations. He examined questions essential to realistic models: what happens to a solution over a long time? Does it remain bounded or stable? Does a periodic regime emerge? Can self-sustained oscillations occur? These questions underpin applications in mechanics, physics and engineering.

## The Problem Book and Teaching

To a wider readership, however, he is known above all for his problem book. *Problems and Exercises in Mathematical Analysis* first appeared in 1952. Its more than four thousand problems cover the principal areas of analysis and guide students from their first skills towards confident calculation and understanding. It is no book for passive reading: it provides a real intellectual workout, patient, sometimes demanding and thoroughly honest.

His name is associated with other textbooks and courses too. For many years he lectured and taught practical classes at Moscow State University and other leading Moscow institutions, wrote teaching materials and popular articles and edited translations and educational publications.

The books and subjects most often associated with his name include:

- *Problems and Exercises in Mathematical Analysis*, repeatedly reissued since 1952;
- research in qualitative ordinary differential equations: stability, periodic and almost-periodic solutions, and boundedness;
- courses in mathematical physics and general higher mathematics, including co-authored works.

Scientific results live on in papers and lectures, while a teacher’s legacy lives in habits of thought. Many engineers and mathematicians can sum up part of their education by saying that they learned from Demidovich—and behind those words stands an entire school.
