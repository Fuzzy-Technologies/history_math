*Marking the 150th anniversary of his birth*

A well-known Russian and Soviet mathematics educator of the first half of the twentieth century. He wrote school textbooks on arithmetic, algebra, geometry and trigonometry.

## Education and Moscow Schools

He was born on 6 July Old Style, 18 July New Style, 1874 in Karkaralinsk, in the Semipalatinsk region of Kazakhstan.

He graduated from Tomsk Gymnasium in 1892 and from the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at Moscow University in 1899.

He taught mathematics at Moscow secondary schools with science and practical curricula: the French Real School from 1899 to 1908, then Voskresensky’s Real School from 1909 to 1919. The latter had been founded by the distinguished Russian educator K. P. Voskresensky.

During this period he began developing his own teaching methods, joined the Moscow Mathematical Society and wrote his first textbooks: *A Short Course in Arithmetic* (1903) and *Foundations of Analytic Geometry* (1907). These books had clear strengths and fully matched the curriculum of the time. Teachers rated them highly, and they were reprinted several times.

K. N. Rashevsky wrote eight textbooks on elementary mathematics and the beginnings of higher mathematics. He also took part in the First and Second All-Russian Congresses of Mathematics Teachers, held in St Petersburg in 1911–1912 and Moscow in 1913–1914.

## Work in Ranenburg and New Editions

After the October Revolution of 1917, the real schools were abolished. By then an established mathematics educator, he moved with his family to Ranenburg in 1919. He taught mathematics and headed a department at the Ranenburg Institute of Public Education, which trained teachers for the Soviet Unified Labour School. In 1921 the institute became the Ranenburg Teacher Training College. The town was called Ranenburg until 1948; it is now Chaplygin in Lipetsk Oblast.

In the 1920s K. N. Rashevsky revised his mathematics textbooks for the new Soviet curriculum and republished *A Short Course in Arithmetic*, *Elementary Geometry* and *Elementary Algebra*. The first edition of his eighth textbook, *Trigonometry*, appeared in 1929. In the 1920s and 1930s his textbooks were very popular in the Soviet educational system and were as widely used as those of A. P. Kiselyov.

Konstantin Nikolaevich’s teaching career comprised fifty-six years of uninterrupted work in educational institutions in Moscow and Chaplygin.

In 1945 K. N. Rashevsky was awarded the Medal “For Valiant Labour in the Great Patriotic War 1941–1945” for his contribution to teacher training. In 1947 he received the title of Honoured Teacher of the RSFSR.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

K. N. Rashevsky wrote eight textbooks on elementary mathematics and the beginnings of higher mathematics:

- *A Short Course in Arithmetic for Secondary Schools*. Moscow, 1903.
- *Foundations of Analytic Geometry: A Textbook for the Additional Class of Real Schools*. Moscow, 1908.
- *Elementary Geometry: A Secondary School Course*. Moscow, 1909 (*Elementary Geometry and Methods for Solving Construction Problems*, Moscow, 1917).
- *A Short Course in Geometry: A Guide for Municipal Schools Established under the 1872 Regulations, Girls’ Gymnasia, Institutes and Other Schools*. Moscow, 1910.
- *Elementary Algebra: A Secondary School Course*. Moscow, 1911; second edition, 1913.
- *Foundations of Infinitesimal Analysis: A Textbook for the Seventh Class of Real Schools, Prepared in Accordance with the Ministry of Public Education Curriculum*. Moscow, 1913.
- *Trigonometry*. 1929.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

K. N. Rashevsky’s first wife was Alexandra Vasilyevna Kirina. Their first son, Pyotr Konstantinovich Rashevsky (1907–1983), held a Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree and headed the Department of Differential Geometry in the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics at Moscow State University. K. N. Rashevsky’s second wife was Sofya Semyonovna Kogan.
