When the French mathematician Charles Bossut (1730–1814) fell ill, his friends gathered at his bedside. Charles was so weak that he did not answer their questions. “He’s stopped breathing,” someone said. “Wait,” another interrupted. “Let me ask him something. Bossut, what is twelve squared?” “One hundred and forty-four,” came his whispered reply.
