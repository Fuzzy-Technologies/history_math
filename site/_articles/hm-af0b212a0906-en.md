Here is a problem from I. Ya. Depman’s *Rasskazy o reshenii zadach* (Stories about Solving Problems). It makes a good mental warm-up for children, in class or at home.

## The problem

Anya, Varya and Klava went to a demonstration, one in a red dress, another in white and the third in blue. Asked which colour each girl was wearing, they answered:

- “Anya is in red.”
- “Varya is not in red.”
- “Klava is not in blue.”

Only one of the three statements is true. What colour was each girl’s dress? Try answering for yourself.

## Answer and solution

Only “Klava is not in blue” is true; the other two statements are false. So Varya is in red, Klava in white and Anya in blue.

How do we reach that conclusion? Suppose Anya is in red and the first statement is true. Then “Varya is not in red” is true too, but exactly one statement is allowed to be true. Therefore Anya cannot be in red.

Suppose the second statement, “Varya is not in red”, is true. Then Klava is in blue and Anya is not in red. Nobody is in red, which is impossible.

Suppose Klava is not in blue, but in white. Anya is then not in red, but in blue, and Varya is in red.

## Notes

I. Ya. Depman, *Rasskazy o reshenii zadach* (Stories about Solving Problems), Leningrad: Detgiz, 1957, 128 pp. (in Russian).
