*Marking the 135th anniversary of his birth*

Nikolai Mikhailovich Nestorovich (24 September 1891–21 December 1955) was a Soviet mathematician and educator, and a professor at Rostov State University. He worked on constructive methods in geometry and on Lobachevskian geometry, explaining difficult subjects in a way that kept the reasoning behind a construction visible through the formulas.

## Warsaw and Rostov

He was born to a village schoolteacher in Verkhlesye, in the Slonim district of Grodno province, within the Lithuanian Governorate-General of the Russian Empire. In 1910 he entered Warsaw University, studying under D. D. Mordukhai-Boltovskoi and A. R. Kolli. That mathematical school gave him an unusual combination of rigour and interest in construction problems defined by the available instruments: the task was not merely to prove, but to construct.

After graduating, he taught mathematics and physics in secondary schools for several years, including in Novohrad-Volynskyi. In 1917 he defended a thesis on the solvability of construction problems using compass and straightedge and other instruments, a subject that would become his hallmark for decades.

From January 1918 his research and teaching were tied to Rostov-on-Don. He was invited to Don University, by then relocated to Rostov, to prepare for a professorial career. He nevertheless continued school teaching for a long time: Nestorovich regarded teaching as a craft that could not simply be delegated.

## University Work and Evacuation

His university career took him from assistant and senior assistant to docent and professor. In 1931–1935 he headed higher mathematics at the Rostov Automobile and Road Institute; from 1935 he headed geometry at Rostov University. During the Great Patriotic War he was evacuated to Osh, where he helped organize the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics and served as dean and vice-rector.

In 1936 he defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, *Geometrical Constructions in Lobachevsky Space*, followed by his doctoral dissertation in 1951.

## Constructions in Lobachevskian Geometry

His main field was constructive geometry in a non-Euclidean world. Nestorovich studied which constructions were possible in Lobachevsky’s plane and space, which sets of instruments were equivalent to the classical compass and straightedge, and how the very idea of construction changed where familiar Euclidean intuition repeatedly failed. He also considered classical problems such as squaring the circle, showing that in Lobachevskian geometry they were solvable in infinitely many cases.

His best-known book, *Geometrical Constructions in the Lobachevsky Plane* (1951), is a rigorous account of hyperbolic geometry accompanied by computation and construction problems. Related works include *Geometrical Constructions in Lobachevsky Space* and studies of twin figures, improper triangles and universal trigonometric formulas in Lobachevsky and Riemann spaces.

## Teaching and Legacy

Nestorovich was also a teacher across a wide range of subjects, from analytic and differential geometry to numerical methods and higher mathematics for physicists and chemists. He developed an original course on the foundations of geometry, published work on teaching methods and advised many schoolteachers in Rostov-on-Don. He served on the editorial boards of the *Herald of Experimental Physics and Elementary Mathematics*, *Mathematical Education* and *Mathematics in School*.

His work brought official recognition: the Order of Lenin, certificates of honour, including for his work during evacuation, the Excellence in Public Education badge, and the Medal for Valiant Labour in the Great Patriotic War. Yet his role can perhaps be described more precisely in another way: he helped Lobachevsky’s ideas live in mathematical practice rather than only in historical surveys, through problems, geometrical constructions and clear teaching.
