*Marking the 200th anniversary of his birth*

He was born on 7 December 1823 in Liegnitz, Germany, now Legnica, Poland.

A German mathematician, he became an extraordinary professor at the University of Berlin in 1883. He belonged to the Berlin and Paris academies of sciences, was a foreign corresponding member of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences and a foreign member of the Royal Society of London.

From 1841 to 1845 he studied mathematics, physics and philosophy in Berlin, Bonn and Breslau, receiving his doctorate in Berlin in 1845. His teachers included Ernst Kummer and Lejeune Dirichlet.

He became an advocate of the arithmetization of mathematics, believing that arithmetic alone possessed true reality. A celebrated remark is attributed to him: “God created the integers; all the rest is the work of human beings.” His philosophical outlook prompted prolonged disputes, notably with advocates of Weierstrass’s function theory and Cantor’s set theory.

Kronecker’s principal works concern algebra and number theory. His name appears in the Kronecker–Capelli theorem on the consistency of a system of linear equations. He also gave a method for factoring a polynomial over the field of rational numbers.

The Kronecker delta is frequently used in mathematics as an equality indicator for two indices: it is one when they are equal and zero otherwise. It is used in matrix and tensor calculus.

By applying elliptic functions, Kronecker obtained several new results in number theory.

His ideas found a partial continuation in twentieth-century research on the foundations of mathematics, in constructive mathematics.
