One day al-Khwarizmi became so absorbed in solving equations that he forgot about lunch. Annoyed by his absence, his wife brought his food straight to his study. Seeing strange symbols, figures and squiggles on his paper, she exclaimed: “What is all this nonsense?!” Without looking up from his work, the scholar replied: “It’s not nonsense—it’s al-jabr!” The word means “restoration”: moving subtracted terms to the other side of an equation as added terms. That, according to the legend, is how the word “algebra” came about.

Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (787–850), a Persian mathematician, is known as the “father of algebra”. In his book *Hisab al-jabr wa-l-muqabala* (*The Calculation of Restoration and Balancing*), he discussed the solution of equations. The work became known in Latin translation, and “al-jabr” came to be used as a name for the science of algebra.
