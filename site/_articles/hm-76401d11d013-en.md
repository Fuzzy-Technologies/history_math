John Forbes Nash (1928–2015) was an American mathematician whose fundamental work spanned game theory, differential geometry and partial differential equations. In 1994, together with John Harsanyi and Reinhard Selten, he received the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel “for their pioneering analysis of equilibria in the theory of non-cooperative games”.

Once, at a university reception, Nash noticed that all the men were wearing ties and immediately proposed a “tie game”: each person should mentally choose someone else’s tie so as to maximise their own “utility”.

According to a humorous legend, he chose the brightest, most eccentric tie in the room himself and, adjusting it, said:

“This isn’t just a tie. It’s a Nash equilibrium in action.”

Nash’s life, with its scientific triumphs and personal dramas, became the basis of Sylvia Nasar’s book *A Beautiful Mind* and the Oscar-winning film of the same name.
