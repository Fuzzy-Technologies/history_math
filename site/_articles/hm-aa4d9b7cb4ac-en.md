$e\approx2.71828$ is a mathematical constant, the base of the natural logarithm. It is irrational and transcendental, as proved by Charles Hermite in 1873; the result also follows from Lindemann’s theorem. The number plays an important role in differential and integral calculus and many other branches of mathematics. It is sometimes called Euler’s number or Napier’s constant.

While studying compound interest, the Swiss mathematician Jacob Bernoulli considered the following limit. If the initial principal is 1, the annual rate is 100% and interest is compounded $n$ times a year in equal instalments, the final balance is $(1+1/n)^n$. As $n\to\infty$, the balance approaches $e$, while the interest earned approaches $e-1$.

One mnemonic for the opening digits of $e$ is this Russian verse:

Экспоненту помнить<br>
Способ есть простой:<br>
Два и семь десятых,<br>
Дважды Лев Толстой!

Its literal meaning is: “To remember the exponential constant, there is a simple way: two and seven tenths, Leo Tolstoy twice.”

The verse gives the first nine decimal places: start with 2.7, then repeat Leo Tolstoy’s birth year, 1828, twice. Next come the angles of an isosceles right triangle—45, 90 and 45 degrees—followed by the first three primes, 2, 3 and 5, and the 360 degrees in a full turn. This gives 21 decimal places: $e\approx2.718281828459045235360$.
