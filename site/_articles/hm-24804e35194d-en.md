A problem from the ancient Chinese treatise *The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art*.

A tree is 2 zhang high and 3 chi in circumference ($1\,\mathrm{zhang}=10\,\mathrm{chi}$). A vine grows at its foot, winding uniformly seven times around the tree on its way to the top. How long is the vine?

Assume that the tree is a right circular cylinder.

## Solution

Answer: 29 chi.

### Hint

Roll the tree along a horizontal plane so that the vine straightens out into the diagonal of a rectangle with sides 20 chi and 21 chi.

## Notes

P.S. Modern units of length: $1\,\mathrm{chi}=33\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$ (a Chinese foot), and $1\,\mathrm{zhang}=10\,\mathrm{chi}=3\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$.

### Historical background

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

*The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art* (Chinese pinyin: jiǔ zhāng suànshù) is an encyclopaedia of ancient Chinese mathematical knowledge. It brings together early works by different authors, written between the tenth and second centuries BC. It was given its final form by the financial official Zhang Cang in the second century BC.

The treatise contains 246 problems, presented in the traditional Eastern manner: a problem is stated, its answer supplied, and the method of solution indicated very briefly. The early core text has no extended proofs, diagrams or methodological explanations. Most of the problems are practical. The treatise is usually published with the commentary by Liu Hui from AD 263.
