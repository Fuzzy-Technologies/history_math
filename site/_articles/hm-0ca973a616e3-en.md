*Marking the 120th anniversary of his birth*

An outstanding mathematician and geometer, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences and professor.

He was born in Saratov on 24 July 1904.

He studied in the mathematics division of Moscow University from 1926 to 1930 and then undertook postgraduate study at the university’s Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics. His teachers were Sergei Finikov and Veniamin Kagan. In 1932 Norden defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “Relative Geometry of Surfaces in Projective Space”; in 1937 he defended his doctoral dissertation, “On the Intrinsic Geometries of Surfaces in Projective Space”.

He taught at Moscow State University and other higher education institutions in Moscow, becoming a professor in 1937. In 1941–1945 he headed the Department of Mathematics at the Novosibirsk Institute of Military Railway Engineers.

From September 1945, he led the Department of Geometry at Kazan University for thirty-five years.

His principal works concern differential geometry. He devoted considerable attention to teaching geometry at university and created courses in the foundations of geometry and differential geometry.

His major books include *Differential Geometry* (1948), *Spaces with an Affine Connection* (1950) and *The Theory of Surfaces* (1956). His *An Elementary Introduction to Lobachevsky’s Geometry* (1953) is of particular interest to teachers.

From 1950 he led the Kazan Physics and Mathematics Society. He played a key role in establishing *Izvestiya Vysshikh Uchebnykh Zavedenii. Matematika* and directed its work for more than twenty years.

He did much to develop and popularize the ideas of the great Nikolai Lobachevsky. Lobachevsky’s works were published under his editorship and with his commentaries.

He held the titles of Honoured Scientist of the Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (1954) and the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic (1964), and received the Lobachevsky Medal (1992).

P.S. Alexander Norden had a remarkable gift for teaching. Generations of students remember his lectures in the general course of differential geometry: concise, and leaving clear geometrical images in the mind. I am grateful for the chance to have met him during my student years (1967–1975).
