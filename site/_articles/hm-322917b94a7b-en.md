*Marking the 105th anniversary of his birth*

A well-known Belarusian Soviet educator and specialist in teaching methods, Doctor of Pedagogical Sciences from 1970 and professor. He held the title of Honoured Worker of Higher Education of the Byelorussian SSR.

## Education and Teaching

He was born on 20 February 1919 in the colony of Tarutino in southern Bessarabia, annexed by Romania, now a settlement in Ukraine’s Odesa Region.

After completing a boys’ lyceum, he entered the Higher Polytechnic School in Bucharest. When Bessarabia became part of the USSR in 1940, he interrupted his studies. At the outbreak of war he was evacuated with his parents to Central Asia and began teaching mathematics in a secondary school. He continued his education through correspondence study at the Bukhara Pedagogical Institute, graduating in 1947.

From 1948 he taught logic at the Saratov Naval School. In 1951 he defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation, “Developing Pupils’ Logical Thinking in Mathematics Lessons”.

From 1950 he taught mathematics at a secondary school in Mogilev. He then joined the Mogilev State Pedagogical Institute, now the Kuleshov Mogilev State University, serving as a department head and pro-rector for teaching.

## The Logic of Teaching and the Laboratory

From the very beginning of students’ studies in the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, Professor Stolyar taught a course he called a logical introduction to mathematics. He believed that teachers should understand the principles governing mathematics’ internal structure and development, reason correctly and teach pupils to do the same.

He defended his doctoral dissertation, “Logical Problems of Mathematics Teaching”, in 1970. In 1979 he took charge of the research laboratory he had established at the Institute for the learning and intellectual development of preschool and younger school-age children. The first mathematics textbook for kindergarten preparatory classes, *Mathematics 0*, and a primary-school mathematics curriculum were developed there. The complete sets of primary-school mathematics textbooks edited by Stolyar have appeared in many editions and continue to be published.

During Stolyar’s time, the Mogilev Pedagogical Institute hosted All-Union conferences on mathematics and its teaching. To put his scientific ideas into practice, he established postgraduate study. A council was also established for the defence of Candidate of Sciences dissertations on the theory and methods of mathematics teaching.

In systematizing his research, he arrived at the pedagogy of mathematics, doing so for the first time, at least in the Soviet Union. His fundamental work *The Pedagogy of Mathematics* provides evidence of this.

In 1992 he moved to Moscow, working at a pedagogical institute during the final year of his life.

Professor Stolyar was a figure of great stature: a scholar and educator of international renown!

## Notes

He wrote books and teaching guides, including the following Russian editions; the titles are translated here:

- *Elements of Mathematical Logic* (1964);
- *Logical Problems of Mathematics Teaching* (1965);
- *An Elementary Introduction to Mathematical Logic* (1965);
- *A Logical Introduction to Mathematics* (1971);
- *Foundations of Modern School Mathematics*, with N. M. Roganovsky (1975);
- *Modern Foundations of the School Mathematics Course*, with coauthors (1980);
- *Methods of Teaching Mathematics: Some Questions of Theory and Practice*, with coauthors (1981);
- *How Mathematics Sets the Mind in Order* (1981);
- *Teaching Notes for the Textbook Mathematics 0*, with coauthors (1982);
- *Teaching Mathematics in Secondary School: General Methods*, with coauthors (1985);
- *How Many Sides Does a Surface Have? Conversations with Senior School Pupils*, with E. V. Korobyonok (1985);
- *The Pedagogy of Mathematics: A Textbook for Pedagogical Institutes* (1986);
- *Why and How We Prove Things in Mathematics: Conversations with Senior School Pupils* (1987);
- *Methods of Initial Mathematics Teaching*, with coauthors (1988);
- *What Is an Algorithm? Conversations with Senior School Pupils*, with Yu. A. Makarenkov (1989);
- *Let Us Play: Mathematical Games for Children Aged 5–6* (1991);
- *Theories and Technologies of Mathematical Development for Preschool Children*, as coauthor (2008).
