Jordanus Nemorarius was a thirteenth-century mathematician and scholar of mechanics. [Little is known about his life](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Jordanus/). His *On the Elements of the Art of Arithmetic* (*De elementis arismetice artis*) long served as a principal arithmetic textbook in medieval schools.

His work *On Weights* (*De ponderibus*) is particularly well known. In it he was among the first to describe mathematically the equilibrium of a lever and an inclined plane, long before Galileo and Newton.

According to a humorous legend, Jordanus decided to create a “library in the air”: he suspended his books on ropes so that they would always be within reach. When one rope broke, scattering the books around the room with a crash, he exclaimed:

“Even books refuse to obey the laws of mechanics!”

That was the first collision of theory and gravity in his cell.
