This article concerns dividing indivisible objects, or objects whose value is lost if they are broken up: houses, cars and equipment. Such problems commonly arise when dividing an inheritance.

Indivisible goods require a different rule. A collection of them can still be divided fairly, and uniquely, but only through an agreed valuation procedure.

## Example {#section-1}

Suppose four brothers must divide an inheritance comprising three items: a television, a motorcycle and a country cottage. How should they divide them?

**Step 1.** Each brother independently estimates the monetary value of all three items. Enter the data into a simple table listing the items, valuations and valuers:

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Heirs 1 and 4 value their respective shares at 2375 rubles, heir 3 at 1875 rubles, and heir 2 at 2500 rubles. Mark the largest valuations in each table row with an asterisk (\*).

**Step 2.** Divide the items among the brothers. Each receives the item he valued more highly than the others: brother 1 receives the television, brother 2 the motorcycle, brother 4 the cottage, and brother 3 nothing. Enter these results in another table, indicating for each heir the number and total actual value of the items received, the share due according to his own valuation, and the underpayment or overpayment.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

**Step 3.** Make balancing payments for the items received and distribute the money among the brothers who received less than their entitlement. Brothers 2 and 4 must pay in 6125 rubles (2500 + 3625). Brother 1 receives 875 rubles and brother 3 receives 1875 rubles, a total of 2750 rubles. The remaining 3375 rubles (6125 – 2750) are divided equally among all four, so each receives another 843 rubles and 75 kopecks.

**The result:**

The first brother receives a television valued at 1500 rubles and 1718.75 rubles in cash (875 + 843.75).  
The second receives a motorcycle valued at 5000 rubles and pays 1656.25 rubles in cash (2500 – 843.75).  
The third receives 2718.75 rubles in cash (1875 + 843.75).  
The fourth receives a cottage valued at 6000 rubles and pays 2781.25 rubles in cash (3625 – 843.75).

After the goods are allocated and all payments settled, each heir has received 843.25 rubles more than his own valuation of his share.
