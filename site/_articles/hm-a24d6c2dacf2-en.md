At an exam, a student argues furiously that mathematics contains a vitally important concept: the “krugloyed”.

The examiner is aghast:

“Show me where I first introduced it in my lectures!”

After some investigation, it emerges that he had copied the notes of a top student. Beside the first mention of a partial derivative, she had written a reminder to herself: “not a straight $d$, but a round $\partial$!”

And so a single note brought a new creature into being: the kruglo-yed, the round-eater!

P. S. In mathematical analysis, an ordinary derivative is commonly written with $d$, while a partial derivative uses the distinct symbol $\partial$ — the “round d” behind the joke.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Source of the joke. The Russian phrase *krugloye d* (“round d”) runs together as *krugloyed*, which sounds like the name of a creature that eats round things.

- S. N. Fedin, [*Mathematicians Joke Too*](https://www.docdroid.net/ytC3FTI/fedin-sn-matematiki-toze-sutiat-pdf.pdf) (in Russian), no. 44, “KRUGLOYED”.
