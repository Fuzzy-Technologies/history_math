Mathematical tricks receive little attention from either mathematicians or magicians. Mathematicians dismiss them as idle amusement, while magicians neglect them as too dull and insufficiently impressive.

The well-known Soviet science popularizer Yakov Isidorovich Perelman disagreed. He wrote many books on mathematical recreation for children. In *Entertaining Problems and Experiments*, he offered ideas for numerous mathematical tricks. Here is one scenario based on his “Amazing Memory” trick.

## How to perform the trick {#section-1}

Prepare 50 cards — or as many as you wish — with numbers and letters written on them, for example as shown in the table.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Each card has a letter and digit in its upper-left corner, forming the card’s number, and a multidigit number in its lower-right corner, which the performer must guess.

Hand the cards to the players.

The performer announces that as soon as a player names a card’s number, they will state the number written on it, as though remembering it. There are many large numbers, so the performer will astonish the players. There is no need to memorize all these numbers; the method is much simpler. What is the secret, and what mathematics does it use?

**The explanation**

The operations are encoded as +, 2, –, ×: add, double, subtract and multiply. Letters are encoded as A = 2, B = 3, C = 4, D = 5, E = 6. Thus the card corners encode two-digit numbers. All subsequent operations use the card’s code.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

Take card Е2 as an example.

Find its code: Е2 = 62.

Then carry out the operations:

+   add the digits: 6 + 2 = 8

2   double the number: 62 × 2 = 124

–   subtract the smaller digit from the larger: 6 – 2 = 4

×   multiply the digits: 6 × 2 = 12

Write the results consecutively: 8124412. To make the task easier, you can say the number’s digits as you calculate them.

You can invent other encodings or combinations of operations yourself.

## Historical background {#section-2}

> Yakov Isidorovich Perelman (1882–1942) was a Russian and Soviet mathematician, physicist, journalist and educator.  
> He popularized the exact sciences, founded the genre of recreational science, and coined the concept “science-fictional”.  
> He was a founder and scientific director of the House of Entertaining Science in Leningrad (1935–1941), preparing its physics, mathematics and astronomy departments.  
> He wrote 47 popular-science books, 40 educational science books and 18 school textbooks.  
> His books were published 126 times in 18 countries.

> On 16 March 1942, Yakov Perelman died of general exhaustion caused by hunger in Leningrad, besieged by Nazi German forces.

> This “unschooled scholar” introduced millions to knowledge. Many people wrote popular-science books before and after him: popular, but sadly not entertaining. Perelman had the rare gift of wonder himself, and knew how to awaken it in others. That is why his books and his memory have endured for so many years.
