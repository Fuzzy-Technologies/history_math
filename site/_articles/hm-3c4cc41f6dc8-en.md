S. N. Fedin’s collection contains a tale about a mathematics professor named Kondratyev who once arrived to give a lecture very drunk, yet went to the board as if nothing were amiss and began proving a rather cumbersome theorem.

His body, however, decided that the theorem could wait.

The professor slowly began to slide to the floor, gradually losing consciousness, while his hand kept a desperate grip on the chalk. The chalk, like a model student, kept its composure and traced a winding line across the board, leaving the class in no doubt: it was a remarkable curve!

After the lecture, the students dubbed it “Kondratyev’s curve”.

## Notes

Further information and the source of the joke:

- S. N. Fedin, [*Mathematicians Joke Too*](https://www.docdroid.net/ytC3FTI/fedin-sn-matematiki-toze-sutiat-pdf.pdf) (in Russian), no. 43, “A Remarkable Curve” (PDF).

- Fedin’s anecdote gives only the surname Kondratyev, without a first name or university, so the protagonist cannot be reliably identified. It may, however, refer to [Vladimir Aleksandrovich Kondratyev](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кондратьев,_Владимир_Александрович), a Soviet and Russian mathematician, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences (1965), professor (1970) and Distinguished Professor of Moscow State University (2002).
