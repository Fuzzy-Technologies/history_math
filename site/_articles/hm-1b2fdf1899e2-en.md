Zu Chongzhi (429–500) was a Chinese mathematician and astronomer. By repeatedly doubling the number of sides of a polygon to approximate a circle, following Liu Hui’s method, he calculated the bounds $3.1415926 < \pi < 3.1415927$. He also gave two celebrated approximations: the “rough” $\pi \approx 22/7$ (Chinese: 约率; pinyin: yuēlǜ) and the “accurate” $\pi \approx 355/113$ (Chinese: 密率; pinyin: mìlǜ, Zu’s term for the closer approximation to π).

This was a record for almost nine centuries, correct to six decimal places. Put simply, $355/113$ was ancient Chinese π in “Pro mode” 😎

A quick accuracy check:

$$\left|\pi - \frac{355}{113}\right| \approx 2.667 \times 10^{-7}$$

Together with his son Zu Gengzhi, he wrote down the formula for the volume of a sphere, $V = \pi d^3/6$, where $d$ is its diameter. In astronomy, Zu proposed the new Daming calendar.

“…I personally checked measures and instruments, scrutinised the smallest details and exhausted my calculations—I venture to create a new calendar.”<br>
(Zu Chongzhi, from his petition for calendar reform; *Book of the Southern Qi*, juan 52, section 祖沖之, translated from Chinese.)
