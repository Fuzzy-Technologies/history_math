*Marking the 160th anniversary of his birth*

A German mathematician born in the Russian Empire, Minkowski created the geometry of numbers and a four-dimensional geometric formulation of special relativity. He was a member of the celebrated Göttingen school of mathematics.

## Education and Teaching

He was born on 22 June 1864 in Aleksoty, Mariampol District, Augustów Governorate, in the Russian Empire, now part of the city of Kaunas in Lithuania.

Hermann Minkowski spent his childhood in Kovno, now Kaunas. In 1872 his family moved to Königsberg, now Kaliningrad.

In 1880 he entered the University of Königsberg and spent part of his studies in Berlin with Kronecker, Weierstrass and other leading mathematicians.

Minkowski received his doctorate at Königsberg in 1885 for a dissertation on quadratic forms. He completed his habilitation at Bonn in 1887 and became an extraordinary professor there in 1892. In 1894 he returned to Königsberg, and in 1896 he took up a full professorship at the Zurich Polytechnic, where he was one of Albert Einstein’s teachers.

## The Geometry of Numbers and Spacetime

From 1902 until the end of his life, Minkowski was a professor of mathematics at the University of Göttingen, working alongside his close friend David Hilbert. In 1896 he published his monograph *Geometry of Numbers*. The whole geometry of numbers rests on Minkowski’s theorem.

The first substantial contribution to the theory of systems of linear inequalities was made by Minkowski, who published several theorems on such systems in *Geometry of Numbers*.

Another monograph, *Diophantine Approximations*, appeared in 1907.

In 1907–1908, Minkowski developed a four-dimensional geometric formulation of special relativity. He introduced the four-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space now known as Minkowski space. In this model, time and space are not distinct entities but interrelated dimensions of a single spacetime. The text of his 1908 Cologne lecture *Space and Time* was published separately in 1909 and had a substantial influence on the development of special relativity. Albert Einstein, in particular, held Minkowski’s contribution to relativity in exceptionally high regard.
