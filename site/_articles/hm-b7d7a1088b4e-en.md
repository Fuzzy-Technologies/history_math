A saying often attributed to Laplace reads: “What we know is limited; what we do not know is infinite.”

Pierre-Simon, Marquis de Laplace, was a French mathematician, physicist and astronomer who also worked in mechanics, and one of the founders of probability theory. He produced fundamental work in mathematics, experimental and mathematical physics, and celestial mechanics.

He was born on 23 March 1749 in Beaumont-en-Auge, Normandy.

Laplace was one of the leading mathematicians at the turn of the nineteenth century. He attended a school run by a religious order and later left theology to devote himself to mathematics.

After the French Revolution, he took an active part in reorganizing education in France and establishing the École Normale and the École Polytechnique. Napoleon and Louis XVIII bestowed many honours on him. In 1799 he served briefly as minister of the interior.

Laplace’s principal mathematical work was *Analytical Theory of Probabilities* (1812), in which he brought together his own results and those of others. It brings together many topics now found in probability courses: addition and multiplication of probabilities, generating functions, mathematical expectation, geometric probability, the normal distribution, least squares and difference equations. Laplace studied the binomial distribution and obtained early limit results for sums of independent random variables.

He greatly advanced linear algebra, in particular by giving the expansion of a determinant in minors.

His mathematical research also concerned partial differential equations. The well-known Laplace equation underlies solutions of problems in potential theory, heat conduction, electrostatics and hydrodynamics.

His other monumental work, the five-volume *Treatise on Celestial Mechanics* (1799–1825), contains theories of the figure of the Earth, the Moon, planetary perturbations and the origin of the Solar System. Laplace completed the construction of celestial mechanics on the basis of Newton’s law of universal gravitation.

Laplace played a leading role in the commission introducing the metric system.

He was one of the examiners of the young Napoleon at the École Royale Militaire. Napoleon made Laplace a count of the Empire and awarded him numerous orders and offices.

A well-known anecdote, transmitted in several versions, recounts the following exchange between Laplace and Napoleon:

> — You have written such an enormous book about the system of the world, yet never once mentioned its Creator!
>
> — Sire, I had no need of that hypothesis.
