This story is usually told as university folklore about Andrey Nikolaevich Kolmogorov (1903–1987), whose 1933 axioms laid the foundations of modern probability theory.

Some context for readers younger than the story: Soviet ideology was fond of the formula “everything is interconnected”, while the words “chance” and “independence” could arouse suspicion. Probability theory, however, has the basic concept of independent events: knowing about one tells you nothing about the other.

The story goes that Kolmogorov was once summoned for a conversation “with the appropriate authorities” and sternly asked:

“Why do you have ‘independent events’ in your theory? Everything here is connected with everything else!”

Kolmogorov replied calmly:

“If I give you an example of two independent events, will you change your mind?”

“Go on, then.”

“Very well: the positions of the stars at the moment of Comrade Lenin’s birth, and the Great October Socialist Revolution.”

Probability theory survived. Astrology lost.

## Notes

The formula “everything is interconnected”, or the principle of universal interconnection, was a fundamental tenet of Soviet ideology, based on Marxist-Leninist philosophy, specifically dialectical materialism. It served as a way to interpret reality, economics and politics, rather than merely as a philosophical statement.
