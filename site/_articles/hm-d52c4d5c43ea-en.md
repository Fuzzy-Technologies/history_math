Nikolay Nikolayevich Bogolyubov (1909–1992) entered science so young that his biographers record how the sixteen-year-old was admitted to postgraduate study by special decision: “prodigy mode” had been officially activated.

And ever since, a joke has circulated: open one of his theoretical papers and you find two or three words of ordinary human language on each page…<br>
“it is known that”, “therefore”, “Q.E.D.”—everything else addresses you in the language of integrals and operators.

Joking aside, his style of using words only when needed and formulas for the rest is easy to appreciate by leafing through any of his papers, such as *On the Theory of Superfluidity* (1947), where prose soon gives way to dense mathematics.

So when someone says, “My notes are too dry”, one wants to reply in Bogolyubov’s manner: no need to panic…

“Do not despair, never despair…”

A talented person truly is talented in everything!

Nikolay Nikolayevich had some favourite exclamations. For example, if he asked, “How are things?”, and you replied, “Thank you, Nikolay Nikolayevich, everything is fine”, he would usually exclaim with that charming smile of his: “Do not despair, never despair…” It was cheerful and inoffensive, but also served as a little warning: do not let your guard down.

For a while he had a habit—one he apparently tried to overcome, since it disappeared in his later years—of occasionally rolling out a resonant “Wha-a-at…?” in conversation and, after a short pause, “Everythi-i-ing…”
