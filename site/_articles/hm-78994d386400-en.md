*Marking the 135th anniversary of his birth*

Alexander Nikolayevich Barsukov (1891–1958) was a Soviet mathematics educator and specialist in teaching methods who helped organize school mathematics education. For many years he headed *Mathematics at School*, edited educational publications and wrote school algebra textbooks and works on teaching.

## Education and Early Teaching

He was born on 29 March 1891 in Ilyinskoye, a village in Sudogda District, Vladimir Province. Education was a struggle: his family was poor, and he had to learn early to rely on himself. Expelled from teacher training seminary for taking part in an underground pupils’ organization, he passed the school-leaving examinations as an external candidate and entered Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. Political upheaval disrupted his student years too: he was expelled in 1911 for participating in public protests, but was reinstated and completed his studies.

From 1914 he taught mathematics and physics in Kovrov. His first publication appeared around the same time: a short work on representing a number as a sum of consecutive odd numbers. After the revolution he organized education locally, establishing workers’ faculties and evening courses. In the late 1920s he was invited to Moscow for work in educational administration and teaching methods.

## Editor and Educational Organizer

In the 1930s and 1940s he worked in teacher education and textbook publishing, serving as dean of Moscow State University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, deputy rector for academic affairs and in senior posts at Uchpedgiz, the educational publishing house. From 1934 he edited *Mathematics and Physics at School*, and from 1937 *Mathematics at School*. As editor-in-chief, he helped shape the journal for many years around teaching methods, problems, school curricula and the culture of mathematics teaching.

He taught mathematics at the Lenin Moscow Pedagogical Institute in 1948–1957 and worked at the Research Institute of Polytechnic Education and the Moscow City Institute for Teacher Development.

During the war against Nazi Germany he volunteered for Moscow’s people’s militia. After the war he returned to teaching and work on educational methods at teacher training institutes and professional development organizations.

## School Textbooks

His school textbooks form a special part of his legacy. Barsukov’s *Algebra* was repeatedly reissued and familiar to generations of pupils, valued for clear exposition, a carefully arranged set of exercises and attention to understanding rather than drilling alone. That was the purpose of his work: to make mathematics a meaningful school subject rather than a formal requirement.

He died in Moscow on 21 April 1958 and was buried at Novodevichy Cemetery.
