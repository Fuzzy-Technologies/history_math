*Marking the 215th anniversary of his birth*

> “But someone who stands alone, as Grassmann does, can develop freely in every direction, bringing his nature and his work to a harmonious completion and forming them into a unified whole.”
>
> Felix Klein

A German mathematician, physicist, educator, philologist, linguist, translator and Sanskrit scholar. He was a corresponding member of the Göttingen Academy of Sciences.

Grassmann was a mathematician and physicist with a highly original mind and strong philosophical interests.

## Education and Teaching

He was born on 15 April 1809 in Stettin, Prussia, now Szczecin, Poland.

He graduated from the Faculty of Theology at Berlin University.

In 1840 he passed an additional examination qualifying him to teach mathematics, physics, mineralogy and chemistry.

He taught at the Berlin vocational school (1834), the Stettin Realschule (1836) and the Stettin Gymnasium (1842–1877), where he was later awarded the title of professor in 1852.

## Vectors and Algebra

In mathematics he specialized in algebra, vector analysis and tensor analysis.

In his work on the theory of tides, he first set out the foundations of his approach to vectors. In *The Theory of Linear Extension* (1844; the title translated here), he constructed a coherent system — Grassmann algebra, the first systematic treatment of multidimensional Euclidean space, which contributed to the development of vector and tensor calculus. He introduced the scalar product of vectors in its modern form. He considered the exterior product of vectors, which is a bivector; in Euclidean space its norm equals the area of the parallelogram spanned by those vectors. He introduced special symbols for these operations and studied the laws governing them.

In multilinear algebra, the exterior algebra of a vector space is known as Grassmann algebra.

Independently of W. R. Hamilton, he developed a theory of hypercomplex numbers.

University courses in linear algebra include Grassmann’s formula for the dimensions of the sum and intersection of subspaces:

$$
\dim(L_1 + L_2) + \dim(L_1 \cap L_2) = \dim(L_1) + \dim(L_2)
$$

## Physics and Linguistics

Grassmann’s work in physics included acoustics and the magnetic interaction of currents. An important discovery of his was that colour sensations can be treated as three-dimensional vectors, an idea underlying modern colour theory. In 1853 he established laws of colour addition.

Grassmann also devoted attention to philology and to Chinese and Sanskrit. In 1863 he formulated a law of aspirate dissimilation in Ancient Greek and Sanskrit, now called Grassmann’s law. He is also known for translating the ancient Indian *Rigveda* into German and compiling a *Dictionary to the Rigveda* (1875).
