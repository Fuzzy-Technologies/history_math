Florence Nightingale (1820–1910) was a British nurse, healthcare reformer and statistician. In 1858, she became the first woman elected to the Royal Statistical Society.

She turned hospital death rates into clear charts to make the case for sanitary reform and reduce deaths among soldiers. As she put it:

“Whenever I am infuriated, I revenge myself with a new diagram.”

[Letter from Florence Nightingale to Sidney Herbert](https://cwfn.uoguelph.ca/files/2019/07/UKWILT.pdf#page=126), dated 19 August 1857 in the *Collected Works* chronology. Wiltshire County Record Office, 2057/F4/66.

This was more than a witty remark. In 1858, she published the famous *Diagram of the Causes of Mortality in the Army in the East*, a polar area chart, or “rose”, showing that preventable diseases caused most deaths during the Crimean War, rather than wounds. Her diagrams helped her push through new sanitary measures.

Nightingale was thus among the first to demonstrate how important the visualisation of medical statistics is to making sound decisions.
