Mathematicians are joking and ingeniously deriving the “beard formula”. 🧔

## The Exam

A student arrives for an exam on asymptotic methods in applied mathematics. He draws a question card. The professor asks:

“Tell me, what grade are you hoping for?”

“Top marks,” the student declares crisply.

“Why so?” the professor perks up, already looking forward to finding and confiscating some cleverly hidden crib sheets.

“You see, I know everything…”

“You don’t say?”

“And anything I don’t know, I’ll derive.”

“Oh, really? Then derive the formula for… er… a beard!”

“The asymptomatics here are fairly straightforward,” the student begins explaining without hesitation.

## Deriving the Formula

Let us represent a beard as the limit of a sum of continuous hair-growth functions. On purely physical grounds, we can assert a priori that the beard function is continuous and bounded, although a detailed analysis of its properties would not be difficult. We may therefore distinguish two subsequences of hair-growth functions and represent the function under investigation as the sum of their limits.

We obtain:

$$\text{БОРОДА}=\text{БОР}+\text{ОДА}$$

The Russian word for beard is *boroda* (БОРОДА).

Consider the first component, *bor* (БОР). Niels Bohr—was it named after him?—showed that this function coincides at every point with the forest function: in Russian, *bor* means a pine forest.

As for the second component, *oda* (ОДА), an ode, it can be represented as a generalized function of verse, *stikh* (СТИХ):

$$\text{БОРОДА}=\text{БОР}+\text{ОДА}=\text{ЛЕС}+\text{СТИХ}$$

The sum of the last two functions essentially describes a physical model of windlessness: *les stikh* means “the forest grew still”, while windlessness is *bezvetriye* (БЕЗВЕТРИЕ). Its expansion is given in Appendix 2 to Kolmogorov’s textbook on functional analysis.

Applying elementary algebraic transformations and keeping in mind the physical meaning of our original function’s arguments, we finally derive:

$$\begin{aligned}\text{БОРОДА}&=\text{ЛЕС}+\text{СТИХ}\\&=\text{БЕЗВЕТРИЕ}\\&=\text{БЕЗВ}e+3e\\&=-\text{В}e+3e=e(3-\text{В})\end{aligned}$$

Here $e$ is the base of natural logarithms, and $\text{В}$, the Cyrillic letter Ve, is the hairiness coefficient! The Russian *bez* means “without”, and the sound of *bezvetriye* is split into “without Ve” and “three e” in the mock calculation.
