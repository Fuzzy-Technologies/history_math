He was born on 21 October 1914 in the village of Ostrozhka, Perm Governorate.

A Soviet mathematician and researcher in mechanics, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, professor and a major organizer of science and education. He held the title of Honoured Scientist of the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic. His research covered boundary value problems, functions of a complex variable, differential equations, continuum mechanics and filtration theory.

Nuzhin graduated from the Yelabuga Mechanical and Technical School in 1930 and then worked for three years as a fitter at factories in Staraya Russa, Zelenodolsk and Bondyuga.

He studied at the Kazan Aviation Institute from 1933 to 1936, then transferred to the mathematics division of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at Kazan State University, graduating in mechanics in 1938. Those two university years were very fruitful. His grade book contains nothing but excellent marks and… the signatures of Parfentyev, Chetaev, Shirokov, Persidsky and Ado. May we all be so fortunate in our teachers! In September 1938 he became a postgraduate student in the Department of Mechanics, supervised by its head, Professor Nikolai Parfentyev.

He joined the Red Army in 1939 and fought in the Great Patriotic War from September 1942.

After the war, his entire life was connected with Kazan State University. In 1947 he defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation and was elected a senior research associate at the University’s N. G. Chebotaryov Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics. In 1954, after defending his doctoral dissertation, he became head of the Department of Theoretical Mechanics. On 16 June 1954 he was appointed rector of Kazan University.

He served as rector for twenty-five years and was one of the University’s most outstanding rectors. During his tenure (1954–1979), new research fields emerged there: bionics, biophysics, biochemistry, and the physics and chemistry of semiconductors. The University’s computing centre was also established.

He helped coordinate the work of higher-education rectors.

He received the Orders of Lenin, the October Revolution, the Patriotic War (First Class), the Red Banner of Labour, two Orders of the Red Star and the Order of Friendship of Peoples.

In 2004, a section of Universitetskaya Street in Kazan was named after him.

He is buried on the main avenue of Arskoye Cemetery.

P.S. I remember Rector Mikhail Nuzhin with gratitude: my student and postgraduate years at Kazan State University were 1967–1975.
