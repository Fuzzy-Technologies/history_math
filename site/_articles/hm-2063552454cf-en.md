*Marking the 150th anniversary of his birth*

Three branches of mathematics—function theory, topology and functional analysis—are regarded as the principal new scientific directions that emerged in the early twentieth century. The theory of functions of a real variable came to the fore. Important contributions to its development came from the Italian mathematician Giuseppe Peano (1858–1922) and the French mathematicians Henri Lebesgue (1875–1941), Émile Borel (1871–1956) and René-Louis Baire (1874–1932).

René-Louis Baire was a French mathematician and topologist, and a university professor.

He was one of the founders of the modern theory of functions of a real variable and descriptive set theory. The Baire category theorem was among his most important contributions. He also developed a classification of discontinuous functions.

## Life and Teaching

Baire was born in Paris on 21 January 1874.

He graduated from the École Normale Supérieure in Paris.

He taught in secondary schools in Troyes, Bar-le-Duc and Nancy. He received his doctorate in 1899. In 1901 he became a *maître de conférences* at the University of Montpellier. He moved to the Faculty of Science in Dijon in 1905 and became professor of analysis there in 1907.

Baire’s classic work *The Theory of Discontinuous Functions* (the Russian title translated here) is available in Russian in a translation by A. Ya. Khinchin.
