At an exam, a student is asked about sections of cylinders and cones. She falters and gets muddled: the answer simply will not come.

The examiner decides to coax at least something out of her and starts kindly, with the most everyday of examples:

“Petrova, have you ever sliced a carrot at home?”

“Yes…” she replies, puzzled.

“And diagonally?”

“Ye-es…” she says, even more puzzled.

“And what did you get in cross-section?”

“Carrot…”

At that moment, mathematics puts on its serious face, but smiles quietly: slicing the carrot’s “cone” at an angle really does give a neat oval section (or other conic sections, depending on the angle). The student, though, remembered the important thing: dinner, rather than an ellipse.

P. S. The classical treatment of conic sections is associated with [Apollonius of Perga](https://t.me/history_math/15), author of the *Conics*.
