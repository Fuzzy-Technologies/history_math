Dear friends and colleagues, I’m delighted that Petropolis has published the long-awaited biobibliographical collection *Historians of Mathematics in the Russian Scholarly Tradition*.

*Historians of Mathematics in the Russian Scholarly Tradition: A Biobibliographical Reference Book*. Compiled by G. I. Sinkevich and G. M. Polotovsky. St Petersburg: Petropolis, 2025. 530 pages.

The book contains information about the lives and work of historians of mathematics, as well as the first translators and publishers of mathematical and historical-mathematical texts, bibliographers and authors who wrote about the history of mathematics within Russian-language scholarship and culture from the eighteenth century to the present. It covers about 400 people.

The reference book opens with G. I. Sinkevich’s brief survey of the formation of the history of mathematics as a discipline and of its development in Russia.

The individual entries follow. Their scope is broader than the title suggests: alongside professional historians of mathematics such as V. V. Bobynin, I. G. Bashmakova, A. P. Yushkevich, S. S. Demidov, G. P. Matvievskaya, K. A. Rybnikov and A. E. Malykh, the book includes authors of Russian-language historical-mathematical texts irrespective of their principal profession — mathematicians, historians, philologists and engineers — as well as translators and publishers of mathematical classics and bibliographers.

The entries provide concise scholarly biographies: origins, education, academic or professional careers, teaching work and principal research interests.

One page of the book — page 142, “Gilmullin, Mansur Fayzrakhmanovich” — is about me. I contributed two entries to this collection: “Modzalevsky, L. B.” and “Morozov, V. V.”

I bought the book at the Red Square Book Festival in Moscow on 4 June 2025. In September, it will also be available at the book fair held at Moscow’s VDNKh exhibition centre.

My thanks to the authors for this work!
