*Marking the 255th anniversary of his birth*

A German and Russian mathematician and educator, teacher of the great investigators of non-Euclidean geometry Gauss and Lobachevsky, and author of research in mathematical analysis and analytic geometry.

He was born on 12 August 1769 in Braunschweig, Germany.

While working at the Braunschweig gymnasium, he met Gauss and helped him in every possible way.

He studied at the University of Göttingen and received his doctorate from the University of Jena in 1799. In 1800 he taught mathematics in Reichenau, Switzerland; in 1801 he moved to the cantonal school in Aarau; and from 1803 he taught at the University of Jena.

Imperial Kazan University was founded in 1804 and opened the following year. In 1807 Stepan Rumovsky, one of the first Russian academicians and an organiser of the new university, invited Bartels to become its professor of mathematics.

The Russian period of his biography began in 1808. In Russia he was known as Martin Fyodorovich.

He spent twelve years in Kazan, teaching analysis, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, mechanics and astronomy. Almost at the same time, the fourteen-year-old future geometer Nikolai Lobachevsky entered the University in 1807. Bartels helped his talent flourish and defended the headstrong student against arbitrary administrative decisions. He encouraged Lobachevsky’s interest in mathematical creation and introduced him to the latest mathematical advances. His other students included the future astronomer I. M. Simonov and the future academician D. M. Perevoshchikov.

After a university staff reshuffle in 1820, Bartels moved to Dorpat. At its university he established a research school in differential geometry. He became a full professor of pure and applied mathematics in 1821 and remained there until his death. He was elected dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics and also lectured on the history of mathematics.

He became a corresponding member of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences in 1826.
