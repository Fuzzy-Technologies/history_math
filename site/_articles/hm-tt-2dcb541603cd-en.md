As we enter the digital age, let us still look back and remember the giants on whose shoulders stands the great edifice of mathematics and of science as a whole. We, the mathematicians and physicists of Yelabuga, mention with pride and respect Academician Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov, in whose department we have the honour to work. The 80th anniversary of the Great Victory is another occasion to remember him: Soviet scholars contributed to that victory, including here in Yelabuga.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-3" %}

Our faculty's history is inseparable from the century-long history of Yelabuga's teacher-training institution. In 1939, the Yelabuga State Teachers' Institute opened on the basis of the teacher-training college, with four faculties, including physics and mathematics. It became a pedagogical institute in 1953, and the Yelabuga Institute of Kazan Federal University in 2011. Thus the institute's history begins in 1939, two years before the Great Patriotic War.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-4" %}

History brought thousands of people to our small town at the beginning of the war. Enterprises, research institutions and educational establishments were evacuated here from the war zone. Yelabuga and its institute became a home for them.

At the end of the summer of 1941, prominent scholars arrived with the evacuated research branch of Leningrad University. Among them was Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov (1887–1974), a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences and professor.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-5" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-6" %}

From 5 November 1941 to 19 June 1944, before the Leningrad State University branch returned from evacuation, Smirnov headed the Yelabuga State Teachers' Institute's physics and mathematics department and taught mathematics part-time. Under his guidance, future mathematics teachers studied mathematical analysis and elementary mathematics. He also undertook other work at the institute. Orders survive appointing him chairman of the State Examination Commission in 1942 and 1943, and a member of the admissions commission in 1943.

Teaching at the institute was only one part of his many-sided activity. He headed the mathematics and mechanics laboratory of the Leningrad University branch, which carried out important defence work in ballistics under his direction. Scientific and administrative authorities repeatedly praised its high quality. This work also contributed to his election as a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences on 29 September 1943, during his years in Yelabuga.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-7" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-8" %}

Smirnov wrote the celebrated five-volume *Course of Higher Mathematics*, from which Soviet physicists and mathematicians learned from the 1920s to the 1980s. In Yelabuga, he worked on its fifth volume, devoted to real-variable function theory and functional analysis. This final volume appeared in 1947, and in 1948 he received the State Prize for the work: an encyclopedia of mathematical analysis and a unique achievement in mathematics. In Russian, there were 23 editions of volume 1, first issued in 1924; 21 of volume 2; 9 of volume 3; 5 of volume 4; and 2 of volume 5, first issued in 1947. The *Course* took about 25 years to create and was translated into 8 languages.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-9" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-10" %}

For half a century, from the 1920s to the 1970s, Vladimir Ivanovich's influence was decisive for many scholars in different branches of mathematics and its applications to theoretical physics. His work on the equations of mathematical physics, complex-variable functions, elasticity and mathematical history became widely known. He played a major role in reorganizing the university teaching of physics and mathematics for physicists. This subsequently helped physics faculties separate from the former faculties of natural sciences. The reorganization required new textbooks and teaching materials. Smirnov undertook the enormous task of creating a comprehensive mathematics course for students, teachers, researchers and engineers. The country's physicists therefore owe their thorough mathematical education to him.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-11" %}

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-12" %}

Smirnov's family lived near the institute, at 77 Lenin Street, now 175 Neftyanikov Avenue. On 5 May 2016, a memorial plaque dedicated to him was unveiled there. The building now houses Yelabuga's Education Department.

During the war, our institute building accommodated the Teachers' Institute, the teacher-training college, the evacuated Leningrad University branch and Voronezh University.

Despite all the difficulties, the Leningrad scholars began work as early as September 1941. Their principal task was, of course, research, mainly for defence and practical purposes.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-13" %}

By the director's order, owing to the Leningrad University branch's return from evacuation, Academician Smirnov was granted leave from 22 June 1944 and released from his position at the Teachers' Institute when that leave expired.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-14" %}

In 2006, Nauka published the second edition of a book about Smirnov in the Russian Academy of Sciences series *Scientific Biographical Literature*. Its editors were Academician Olga Alexandrovna Ladyzhenskaya and Vasily Mikhailovich Babich, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences. It was compiled by Professor Galina Pavlovna Matvievskaya and the prominent Soviet mathematical historian Elena Petrovna Ozhigova. The book was issued under the programme of the Historical and Methodological Commission of the Academy's S. I. Vavilov Institute for the History of Science and Technology to develop scientific biographies of figures in science and technology. It gives a scholarly biography of this outstanding mathematician.

The beautifully produced edition included additional material: M. F. Gilmullin's account “During the Harsh Years of War…”, on Smirnov's Yelabuga period in 1941–1944, and the memoirs of his niece, Nadezhda Alexeyevna Nikiforovskaya, much of which likewise concerns wartime Yelabuga.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-15" %}

Nadezhda Nikiforovskaya recalls Yelabuga with gratitude: *“It was undoubtedly fortunate for Vladimir Ivanovich that he found himself in Yelabuga with the Leningrad State University branch during the war. There he had excellent conditions for scientific work while also being able to teach.”*

*“Preserve the traces of a human being on the sands of time!”* These words, once spoken by Academician Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov, can be seen as a kind of testament to posterity, urging future generations to care for the intellectual legacy of outstanding individuals.

I believe that a memorial plaque should be installed in our institute and a portrait of Academician Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov should hang in our department.
