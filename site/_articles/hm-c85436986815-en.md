*A Magician and Wizard of Logic.*

*Marking the 105th anniversary of his birth*

An American mathematician, logician, philosopher, writer, pianist and sleight-of-hand magician. He wrote numerous popular books on logic and mathematics, exploring logical puzzles, paradoxes and advanced ideas in logic. He taught at Princeton University.

## Music, Magic and Education

He was born on 25 May 1919 in New York, USA, to Isidore and Rosina Shmulyan, later Smullyan, Jewish immigrants of Russian origin.

As a child he took piano and violin lessons. He attended classes at several colleges, studying mathematics and music.

His first professional occupation was magic: he worked as a stage magician.

In 1955 he received a bachelor’s degree from the University of Chicago, and in 1959 a doctorate from Princeton for his dissertation *Theory of Formal Systems*. His supervisor was Alonzo Church. While a graduate student, Smullyan published “Languages in Which Self Reference Is Possible” in the *Journal of Symbolic Logic* in 1957, examining the conditions under which self-reference is possible in a formal system.

## Scholarly Work and Teaching

His scholarly works include:

- *Theory of Formal Systems* (1961)
- *First-Order Logic* (1968)
- *Gödel’s Incompleteness Theorems* (1992)
- *Recursion Theory for Metamathematics* (1993)
- *Diagonalization and Self-Reference* (1994)
- *Set Theory and the Continuum Problem* (1996)

He taught at Yeshiva University in 1961–1968, at Lehman College from 1968 and at Indiana University from 1982. Smullyan taught logic and wrote books about it.

He also wrote several books on Taoist philosophy, attempting to resolve most philosophical problems and bring mathematics, logic and philosophy together.

## Books, Philosophy and Music

Professor Smullyan’s literary interests are inseparable from his scientific work: he is a recognized authority on mathematical logic. His popular books display an inexhaustible inventiveness. He delights in dressing such a serious subject as logic in carnival costumes.

Many of his popular books have been translated into Russian. The following are among the best known; their Russian-edition titles are translated here and retain the Russian publication dates:

- *What Is the Name of This Book?* (1981)
- *The Princess or the Tiger?* (1985)
- *Alice in the Land of Ingenuity* (1987)
- *Alice’s Adventures in Puzzleland* (2008)
- *Chess Mysteries: One Hundred Difficult Problems Involving Chess Investigations* (2012)
- *Scheherazade’s Riddle and Other Amazing Ancient and Modern Puzzles* (2012)
- *Satan, Cantor and Infinity, and Other Puzzles* (2014)
- *It Is All about Logic* (2019)

He released a recording of his own performances of favourite classical pieces by Bach, Scarlatti and Schubert.

He wrote an autobiography entitled *Some Interesting Memories: A Paradoxical Life*.
