John Napier (1550–1617), the inventor of logarithms, had a reputation as a practitioner of the dark arts. One day, he put it to use with a distinctly mathematical elegance.

Something had been stolen in his house. The servants were under suspicion, but no one knew which was responsible. Napier gathered them and announced that he had a black rooster that could read minds and would certainly expose the thief. For the ritual, he ordered the servants to enter a dark room one at a time and stroke the bird: if the thief touched it, the rooster would immediately crow.

The servants went in one after another, but the rooster remained silent.

Then Napier asked everyone to show their hands. Everything became clear: the innocent servants’ hands were stained with the soot he had rubbed onto the rooster beforehand. The culprit’s hands were clean—frightened of the “clairvoyant” bird, he had not touched it.

A control test, Napier-style.

P.S. There is more to discover in [“Napier’s Magic Rods”](https://teletype.in/@history_math/Volshebnye-palochki-Nepera-12-05).
