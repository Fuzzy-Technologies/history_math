Some questions sound philosophical but invite a mathematical approach: first we clarify the terms… and suddenly discover that this is the most dangerous part.

Turing opens his famous paper with precisely this move:

“I propose to consider the question, "Can machines think?" This should begin with definitions of the meaning of the terms "machine" and "think."”

Then comes his characteristic response: rather than getting bogged down in definitions, Turing replaces the original question with a more testable one—the imitation game. It becomes almost an engineering recipe: if a term resists a rigorous definition, try framing the problem so that it can be tested fairly.
