The Swiss mathematicians Jacob and Johann Bernoulli were the first generation of the remarkable Bernoulli family of Basel, which contributed several generations of mathematicians.

Two of Johann's sons also left their mark on mathematics: Nicolaus Bernoulli (1695–1726) and Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782). Both worked for a time at the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences from 1725, at Peter I's invitation. Daniel remained from 1725 to 1733, then returned to Basel.

Daniel Bernoulli was a physicist, mechanician and mathematician, and one of the founders of the kinetic theory of gases, hydrodynamics and mathematical physics.

He was an academician and foreign honorary member, from 1733, of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences; a member of the academies of Bologna (1724), Berlin (1747) and Paris (1748); and a Fellow of the Royal Society of London (1750). His scientific standing was exceptionally high. He won the Paris Academy of Sciences prize 10 times.

Daniel Bernoulli was born on 29 January (8 February) 1700 in Groningen, Holland, where his father Johann was then teaching mathematics at the university.

He became interested in mathematics at an early age, studying first with his father and his brother Nicolaus while also studying medicine. After returning to Switzerland, he became friends with Euler. In 1721, he passed his medical examinations and defended a dissertation in Basel. He then went to Italy to gain medical experience.

Daniel performed an invaluable service for the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences by inviting Leonhard Euler to work there. During his time in Russia, he published *Observations on Recurrent Sequences* (1728) and prepared his principal work, *Hydrodynamica*, published in 1738. In its preface, he wrote: *“I willingly declare that the greater part of this work owes its existence to the direction, ideas and support of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences.”*

Daniel's relations with his father are known to have ranged from strained to hostile, and their disputes over priority in hydrodynamics continued for a long time.

In 1733, he became professor of anatomy and botany in Basel, as no other posts were available. He maintained an active and mutually beneficial correspondence with Euler. In 1750, he moved to the chair of physics at the University of Basel, where he worked until his death in 1782. He was elected rector twice.

Daniel mainly studied astronomy, physics, hydrodynamics and gas dynamics. He laid the foundations of the kinetic theory of gases. In his study of oscillations, he was the first to use trigonometric series, later called Fourier series, to solve the corresponding differential equation.

He is best known for his work in mathematical physics and differential equations, and is regarded, alongside d'Alembert and Euler, as a founder of mathematical physics. He gave one of the earliest formulations of the conservation of energy and, simultaneously with Euler, the first formulation of the conservation of angular momentum (1746). For many years, he studied and mathematically modelled elastic oscillations, introduced the concept of harmonic oscillation, and formulated the principle of superposition of oscillations.

He laid the foundations of the theory of partial differential equations.

In algebra, he devised an approximate numerical method for solving algebraic equations.

In probability theory, he was the first to apply infinitesimal calculus (1768), and he applied probability to population statistics.

He also defined the number *e* as a limit.
