*Marking the 105th anniversary of his birth*

Naum Yakovlevich Vilenkin was a Soviet mathematician, popularizer of mathematics and author of school and university textbooks. He received his Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1950 and became a professor in 1951.

He was born on 30 October 1920 in Moscow and graduated from Moscow State University’s Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics in 1942.

## Teaching and Research

From 1943 he worked at several higher education institutions, including the Moscow Power Engineering Institute, Saratov State University and other Moscow institutions. From 1961 he worked at Moscow State Correspondence Pedagogical Institute, which became part of Moscow Pedagogical State University in 2015. He headed its Department of Mathematical Analysis for eleven years.

Vilenkin organized and was the first leader of the national research and teaching seminar for mathematics lecturers at teacher training institutes, “Preparing Mathematics Students for the Teaching Profession”.

His early work, including his dissertation, concerned topological groups. From the mid-1950s he studied the representation theory of Lie groups. His research covered general algebra, topology, real-variable function theory and functional analysis.

## Textbooks and Popular Mathematics

From the mid-1960s he helped write school mathematics textbooks and books and teaching materials for students at teacher training institutes and for school teachers.

Vilenkin became widely known for his school mathematics textbooks, including those for grades 5 and 6. The first edition of the fourth-grade textbook issued for widespread classroom use reached schools in September 1970. He wrote it with K. I. Neshkov, S. I. Shvartsburd, A. D. Syomushin, A. S. Chesnokov and T. F. Nechayeva.

He also taught at Moscow’s Physics and Mathematics School No. 444, where his teaching materials were introduced.

Vilenkin was a prominent popularizer of science. His *Stories about Sets* and *Combinatorics* became classics of popular mathematics.

He wrote more than 300 research papers, as well as school textbooks. More than twenty Candidate dissertations in mathematics and mathematics education were written and defended under his supervision.

He received the K. D. Ushinsky Prize for his educational work.

## Notes

P.S. As an algebra lecturer at a teacher training institute in Soviet times, I would like to mention an excellent series of textbooks for correspondence students at Moscow State Correspondence Pedagogical Institute, to which Vilenkin contributed:

*Algebra and Number Theory*, by N. A. Kazachek and others, edited by N. Ya. Vilenkin. Russian edition. Moscow: Prosveshchenie, 1984. 192 pp.

In my work with future mathematics teachers, I used the following books:

- Boltyansky, V. G., and Vilenkin, N. Ya. *Symmetry in Algebra*. Russian edition. Moscow: Moscow Center for Continuous Mathematical Education, 2002. 240 pp.
