The Pythagoreans’ favourite geometric figure was the pentagram, a regular five-pointed star. It served as a password and symbolised health and happiness. They drew it in the sand to greet one another. They are thought to have known how to construct a regular pentagon—from the Greek πενταγωνον.

A regular pentagon can be constructed with compass and straightedge. Euclid’s Elements, around 300 BC, gives one algorithm.

Various sources describe an experiment that constructs it by knotting a paper strip.

Tie the strip in a simple knot and carefully flatten it. You will obtain a regular pentagon. Trim and fold the ends if you wish. Try the experiment.

Even better, see whether you can prove for yourself that the resulting figure is a regular pentagon.

### A hint for the proof

The strip itself provides a hint: unfold the knot to reveal trapezia whose bases are a side and a diagonal of the pentagon.

A regular pentagon’s diagonal-to-side ratio is the golden ratio.

For more beautiful properties of regular pentagons, try these sources:

- [“A Regular Pentagon: a Knot in a Paper Strip”](https://etudes.ru/models/paper-knot/), Models, Mathematical Etudes (in Russian).

- A. Daniyarkhodzhaev, “Vokrug pravilnogo pyatiugolnika” (Around the Regular Pentagon), *Kvant*, 2020, no. 5, pp. 37–39 (in Russian).

- M. F. Gilmullin, [*Istoriya matematiki* (History of Mathematics)](https://ridero.ru/books/istoriya_matematiki/) (in Russian).
