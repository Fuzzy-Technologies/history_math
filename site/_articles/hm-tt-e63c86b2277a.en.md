Mulled wine is the finest warming drink, and one of the familiar features of New Year and winter celebrations. Its ingredients are simple, yet they leave enormous scope for experimenting with different additions. You probably know several recipes yourself. Ordinary mulled wine is wine heated to a particular temperature, together with spices, seasonings and fruit.

Today, however, we are interested in its alcoholic strength. Homemade mulled wine usually has an alcohol content of 6–12% by volume. In Germany this figure is even regulated. The best mulled wine is considered to be a drink with exactly 7% alcohol.

This raises a mathematical, and partly chemical, problem. Given any volume (v₁ litres) of wine—the main ingredient of mulled wine—and its strength (p₁% by volume; ordinary table wine has a strength of 10–13% by volume), how can we bring its strength (p₂% by volume) to exactly 7% by volume by diluting it with a certain volume (v₂ litres) of a non-alcoholic liquid, such as juice or water?

Using fractions and percentage ratios, we can easily obtain a simple formula:

v₂ = v₁·(p₁ – 7) / 7 litres

Thus, starting with v₁ litres of wine at p₁% by volume, we obtain (v₁ + v₂) litres of a drink at p₂ = 7% by volume. For example, for two 0.75-litre bottles of wine at 13% by volume, the formula tells us to add 9/7 litres of water so that the resulting mixture contains 7% alcohol.

P. S. Whatever fruit and spices you choose, the cards provide a recommendation for preparing New Year's mulled wine, together with an explanation of the formula for diluting wine with water.
