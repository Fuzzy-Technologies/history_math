Robert Grosseteste (1168–1253), the founder of the Oxford school of philosophy and natural science, was a theologian, mathematician and physicist. He was Chancellor of the University of Oxford, Bishop of Lincoln, and the author of the scientific treatises *On Lines, Angles and Figures* and *On Light, or the Beginning of Forms*.

In a humorous legend, Grosseteste tries to measure the speed of light with mirrors and candles. The result proves… elusive.

In this story, light is too fast, and the scholar is given the line:<br>
“Light is not only a divine phenomenon but also a mathematical riddle!”

Grosseteste’s real treatises on light and optics date from the thirteenth century, long before Galileo and Descartes.
