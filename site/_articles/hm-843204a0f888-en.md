All you need to know is that five times five is twenty-five.

You may remember that $15^2=225$ and $25^2=625$. But you will not remember the squares of all such numbers. Nor do you need to!

Here is a general rule worth remembering: to square a positive integer ending in 5, remove the final 5, multiply the remaining number by one more than itself and append 25 to the result.

For example, to square 35, multiply 3 by 4 to get 12, then append 25 to obtain 1225. That is all!

The rule follows from a simple algebraic formula: the square of $10a+5$ is $100a(a+1)+25$. Here $a$ is the number of tens. For the number 5, take $a=0$.

## Where can you use this?

1. You can square any positive integer with several digits ending in 5. This saves time when multiplying on paper. For example, $1235^2=1525225$. There is less to calculate: $123\times124=15252$.

2. The same trick works for decimals ending in 5. Remove the decimal point, square the resulting integer, then put the point back with twice as many decimal places as before. For example, $55^2=3025$, so $(5.5)^2=30.25$.

3. If someone asks whether numbers ending in 5 but not in 25 can be perfect squares, your answer is no. For instance, 1235 is not a square.

4. The rule helps distinguish perfect squares ending in the same digits. For example, 4096 is the square of 64 rather than 66, since $65^2=4225$, which is greater than 4096.

5. It is easier to take the square root of a perfect square ending in 25. For example, $\sqrt{7225}=85$, since $8\times9=72$.
