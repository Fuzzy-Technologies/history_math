Yakov Borisovich Zeldovich (1914–1987) was a physicist and physical chemist, and a leading theorist of the Soviet nuclear project. But in the corridors of science, people remembered more than his formulas: Zeldovich had a rare gift for folding a whole philosophy into a single clear turn of phrase.

## 💁‍♀️ On form and content

At one meeting, he was asked to speak on the philosophical topic “Form and Content”. Instead of building a system of categories, he offered one sentence: “Forms should be such that you would want to keep them.” In Russian, the joke moves from philosophical form and content to a woman’s figure and financial upkeep.

## 🛠️ The vacuum-pump legend

One story has it that early in his career, Zeldovich was “traded” away from Mekhanobr for a vacuum pump. The memoirs add a qualification: the rumour began as a joke, and Zeldovich himself liked the legend. Years later, he joked, “So even then I was worth something.”

## 🍳 The Odesa frying pan

In public debates, he could expose an opponent’s contradiction so neatly that the awkward silence belonged to the other side. Eyewitnesses recalled him telling this “Odesa” dialogue:

“Sofochka, when are you going to give me back my frying pan?”<br>
“First of all, I never borrowed it; and second, I have already given it back!”

He would immediately translate that into the language of scientific debate:

“Leonid Ivanovich! You claim that, first, our results are wrong; and second, you did all this yourself long ago!”

P.S. Yes, he wrote about higher mathematics too. Here is a detail for those who enjoy surprises: Zeldovich co-authored popular books on higher mathematics for physicists and technicians — the sort that explain calculus as a working tool rather than some museum curiosity.
