For the ancient Greeks, the sciences and arts were the concern of the mythical Muses: nine beautiful young women, daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne, the goddess of memory, who lived on Mount Parnassus and Mount Helicon. Each inspired people in a different art:

- Euterpe — music;
- Clio — history;
- Calliope — epic poetry and eloquence;
- Erato — poetry;
- Polyhymnia — lyric poetry;
- Thalia — comedy;
- Melpomene — tragedy;
- Terpsichore — dance and choral singing;
- Urania — astronomy.

Sanctuaries dedicated to the Muses were called *mouseia*. The word “museum” comes from this Greek term.

There are countless works featuring the Muses. Artists painted them on canvas and in frescoes, sculpted them in marble and bronze, and wrote poems and plays about them.

## The problem

The collection of famous historical mathematics problems includes this “Problem of the Muses”:

Eros, the god of love, complains about the Muses to Cypris, Aphrodite, the goddess of beauty and love. Seeing him in tears, she asks, “What has upset you so? Tell me at once!”

“I carried plenty of apples from Helicon,” Eros replies. “The Muses appeared from nowhere and fell upon my sweet load.<br>
Euterpe seized one twelfth at once, and Clio took a fifth.<br>
Thalia took an eighth; Melpomene departed with a twentieth.<br>
Terpsichore took a quarter, and Erato ran off with a seventh.<br>
Polyhymnia carried off thirty apples; Urania took a hundred and twenty.<br>
Calliope took three hundred.<br>
I am going home almost empty-handed: the Muses left me only fifty apples for myself.”

How many apples was Eros carrying before he met the Muses?

How would you solve it? Our answer is below.

## Solution

Answer: 3360.

$\frac1{12}+\frac15+\frac18+\frac1{20}+\frac14+\frac17=\frac{715}{840}$<br>
$1-\frac{715}{840}=\frac{125}{840}$

The apples taken by Polyhymnia, Urania and Calliope, together with those Eros kept, total 500.

$500\div\frac{125}{840}=3360$.

## Notes

[*Parnassus*](https://gallerix.ru/album/Hermitage-8/pic/glrx-305142211) (1761) is a fresco by Anton Raphael Mengs (1728–1779), a German painter and theorist and one of the founders of Neoclassicism. Parnassus, the haunt of artists and Muses, is regarded as an exemplary embodiment of the theory of art. In a composition arranged like a painted panel, Apollo, god of light and patron of the arts, appears surrounded by the nine Greek Muses and their mother, Mnemosyne. The composition refers to Raphael’s famous fresco *Parnassus*: Mengs sharply reduced the number of figures and clothed them in keeping with the contemporary understanding of ancient dress.
