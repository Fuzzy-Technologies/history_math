*Marking the 630th anniversary of his birth*

Ulugh Beg—Muhammad Taraghay ibn Shahrukh ibn Timur Ulugh Beg Guragan—was a Central Asian statesman, a sultan of the Turkic Timurid state and a grandson of Tamerlane. An outstanding mathematician, astronomer, educator and poet, he founded one of the most important observatories of the Middle Ages.

## Ruler and Patron of Scholarship

He was born on 22 March 1394 in Soltaniyeh, Persia (now Iran).

In 1409 Shahrukh appointed his eldest son Ulugh Beg governor of Transoxiana, a historical region in Central Asia, with Samarkand as its capital. Shahrukh himself ruled from Herat.

Under Ulugh Beg, Samarkand became one of the world’s centres of medieval scholarship. Among those working there was Ghiyath al-Din Jamshid al-Kashi, one of the leading mathematicians and astronomers of his time.

## The Observatory and Tables

Ulugh Beg’s greatest interest was astronomy. His observatory was built in the 1420s. Its principal instrument was an enormous meridian sextant with a radius of about forty metres, partly set in a trench.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

By 1437 the observatory had compiled the Guragan Zij, a catalogue describing 1,018 stars. It also calculated the length of the sidereal year as 365 days, six hours, ten minutes and eight seconds.

Ulugh Beg’s principal scientific work was the *Zij-i Jadid-i Guragani*, or *New Guragan Astronomical Tables* (1444). The work remained influential in the Islamic world, and parts of it were published and translated in Europe in the seventeenth century.

Ulugh Beg was a generous patron of science and the arts. In addition to astronomy and geography, he enjoyed poetry and history.

## Final Years

In 1449 the armies of Ulugh Beg and his son Abd al-Latif fought a battle in which Ulugh Beg’s forces were defeated. Soon afterwards, he was treacherously murdered.

After Ulugh Beg’s death, the observatory declined and was destroyed. Archaeologists uncovered the surviving underground part of its main instrument in 1908.

## Notes

The name Mirzo Ulugh Beg was also used in Central Asian, Russian and Soviet literature. Russian encyclopaedias describe Ulugh Beg as a Central Asian or Uzbek figure. The Brockhaus and Efron Encyclopedic Dictionary called “Ulugh Beg” a “Tatar prince”.

The National University of Uzbekistan bears the name of Mirzo Ulugh Beg. A museum devoted to him has been established in Samarkand, and the Ulugh Beg Astronomical Institute of the Academy of Sciences of Uzbekistan is named in his honour.
