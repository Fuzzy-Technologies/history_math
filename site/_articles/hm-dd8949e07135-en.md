Three students — a mathematician, a physicist and a psychologist — are working on the same probability problem:

A coin has been tossed a hundred times, coming up tails every time. What will come up on the hundred and first toss?

The mathematician says dryly:

“There is a 50% chance of tails.”

The physicist says thoughtfully:

“The experiment shows that it should come up tails…”

The psychologist replies confidently, as though about to explain everything:

“I think it will be heads.”

“But why?” ask the puzzled physicist and mathematician.

“Well… a hundred tails in a row: this coin is clearly in a toxic relationship with heads. It needs closure!”

## Notes

The mathematician’s answer assumes a fair coin and independent tosses.
