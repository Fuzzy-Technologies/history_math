An alidade, from the Arabic *al-ʿiḍāda*, meaning a ruler or support, is a ruler with perpendicular plates at its ends. The plates have slits called diopters, which serve as sights. It rotates around the centre of a graduated circle or semicircle.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

The alidade is a rotating component of several simple angle-measuring surveying, astronomical and physical instruments, including the astrolabe, sextant and theodolite, used for measurements in the field. For example, a theodolite's horizontal circle measures horizontal angles and consists of a graduated circle and an alidade.

The angles being measured may be horizontal, vertical or in any other chosen plane.

The alidade is not an independent instrument. It was used in tacheometry: a quick, though not always precise, method of measurement using the arc of the graduated circle. The word combines the Greek τάχος, meaning speed, and μέτρον, meaning measure.
