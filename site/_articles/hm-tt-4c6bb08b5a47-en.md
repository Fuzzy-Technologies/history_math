(On the 85th anniversary of her birth)

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-1" %}

A Soviet and Russian historian of mathematics and educator. Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences (1992), professor (1993), Honoured Worker of Higher Education of the Russian Federation and recipient of the Excellence in Public Education distinction.

Alla Yefimovna worked at the Perm State Pedagogical Institute for more than half a century. Starting as an assistant, she later became head of the mathematics faculty's Department of Geometry (1986), and subsequently of the Department of Higher Mathematics at the Humanities and Pedagogical University. She founded Perm's scholarly schools in the history of mathematics and mathematics teaching methods.

She was co-director of the Ural Centre for the History of Science and Education, a branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences' Institute for the History of Science and Technology.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

She was born on 1 May 1939 in Leningrad. Together with her mother, she survived the difficult years of the siege.

In 1962 she graduated with honours from the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of the V. G. Belinsky Nikolayev Pedagogical Institute in Ukraine. She worked as a teacher in a district centre in Nikolayev Region (1962–1965), teaching mathematics, physics, English, astronomy, technical drawing and art.

In 1965 she entered postgraduate study in Geometry and Topology at the Perm State Pedagogical Institute under Professor E. G. Gonin. She was retained at Perm State Pedagogical University in the newly established Department of Mathematics Teaching Methods. Perm thus became her home, where she spent most of her life.

She successfully defended her Candidate dissertation, “The Emergence and Development of Finite Geometries”, at the intersection of two specializations: Geometry and Topology, and History of Science and Technology (1983).

A. Ye. Malykh's doctoral dissertation was entitled “Combinatorial Analysis in Its Historical Development” (1992).

Professor A. Ye. Malykh's principal research areas were finite geometries, combinatorial analysis, discrete mathematics, the history of mathematics and questions of mathematics teaching in universities and schools. She wrote an enormous number of articles of different kinds: research papers, methodological articles and popular-science pieces. All were connected with the history of science and mathematics teaching methods.

She served on the editorial boards of History of Science and Technology, Applied Physics and Mathematics, Mathematical Bulletin of Pedagogical Institutes and Universities of the Volga–Vyatka Region, Bulletin of Perm State University: Mathematics, Mechanics, Informatics, Bulletin of Perm State Humanities and Pedagogical University: Natural Sciences, and Living Mathematics, a popular-science magazine for young people covering mathematics, mechanics and computer science.

Alla Yefimovna knew several foreign languages, including Latin, and believed that all scholarly texts should be read in their original language.

In her youth she actively pursued radio sport, artistic gymnastics and fencing, and held the title of USSR Master of Sport.

A. Ye. Malykh's name appears in the World Dictionary of Historians of Mathematics (Toronto, 1987). In 1993 she was elected to the board of the international association Women Mathematicians.

*P. S. I was fortunate to meet A. Ye. Malykh several times at conferences in Perm and to speak in sessions she chaired. While developing my own course, “[History of Mathematics](https://ridero.ru/books/istoriya_matematiki/)” (2018), I used many of her materials: the teaching manuals History of Mathematics in Problems (in 5 parts), Essays on the History of Elementary Geometry: Materials for a Special Geometry Course (co-authored with I. N. Vlasova), Methods for Solving Old Arithmetic Problems (co-authored with I. V. Musikhina), and Lectures on the History of Mathematics.*
