*Émile Borel: marking the 155th anniversary of his birth*

> “Mathematics is a science whose name is familiar to almost all educated people; one might even say that almost all of them regard it with a certain respect. Yet that respect is too often shown, so to speak, from a considerable distance. Mathematics is so highly respected that people are wary even of opening a mathematical book, for fear of profaning it. I thought it desirable to replace this reverence from too great a distance with closer acquaintance, and to suggest paths by which educated minds might try to understand what mathematics is and where its beauty lies.”
>
> Émile Borel

Félix Édouard Justin Émile Borel (1871–1956) was an outstanding French mathematician and educator, one of the best-known mathematicians of the twentieth century.

He popularized science, wrote school mathematics textbooks and actively participated in the international movement to reform mathematics education.

In 1929 he became a foreign corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences, in its Division of Physical and Mathematical Sciences.

A political figure, he also served as president of the French Academy of Sciences in 1934 and received the Grand Cross of the Legion of Honour.

## Education and Public Life

He was born on 7 January 1871 in Saint-Affrique, in the French department of Aveyron.

His earliest lessons came from his father, who ran a Protestant school. “Our pastor’s child is extraordinary,” local people would say. “He has learned the rudiments of geometry, and he is only three.”

At eleven he entered the lycée in Montauban, and at seventeen the Collège Sainte-Barbe in Paris; he studied mechanics and geometry at the Lycée Louis-le-Grand. Winning two entrance-competition prizes entitled him to study either at the École Normale Supérieure, a stronghold of scholarship, or at the École Polytechnique. Émile chose scholarship and entered the École Normale Supérieure. His earliest work, influenced by Gaston Darboux, concerned geometry.

Borel began teaching at the Faculty of Sciences in Lille in 1893. He worked intensively, producing twenty-two papers and a dissertation in three years. In 1897 he returned to the École Normale Supérieure as a lecturer. From 1910 to 1920 he was its deputy director for science.

He was a member of parliament from 1924 to 1936 and served as France’s Minister of the Navy from April to November 1925 in the government of fellow mathematician Paul Painlevé.

During the Second World War he participated in the French Resistance and was awarded the Resistance Medal.

## Mathematics and Scientific Institutions

Together with René Baire and Henri Lebesgue, Borel helped establish measure theory and its applications to probability. His principal research concerned function theory, set theory and probability. He also worked on number theory, algebra, analysis, geometry, mechanics, mathematical physics and statistics, publishing more than three hundred works over his lifetime.

Borel was exceptionally versatile and productive. He left an enormous scholarly legacy: original research, monographs and textbooks across mathematics and its applications to physics and other natural sciences. He also wrote on the philosophy of mathematics and produced many articles and books for general readers.

The *Treatise on Probability and Its Applications* was a landmark publication. Issued in separate fascicles between 1924 and 1939, the four-volume work brought together contributions from many researchers under Borel’s direction.

Borel was also a major organizer of science. His persistence and organizational ability helped establish French institutions including the Henri Poincaré Institute, the National Centre for Scientific Research and the Institute of Statistics.

He devoted considerable attention to popularizing science and improving its teaching. To this end he organized two publishing series, the New Scientific Collection and the Library of Scientific Education, to which he himself contributed actively. Some books in these series were translated into several languages, including Russian.

About Borel:

- Polishchuk, Y. M. *Émile Borel, 1871–1956*. Russian edition. Leningrad: Nauka, 1980.

This book explores the eventful life and varied work of Émile Borel (1871–1956), one of the prominent French mathematicians of the modern era. It describes in detail his now-classic discoveries in analytic function theory, divergent series, measure and integration, and theoretical arithmetic, his work on probability and its applications, and his foundational contribution to the theory of strategic games. It also examines his organizational, literary and political activities and his debates with contemporary philosophers, biologists and mathematicians. The book addresses a broad readership: mathematicians, specialists in mechanics, physicists and anyone interested in the history of mathematics and the natural sciences.
