Three neighbours each contributed 15 roubles and bought a ham with no skin, fat or bone. One of them divided it into three pieces, insisting that they were equal in weight. Another said that she trusted only the scales at the shop on the corner. There, when the weights were converted into prices, the supposedly equal pieces came to 14, 15 and 16 roubles. The third woman checked them on her scales at home and obtained yet another result.

An argument broke out: the first insisted that she had divided the ham into equal pieces, the second accepted only the shop’s scales, and the third only her own.

How can the argument be settled and the pieces divided, without cutting them any further, so that each woman agrees she has received at least 15 roubles’ worth of ham, measured by the scales she trusts?

## Solution

Let the third woman choose first. She will take the piece that weighs more than the others on her scales, so it is worth at least 15 roubles. Such a piece must exist, because when a whole is divided into three parts, at least one part is no smaller than one third of the whole.

Next let the second woman choose. She too must be satisfied: after the third woman has chosen, at least one of the remaining pieces is worth at least 15 roubles by the shop’s scales.

The first woman takes the remaining piece and must also be satisfied, since she believes all three pieces to be equal in weight.
