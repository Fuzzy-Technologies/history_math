The founder of the Kazan algebraic school.

N. G. Chebotaryov was a leading Soviet algebraist and a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences from 1929. He worked on algebraic number theory, Galois theory and Lie groups.

He was born on 3 June, or 15 June in the Gregorian calendar, in 1894 in Kamenets-Podolsk. He studied at Kyiv University and belonged to D. A. Grave’s first research school in algebra. He worked in Kyiv and Odesa. In 1927 he was appointed professor at Kazan University and took up the post in January 1928; he later headed its algebra department. He directed the Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics, which now bears his name, and chaired the Kazan Physical and Mathematical Society. He wrote the well-known monographs *Galois Theory* and *The Theory of Lie Groups*.

During the Great Patriotic War, Chebotaryov remained in Kazan, studying the vibration of naval-gun barrels during firing. He was nominated twice for the Stalin Prize for the results of this work. In 1948 he was posthumously awarded the Stalin Prize, first class, for a body of work on the problem of resolvents. He was awarded the Order of Lenin and two Orders of the Red Banner of Labour, and received the titles of Honoured Scientist of the RSFSR and the Tatar ASSR. His pupils in Kazan included I. D. Ado, V. V. Morozov and N. N. Meiman.

Nikolai Grigoryevich strongly disliked filling in forms and writing about himself. In his autobiography he wrote: “Autobiography. I was born in 1894 in Kamenets-Podolsk. Nothing of significance has happened in my life. N. Chebotaryov.”
