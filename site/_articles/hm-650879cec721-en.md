Dedicated to the 300th anniversary of Immanuel Kant’s birth.

“He studied his bodily constitution, his machine, his organism to perfection, observing it as a chemist observes a compound, adding first one element, then another.”<br>
— Mikhail Zoshchenko

Here is an interesting story from the life of the great German philosopher Immanuel Kant (1724–1804). In this anniversary year, the celebrated philosopher, a central figure of the Enlightenment, will be the subject of both commemoration and critical study.

Immanuel Kant, a professor at the University of Königsberg, was a solitary bachelor. His life was so regular that the citizens of Königsberg set their clocks by the sight of him leaving home and striding off to lecture at the university.

One evening Kant was horrified to discover that his wall clock had stopped because it had not been wound. Apparently the servant he had hired the day before did not know it needed winding. The great philosopher wound it but could not set it correctly: he had left his pocket watch for repair the previous day.

Glancing at his clock, Kant went to visit his friend Schmidt, who lived nearby. On entering Schmidt’s flat, he looked at the clock in the hall. After spending some time there, he looked at it again as he said goodbye.

He returned by the same route at the same pace as on the way to Schmidt’s. Schmidt had moved recently, and Kant had not yet worked out how long the walk to his friend’s house took. Nevertheless, once home Kant immediately set his clock to the correct time. How could he know it?

We invite you to solve this logic problem too.

## Solution

Kant found the time as follows.

Step 1. He noted the reading of his own clock as he left and again as soon as he returned. He could therefore calculate how long he had been away, $A$ hours or minutes.

Step 2. He also noted Schmidt’s clock on arrival and departure, so he could calculate how long he had stayed there, $B$ hours or minutes.

Step 3. The difference $A-B$ was the total walking time. Since the two walks took equally long, Kant added $(A-B)/2$ to the time shown by Schmidt’s clock when he left and obtained the time of his return.

### Kant’s daily routine

After lunch Kant took his legendary daily walk through town. When he stepped out in his grey coat, cane in hand, to walk along a narrow lime-lined street — later called the Philosopher’s Walk — the citizens of Königsberg knew it was precisely half past three.
