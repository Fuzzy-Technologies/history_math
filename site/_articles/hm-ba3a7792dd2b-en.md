Omar Khayyam (1048–1131), the famous Persian astronomer, was both a mathematician and a poet, the author of the *Rubaiyat*. He was the first mathematician to develop a theory for solving equations up to and including the third degree and gave a general classification of all equations in his *Treatise on the Demonstration of Problems of Algebra and Balancing* (1069).

One day he became so absorbed in solving cubic equations that he lost track of time. Knowing his fondness for wine, his students brought him a jug to give him a break. Looking at it, Khayyam exclaimed: “Wine is an equation too, only with three unknowns: taste, colour and aroma!”
