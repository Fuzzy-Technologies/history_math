*Marking the 125th anniversary of her birth*

Pelageya Yakovlevna Kochina, also known as Polubarinova-Kochina, was a Soviet scientist specializing in hydrodynamics and a historian of science. She received her Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree in 1940. She became a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1958, in mechanics and hydrodynamics, and a member of the Russian Academy of Sciences in 1991.

## Education and Early Research

She was born on 1 May 1899, or 13 May in the Gregorian calendar, in Verkhny Khutor, later renamed Pokrovskoye, in Tsarev District, Astrakhan Governorate.

She attended gymnasia in Astrakhan and St Petersburg, graduating from the Pokrovskaya Girls’ Gymnasium with a gold medal in 1916. She continued her education at the Bestuzhev Higher Courses for Women.

In 1919 the courses merged with Petrograd University, and Kochina became a university student. Her teachers included N. N. Gernet, I. M. Vinogradov, A. A. Markov, V. I. Smirnov and A. A. Friedmann.

In 1921 she graduated from Petrograd University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics and began working in the Department of Theoretical Meteorology at the Main Geophysical Observatory.

In 1925 she married Nikolai Evgrafovich Kochin, a Soviet scholar in mathematics and mechanics, one of the founders of modern dynamic meteorology and a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences from 1939.

She taught at various higher education institutions in Leningrad in 1925–1931 and became a professor in 1933.

## Moscow and Siberia

Then came work in Moscow. She was a senior research associate at the Steklov Mathematical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1935–1939. She headed the Department of Higher Mathematics at the Moscow Hydrometeorological Institute in 1935 and then the corresponding department at the Moscow Aviation Technology Institute in 1937. In 1941 she was a professor in the Department of Higher Mathematics at the Moscow Petroleum Institute. She worked at the USSR Academy of Sciences’ Institute of Mechanics in 1939–1959 as a senior research associate, taking charge of its Department of Hydrodynamics in 1948.

When German troops approached Moscow at the end of 1941, Pelageya Yakovlevna left with her children for Kazan, where the Institute of Mechanics had been evacuated. She returned to Moscow when the opportunity arose in 1943. Her husband, Nikolai Kochin, died in 1944.

From 1959 Kochina worked in the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences, heading the Department of Applied Hydrodynamics and the Filtration Laboratory at the Institute of Hydrodynamics. She headed Novosibirsk University’s Department of Theoretical Mechanics in 1962–1966.

In 1970 she returned to Moscow and worked at the USSR Academy of Sciences’ Institute for Problems in Mechanics, heading the Department of Mathematical Methods in Mechanics.

## Research and the History of Science

Her widely known scientific results concern continuum mechanics, hydrodynamics, filtration theory and flow theory. Her work also addressed solid mechanics, structural mechanics, theoretical fluid mechanics and dynamic meteorology.

Many of her works concern the history of natural science and technology. She translated Sofya Kovalevskaya’s collected works and correspondence from French, prepared them for publication and became her biographer. She also wrote books about Karl Weierstrass, Gösta Mittag-Leffler, Nikolai Kochin and Alexander Friedmann.

## Honours and Commemoration

She received many honours, including:

- Stalin Prize, second class (1946), for research on the hydrodynamics of groundwater;
- four Orders of Lenin (1953, 1960, 1967 and 1969);
- Hero of Socialist Labour (1969);
- Order for Merit to the Fatherland, third class (1999), for her contribution to science, training highly qualified specialists and on the occasion of her 100th birthday.

The asteroid 6763 Kochiny was named in honour of Pelageya Yakovlevna and her husband.
