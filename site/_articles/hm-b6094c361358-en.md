*Marking the 160th anniversary of his birth*

A Russian and Soviet mathematician and researcher in mechanics, a full member of the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences (1912) and vice-president of the Russian Academy of Sciences and then the USSR Academy of Sciences (1919–1926).

He was born in Nizhny Novgorod on 28 December 1863, or 9 January 1864 in the Gregorian calendar.

He showed an aptitude for mathematics and physics while studying at the Nizhny Novgorod Nobles’ Institute (1874–1882). He entered Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics in 1882 and transferred to Kharkiv University in 1883. He was a pupil of Alexander Lyapunov, an academician from 1901 and the founder of stability theory.

He graduated from Kharkiv University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics in 1887 and became an assistant in the Department of Mechanics in 1888. In 1902 he received a doctorate in applied mathematics and a full professorship. He chaired the Kharkiv Mathematical Society from 1902 to 1906 and was dean of the University’s mathematics faculty in 1904.

From 1906 he was a professor in the Department of Mathematics at St Petersburg University and a professor at the Bestuzhev Courses.

Steklov founded and became the first director of the Russian Academy of Sciences’ Physics and Mathematics Institute, established in 1921. The institute became part of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1925 and was named after Steklov following his death in 1926. In 1934 it split into two institutions: the Steklov Mathematical Institute, directed by Academician Ivan Vinogradov, and the Physics Institute, directed by Academician Sergei Vavilov.

His principal works, numbering more than 150, concern mathematical physics, mechanics, approximation theory, asymptotic methods, closure theory and orthogonal polynomials.

In hydrodynamics, he investigated the motion of a rigid body in a fluid, vortex theory, the motion of an ellipsoid, and the motion of a rigid body containing an ellipsoidal cavity filled with fluid.

His well-known pupils included Vladimir Smirnov and Nikolai Saltykov.
