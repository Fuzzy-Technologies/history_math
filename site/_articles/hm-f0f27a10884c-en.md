This problem comes from Leonty Magnitsky’s [*Arifmetika* (*Arithmetic*)](https://search.rsl.ru/ru/record/01004091683), published in 1703:

“A man drinks a cask of drink in 14 days, and together with his wife drinks the same cask in 10 days. Find in how many days his wife alone will drink that cask.”

## Solution: first method

Assume that each person drinks a fixed amount per day.

1. $\frac1{10}-\frac1{14}=\frac2{70}=\frac1{35}$.

2. $1\div\frac1{35}=35$.

Answer: 35 days.

Can you find other arithmetic solutions?

## Notes

### Second method

We can also work without fractions. Take 140 days, which is fourteen periods of 10 days or ten periods of 14 days. The couple would drink 14 casks in that time. The husband would drink 10 on his own, leaving four for the wife. She would therefore need $140\div4=35$ days per cask. A. V. Shevkin explains this early method in [*Word Problems in Mathematics, Grades 7–11*](https://ru.djvu.online/file/Yb8gvMhoEiG6i) (pp. 7–8, in Russian).
