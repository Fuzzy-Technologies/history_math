Almost all the scholars of medieval Europe were religious figures.

The scholar and theologian Alcuin worked at the court of the Frankish king Charlemagne, who was later crowned Roman emperor. Alcuin organised schools and wrote several textbooks. The one of greatest interest to us is *Problems to Sharpen the Minds of the Young*, which includes logical puzzles and problems of ingenuity that have survived to this day, such as the familiar wolf, goat and cabbage puzzle.

Alcuin (Flaccus Albinus; Latin Alcuinus, also Albinus; c. 735–804) was an Anglo-Saxon scholar, theologian and poet.

In 781, he travelled to Rome on an ecclesiastical diplomatic mission to the papal court. On the return journey, he met Charlemagne in Parma and was invited to join him. In 782, Alcuin moved to Aachen and became head of the Palatine Academy, which developed into a major centre for the spread of classical learning in Europe. He was one of Charlemagne’s closest advisers on reforms to education and worship.

He wrote textbooks on several subjects. His *Art of Grammar* became particularly well known; his textbooks on dialectic, theology and rhetoric are also known.

Alcuin is credited with the collection *Problems to Sharpen the Minds of the Young*, also rendered as *Problems for the Refinement of Young Minds* (*Propositiones ad acuendos juvenes*). It is the earliest known collection of recreational mathematical problems written in Latin. The oldest copy dates from the late ninth century, contains 56 problems and is held in the Vatican Library. These problems later found their way into other puzzle collections.

## The dog and the rabbit

Here is one of its well-known problems, “The Dog and the Rabbit”:

A dog is chasing a rabbit 150 feet ahead of it. The dog jumps 9 feet each time the rabbit jumps 7 feet. How many jumps must the dog make to catch the rabbit?

How would you solve it? Our answer is below.

## Solution

Answer: 75.

With each jump, the dog gains $9-7=2$ feet on the rabbit. The initial gap is 150 feet, so the number of jumps needed to catch it is $150\div2=75$.

The wolf, goat and cabbage puzzle from Alcuin’s *Problems to Sharpen the Minds of the Young*:

“A man had to take a wolf, a goat and a cabbage across a river. He could find no boat except one that could carry only two of them. The problem was therefore how to get them all to the other bank safe and sound. Tell me, whoever can: how can they make the crossing unharmed?”
