One can argue endlessly about how much mathematics a person needs. But life can sometimes tell a better joke than any collection of anecdotes.

In 1938, Enrico Fermi (1901–1954), already a famous physicist, the 1938 Nobel laureate and a central figure in the history of nuclear physics, was arranging to leave for the United States. At the American consulate, he and the other emigrants had to take a compulsory arithmetic test “for sanity”.

The questions were along these lines:

“What is $15+27$?”<br>
“What is $29\div2$?”

Fermi calmly answered 42 and 14.5. Apparently satisfied, the examiner deemed the future builder of the first nuclear reactor mentally fit to live in the United States.

The funniest thing is not even that a Nobel laureate was given an arithmetic test. It is that sometimes the system wants neither genius, discoveries nor theories: it simply wants to make sure that someone can still manage $15+27$.
