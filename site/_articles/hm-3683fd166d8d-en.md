The well-known Soviet mathematician Nikolai Nikolayevich Luzin (1883–1950) once said of number systems: “... The advantages of the decimal system are zoological rather than mathematical. If we had eight fingers on our hands instead of ten, humanity would use an octal system.”

P.S. Our book [History of Mathematics](https://ridero.ru/books/istoriya_matematiki/) includes an entire chapter on the history of the Russian school of mathematics, including mathematicians’ lives and achievements.

## Notes

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

[Nikolai Nikolayevich Luzin](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9B%D1%83%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BD,_%D0%9D%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B9_%D0%9D%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87) (1883–1950) was a Soviet mathematician, professor at Moscow University and member of the USSR Academy of Sciences. He founded the Moscow school of mathematics. His students included M. A. Aizerman, P. S. Alexandrov, N. K. Bari, V. I. Glivenko, L. V. Keldysh, A. N. Kolmogorov, A. S. Kronrod, M. A. Lavrentyev, L. A. Lyusternik, A. A. Lyapunov, D. E. Menshov, V. V. Nemytsky, P. S. Novikov, M. Ya. Suslin, P. S. Urysohn, A. Ya. Khinchin and L. G. Shnirelman.

In one of his early papers, [On a Particular Case of a Taylor Series](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/sm6532), Luzin showed that a trigonometric series with coefficients tending to zero can diverge almost everywhere. The paper first appeared in German in 1911, followed by the Russian version in 1912. This example came as a complete surprise to most mathematicians.

Luzin was one of the principal founders of descriptive set theory and the descriptive theory of functions. He also obtained important results on the bending of surfaces and new fundamental results in the matrix theory of differential equations, directly related to automatic control theory.
