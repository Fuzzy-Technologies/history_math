*Marking the 125th anniversary of his birth*

Lazar Aronovich Lyusternik was a Soviet mathematician. He received his Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences degree and professorial rank in 1935, and became a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1946.

## Education and University Career

He was born on 19 December 1899, or 31 December in the Gregorian calendar, in Zduńska Wola, Congress Poland, in the Russian Empire, now in Poland’s Łódź Voivodeship, into a large Jewish family. The family soon moved to Łódź, where Lazar began attending gymnasium. During the First World War they lived in Smolensk, where he completed his schooling. He studied at Moscow University’s Faculty of Physics and Mathematics in 1918–1922, graduating as a pupil of Nikolai Luzin and Dmitri Egorov.

- Postgraduate study at Moscow State University’s Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics, 1924–1926.
- Privatdozent at Moscow University, 1927.
- Professor at Nizhny Novgorod University, 1928–1930.
- Professor at Moscow University, 1930–1981.
- Head of the Department of Functional Analysis in the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, 1936–1943.

## Analysis, Topology and Computation

In 1934–1948, Lyusternik worked at the Steklov Mathematical Institute as a senior research associate and department head. He created and led a computing group. In 1945 the Institute established a Department of Approximate Computations on the basis of this group, with Lyusternik at its head. In 1948 the department moved to the USSR Academy of Sciences’ Institute of Precision Mechanics and Computer Engineering, where he remained its head. In 1955 it became part of the newly established Computing Centre of the USSR Academy of Sciences. Lyusternik was also deputy director of the Steklov Institute in 1945–1948.

His principal scientific works concern functional analysis, the calculus of variations, differential geometry, algebraic topology, computational mathematics and programming.

He received the Stalin Prize in 1946 for developing new topological methods in mathematical analysis and for major achievements in the theory of geodesics.

During the Great Patriotic War, Lyusternik took part in defence-related work. He was one of the authors of tables for determining a ship’s position from radio bearings, published in 1944. From 1942 he worked on numerical and machine computation, becoming one of the advocates for establishing the USSR Academy of Sciences’ Institute of Precision Mechanics and Computer Engineering after the war.

At his initiative, Moscow State University established a Department of Computational Mathematics. He was the first professor in the country to teach a course on programming methods at the University.

## Textbooks and the Journal

He wrote well-known university textbooks, including M. A. Lavrentyev and L. A. Lyusternik, *A Course in the Calculus of Variations* (1938), and L. A. Lyusternik and V. I. Sobolev, *Elements of Functional Analysis* (1965), both in Russian.

Lyusternik was one of the organizers of *Uspekhi Matematicheskikh Nauk*. Its first issues appeared in 1936 as irregular collections under his editorship; it later became the journal whose English edition is known as *Russian Mathematical Surveys*. He remained on its editorial board until the end of his life.
