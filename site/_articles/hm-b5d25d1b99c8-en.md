*Marking the 100th anniversary of his birth*

A Soviet and later American mathematician and educator, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences (1951).

He was born in Leningrad on 11 May 1924.

He entered Moscow State University in 1940 and graduated from it. Poor eyesight exempted him from wartime conscription. He was a pupil of Andrey Kolmogorov and Sofya Yanovskaya.

In 1948 he completed postgraduate studies, defended his Candidate of Sciences dissertation under Kolmogorov’s supervision and began working in the Department of Probability Theory of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics. He defended his doctorate in 1951, at twenty-seven, and became a professor in 1954.

He is known for his work on Lie groups and Lie algebras. In a paper submitted in 1951 and published in 1952, he classified semisimple subalgebras of semisimple complex Lie algebras. That year he received the Moscow Mathematical Society Prize for a series of works on classifying Lie algebras.

He also made important contributions to probability, particularly Markov processes, and mathematical economics.

In 1968 he was dismissed from Moscow State University for signing a letter in defence of Ginzburg and Galanskov.

He emigrated in 1976 and became a professor at Cornell University in the United States in 1977. He became a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences in 1978, the US National Academy of Sciences in 1985 and a Fellow of the American Mathematical Society in 2012.

He became an honorary member of the Moscow Mathematical Society in 1995.

Dynkin played a prominent part in mathematics education beyond the classroom. In spring 1945, while still a university student, he organized a mathematics circle for schoolchildren. From the 1950s he ran circles at Moscow State University and organized evening mathematics classes at Moscow Physics and Mathematics School No. 2 with a group of university students in 1963. From 1964 he led a stream there. At his initiative, Moscow University’s duplicating press produced *Mathematical School* in 1965 and 1966, a journal that preceded *Kvant*.

He wrote mathematics teaching books for secondary schools. One example is E. B. Dynkin and V. A. Uspensky, *Mathematical Conversations* (in Russian). Moscow–Leningrad: Gostekhizdat, 1952.
