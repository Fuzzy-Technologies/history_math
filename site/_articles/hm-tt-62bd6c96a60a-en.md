In this article we describe a convenient algorithm for dividing plots of land by using their plans. The quality of the land is also taken into account. Ensuring that all the terms of the agreement are observed requires an independent expert divider — an arbiter — but the division will nevertheless be fair.

Suppose three people jointly own a plot of land. They are given three identical plans drawn on tracing paper, with a straight line, the “baseline”, as shown in the figure below.

{% include article-figure.html id="fig-2" %}

Each partner must draw two lines perpendicular to the baseline on their copy of the plan, dividing the plot into three parts that they regard as equal in value. These parts need not have equal areas, since the quality of the land may be valued unevenly. The sheets of tracing paper are then superimposed, producing 6 lines on the combined plan: 2 from each of the three partners А, В, С. The lines can occur in different orders, but there are 36 possibilities altogether (see the table below).

In each case an allocation is possible in which every participant's plot is at least as large as one of the three parts that they regard as equal in value. If the lines are arranged as in the figure above, the arbiter awards plot МВ to А, plot ВС to С and plot CN to В.

Sometimes the arbiter can establish new boundaries midway through the boundary strips, although this is not essential.

The advantage of division using plans is that the partners submit their proposals simultaneously, without knowing beforehand what the others propose. The arbiter only has to ensure compliance with the agreement that each partner receives a plot containing one of the three parts they marked.

Our relatives described a method of allocating plots among villagers. A specially appointed, experienced committee divided the land into numbered plots, then assessed their quality as good or average and set the ratio of good to average plots that each participant would receive. The plots in each category were then allocated by drawing lots. This is another method of dividing land fairly.

The same approach can be used, for example, to divide school grounds for cleaning, or a school garden for maintenance.
